# TADR-010. Open stages and the event director

Status: **Accepted engineering direction.** Andrew set this direction by play (playtest log (`../docs/playtests/PLAYTEST-LOG.md`) entries 1 and 4; [TRACKING](../TRACKING.md) EV-0479, EV-0578) and authorized recording it on 26 September 2026 (TRACKING EV-0579, card [DF-03](../BACKLOG.md#df-03)). This is not authorization to build.

Date: 26 September 2026. Depends on [TADR-002](TADR-002-runtime-model.md), [TADR-003](TADR-003-content-pipeline.md) and [TADR-004](TADR-004-persistence.md).

## Context

The Bible makes the run's level unit a "compact procedural encounter level" ([BIBLE.md](../BIBLE.md) lines 169, 578) whose encounter grammar is waves (line 519: "Waves create local pressure and release"), with the orchard's three-wave arrangement as bounded proof (line 525). Andrew played that shape and rejected it: "it was a static room like level - looking for more of a exploring, more open world" (playtest log line 92). The target he named is Risk of Rain 2 open stages on terrain built for rolling, with roaming, loot, secrets and an exit trigger, and combat in the world rather than in rooms (line 93; TRACKING EV-0479).

Spillway Drop then gave the event half. Andrew kept its basin flood as a core stage mechanic and asked for events that "ebb and flow or happen in different areas or can be unique to the map", layered with enemy spawns such as "a mini boss that's just spawned or uh, is over in a a randomly spawned location", weather and special events, so the player can "weave in and out of the chaos" and use it (log lines 56 to 58). Cable Bench left its own question open. Its 45 s tier clock and rising cache prices were unreadable, so whether a threat clock makes "one more cache" a decision is unanswered (log lines 43 to 50).

The Bible harmonization review (`../artifacts/bible-harmonization-2026-09-26/REVIEW.md`) records the conflicts this ADR answers: the run unit at seven Bible lines (REVIEW line 9), the wave grammar at 519 and 525 (line 10) and the threat clock against the elapsed-time bans at 432, 513 and 538 (line 15). It judges that a difficulty clock scales threat and is not a progression gate, and labels that judgement inference. It also finds the Spillway terrain vocabulary at line 207 (shelves, basins, chutes, dry bypass) already reads as an open-stage kit (REVIEW line 30).

TADR-002 line 26 gives one encounter coordinator the fixed physics callback and forbids actor callbacks from advancing combat. TADR-004 lines 24 to 30 commit the run at completed-level checkpoints and lifecycle facts. The admitted sections TH-06 (`../docs/tech/sections/TH-06-encounters-waves-and-enemies.md`), TH-11 (`../docs/tech/sections/TH-11-runs-regions-exits-and-saves.md`) and TH-14 (`../docs/tech/sections/TH-14-randomness-generation.md`) were written for compact levels and waves; the sections index had labelled TH-06 and TH-14 parked on that shape (TRACKING EV-0480).

The two sketches already contain a director, and they are prototype evidence, not proof. `spillway-drop/src/director.gd` (`../sketches/spillway-drop/src/director.gd`) is a pure scheduler stepped with a delta: spawn interval, cap and enemy health scale with a stage-time tier (`TIER_SECONDS := 45.0` at line 9, tier, cap and interval at lines 28 to 41, health at lines 62 to 67), and a spike raises the tier while the exit charges (stage.gd line 175). `flood_cycle.gd` (`../sketches/spillway-drop/src/flood_cycle.gd`) runs one fixed loop of dry, klaxon, rising, flooded and draining (lines 5 to 8), announces before it rises (line 49) and reveals a hatch after the first flood (line 59). `cable-bench/src/director.gd` (`../sketches/cable-bench/src/director.gd`) is a node that advances itself in its own `_physics_process` (lines 39 to 54), places spawns on a random ring behind the camera (lines 74 to 93) and spawns a miniboss when the forge charges (line 68; stage.gd line 159). These show that the pieces can be built and played. They do not show readability, balance, determinism or the ownership TADR-002 requires; the Cable Bench director's self-advancing node is the shape TADR-002 line 26 forbids.

## Options

| Option | Disposition | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open stages with one event director, a composed controller inside the encounter, that composes stage events, enemy spawns and discoveries; waves become one event type | **Accepted** | Matches entries 1 and 4 and keeps TADR-002's single tick owner. The Bible's region vocabularies become the stage kit without new authority. |
| Keep compact encounter levels with the wave grammar as the Bible and TH-06 describe | **Rejected** | Andrew played it and rejected it (entry 1). Waves as the only grammar cannot express an event that ebbs, moves between areas or belongs to one map (entry 4). |
| Open stages driven by a global elapsed-time difficulty clock and time-priced caches, as in Cable Bench | **Rejected as the baseline** | Its question went unanswered because the stage was unreadable (entry 5). Pricing progression by elapsed time conflicts with BIBLE lines 432, 513 and 538. Stage time survives below only as an optional threat input. |
| A fixed authored event timeline per stage, as in Spillway's single flood loop | **Rejected** | The same stage plays the same way each time, so events cannot vary by area or ebb and flow as entry 4 asks. The fixed loop is a sketch convenience, not a design. |
| Autonomous hazards and spawners, each with its own clock, coordinated by signals or a global event bus | **Rejected** | TADR-002 lines 24 and 26 and HF-04a line 20 forbid an actor clock, a second gameplay advance path and a global gameplay event bus. The Cable Bench director is this shape. |

## Decision

**The open stage is the run's level unit.** A stage is one bounded 3D space on momentum terrain, entered at an entrance, roamed freely, holding loot, secrets and threats, and left through an exit the player takes. Where an admitted section or the Bible says "level" or "compact encounter level", production reads "stage". The run structure around it holds as written: a successful run crosses four worlds (BIBLE line 578), each world has a boss and an ordinary stage needs none (line 519), and a world transition offers two random destinations independent of item rewards (line 580). The recorded route policy (TH-11 RT-01 to RT-04, lines 91 to 94) is unchanged. A stage completes when its exit is taken. This exit is the in-stage trigger entry 1 names; it is not BIBLE line 580's onward exit, which the player chooses after the stage completes (TH-11 RX-01 and RN-3, lines 55 and 118). What the exit requires before it opens, for example a charge or a defeated miniboss, is an objective under [Q-P38](../BACKLOG.md#q-p38); completion is read from objective state, never from an emptied population (TH-06 OB-04, line 63).

**One event director composes what happens in a stage.** The director is a composed controller inside Encounter's boundary (HF-03a line 21), not a mutation owner. Encounter evaluates it at P2 of each tick with the same `h` (HF-04a lines 22 and 63), and its state lives in `EncounterState`; adding those fields is a revision of HF-03a's record, not a new owner. The session owner keeps run continuity and never runs the director. Session resolves the stage's event, spawn and discovery definitions at the run's pinned revision and supplies them as values at `initialize_encounter` (TH-06 OB-01, line 60), and it receives only the published `EncounterResult` it settles once. The director therefore dies with the stage's encounter and a replacement stage starts a fresh one. It composes three kinds of stage content from typed, stable-ID definitions ([TADR-003](TADR-003-content-pipeline.md)) that a map or region supplies:

1. **Stage events.** Floods, drains, weather and special events, plus currents, which BIBLE line 207 already proposes as a Spillway traversal variable. Each event is scoped to an area of the stage, so events can differ by area; a definition can belong to a single map, so an event can be unique to it. An event announces itself, becomes active and recedes. BIBLE line 209 requires a changing basin to announce the new ground and reachable exits before it becomes dangerous, and that rule binds every event that changes the ground.
2. **Enemy spawns.** Arrivals of ordinary enemies and minibosses, including a miniboss at a varied location. A wave is one spawn event among these, not the stage's grammar. Every spawn is an arrival under TH-06 AV-01 to AV-06 (lines 76 to 81): admitted at P2, placed through CollisionQueries at P3 before any attack query, and published as the same committed fact the player sees.
3. **Discoveries.** Reveals such as the hatch a drained basin exposes. What a discovery grants and which facts create a permanent one stay with [Q-P37](../BACKLOG.md#q-p37); when one becomes durable stays with Q-E09 (next durable transition, HF-07b line 139).

Events may overlap and layer, which is the chaos entry 4 asks for. Overlap does not relax readability: every admission decision must still leave a usable response (BIBLE line 525; TH-06 AM-04 and EO-11, lines 92 and 263), and the counts and budgets stay with [Q-E10](../BACKLOG.md#q-e10).

**Events change the world through committed state.** An event's physical change, such as a water surface rising or a basin draining, is committed encounter state published at P6. CollisionQueries reads it for the next tick's movement and attack queries, and rendering interpolates it without advancing it (TADR-002 lines 26 and 28). Damage and presentation consume the same committed geometry. What an ability does to an event's material, for example freezing flood water into a surface, belongs to the element and material decision of card [DF-02](../BACKLOG.md#df-02).

**The director draws from its own stream.** Its random choices (which event, which area, where a miniboss arrives) come from a director stream held in `EncounterRandomState` beside the loot and miscast streams. The mechanism of TH-14 GS-02, GS-03 and GS-05 (lines 73, 74 and 76) still holds: one position per encounter-scoped `stream_id`, no durable projection, an end at `retire`, and entry only as values at `initialize_encounter`. Their count does not. This adds a fifth random responsibility to TH-14's table and raises the encounter-scoped streams from two to three; the sections index (`../docs/tech/sections/README.md`) lists every row that states the old count. It changes no durable field: the two durable streams stay route and layout (TH-14 GS-04, line 75). Stage layout is still assembled before the stage is entered from the run-level layout stream (TH-14 GG-02 and GG-05, lines 97 and 100). The director composes inside an assembled stage and is not the layout generator.

**Elapsed time may scale threat and never gates progression.** Stage time, counted in ticks of `h` and frozen while paused (O3, HF-04a lines 79 and 89), may be an input to threat, such as spawn pressure or event intensity. It may not open or close an exit, advance a tree, price an upgrade or cache, or otherwise gate progression (BIBLE lines 432, 513 and 538; TH-01 LO-14, line 141; TH-06 EO-15, line 267). A timed objective, if Q-P38 selects one, counts ticks and is an objective, not elapsed-time progression. The distinction between threat and progression is the review's inference, adopted here as the rule. Whether a stage uses a threat clock at all is not decided; Cable Bench's question stays open until a stage explains its economy (entry 5).

**Saves follow TADR-004 unchanged.** A stage completion is TADR-004's completed-level checkpoint, committed through HF-07b's Transition A (lines 74 to 81). Revival spend and terminal settlement commit as TADR-004 line 30 states. No director, event, spawn or stage-time fact is durable; all of it ends with the encounter at `retire` (TH-01 line 60). An interrupted stage follows the adopted interruption policy P1 and resumes at the banked entrance with everything the stage produced lost (HF-07b line 147). This ADR adds no durable field and no timing.

## Consequences

The Bible's region catalogue becomes the stage design kit, and its compact-level and wave wording needs harmonization, which card [DF-04](../BACKLOG.md#df-04) owns. TH-06, TH-11 and TH-14 stay admitted as written; the sections index (`../docs/tech/sections/README.md`) records which parts this ADR supersedes and which remain, and a later heavy-lane card revises their bodies. Most of TH-06's mechanism carries over to director spawns unchanged. Its wave-cycle positions do not.

Encounter state grows. A stage can hold several active events, their areas, their phases and pending spawns at once, all inside one coordinator's tick. The collision world changes during a stage when events move surfaces, so query ordering and world synchronization carry more load than a static arena. Entity and geometry scale for an open stage is unmeasured (TADR-002 line 46).

Content carries the variety. Per-map event catalogues, area scoping and spawn definitions become authored, admitted data, so a new event is content rather than code when its behaviour fits an existing event kind. An event kind the director cannot express is a contract revision.

Interruption costs more (inference). An open stage is larger than a compact level, so P1's rule that an interrupted stage restarts loses more play. That is a reason to revisit the interruption policy after stage length is measured, not a decision here.

Readability is the main risk. Layered chaos is the stated goal, and BIBLE line 525 says an unreadable crowd is weak evidence of challenge. Entry 5 shows an unreadable stage fails its own question. Announcements, the objective display and a pause (entry 5 requests) carry that risk, and only Andrew's play settles it.

## Later qualification and reconsideration

Open and not chosen here: the generation technique and spatial module set ([Q-P23](../BACKLOG.md#q-p23)); stages per world ([Q-P12](../BACKLOG.md#q-p12)); stage size; which events, weather and terrain consequences each region carries ([Q-P24](../BACKLOG.md#q-p24)); exit requirements and objective types (Q-P38); the enemy, miniboss and boss roster ([Q-P25](../BACKLOG.md#q-p25)); event frequency, duration, intensity and overlap limits; spawn rates, budgets and pressure (Q-E10); whether a threat clock exists, its shape and whether threat carries across stages; and every cache and scrap price. No number in the sketches is adopted.

Qualify in play and in engine-hosted tests. Andrew plays a stage with at least two overlapping events and a miniboss at a varied location and judges whether it reads and whether he can use the chaos. Spillway's route-choice question is asked at its next play (entry 4). A director unit test steps a stage's schedule headless at fixed `h` with no wall clock, as the sketches' tests already step theirs. Test that an event's surface change reaches collision, damage and presentation from one committed state, under the physics authority DF-01 records. Test that interruption mid-stage loses director state and resumes at the banked entrance, and that pause freezes stage time. Director draws repeat within one build on one platform only (TH-14 GT-01, line 143).

Revisit this ADR if threat must carry across stages or a mid-stage checkpoint is wanted; either needs a durable field and so a revision of HF-07a, HF-07b and TADR-004. Revisit it if the director needs to run outside the encounter tick, which would change TADR-002's timing authority.
