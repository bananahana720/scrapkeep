## Context

**The ground today is too small and has no holes.** `src/world/terrain.gd:26-29` builds every stage as a 201 by 201 heightfield at 2 m, about 400 m square. One trimesh collider comes from the same samples (`terrain.gd:383-391`), and height queries read that grid, so the ground the code asks about is the ground the ball rolls on (`terrain.gd:20-24`). A world's stages are built together on first read (`terrain.gd:104-115`), and the idle stage is parked 5 km below the play space (`terrain.gd:116-131`, `146-148`). A heightfield cannot hold a tunnel, a cavern or an overhang.

**Andrew asked for a park.** His seed (`artifacts/local/alpha/push/w12/DESIGN-SEED-park.md`, 9 October) asks for maps "way bigger", with ramps, ways to navigate, underground and flooded caverns, a huge skate park per world disguised in Arizona's own landforms, and the societies that terraformed them. Boss arenas keep their own small spaces. His principle 24 (2 October) asks for a natural, uncanny jungle gym where every landform carries a traversal verb and enemies spawn from homes that give them an advantage. The traversal measure exists because he said the map "is just not fun to traverse" (`design-v3/TRAVERSAL.md:3`).

**Terrain3D was measured on this engine.** The P-ADDON probe (`artifacts/local/alpha/push/w12/probes/addon/REPORT.md`, verdict row in `probes/VERDICTS.md`) found that Terrain3D v1.0.2 (MIT) loads headless and on the RTX 3060 under Godot 4.7.2. Regions run from 64 to 2048 vertices a side at a settable vertex spacing and need not touch. Holes cut both render and collision through `Terrain3DData.set_control_hole`. Its own collision can be switched off: mode 0 left the game's trimesh as the only collider, and the load test fails loud when the mode is wrong. Level of detail is a camera-centred clipmap with up to 10 levels. Costs on the s3 bench: frame p99 0.96x, GPU p99 1.14x, draws p99 2.06x (scatter chunks). The editor plugins must stay disabled or every smoke import fails. Proton Scatter 4.0 does not parse on 4.7.2. The coordinator parked Terrain3D "for the wave 13 park map". The P-GROUND probe adopted native MultiMesh scatter and the 1 m relief view over the 2 m collider. P-GEN and P-GEN-2 failed twice, so generated meshes are out and Blender recipes stay the asset route.

**Wardens today are one clamp with different stickers.** `src/combat/warden.gd:10-16` describes one immobile posture clamp at the base of the beacon mast, the same for every stage. The family comes from the stage row (`src/core/alpha_defs.gd:69-120`) and runs reactor, swarm, reactor, swarm, mind, mind across stages 1 to 6, so world one fights two different kinds and the Dig repeats world one's pair. Three variants each change one thing per family (`warden.gd:28-34`, `53-54`). The harpoon already moves its anchor with a moving body (`src/movement/augments.gd:697-713`), but every enemy line drops once the ball passes inside the minimum rope (`augments.gd:711`).

**The alpha fires no stage events.** Every alpha row carries `"event_mix": {}` (`alpha_defs.gd:13-15` says so), so the event director returns before it builds anything (`src/world/event_director.gd:62-64`). The director, the terrain-change hold rule (`src/world/terrain_event.gd:7-11`) and the contraptions (`src/world/contraptions.gd:86-101`) exist and are unused on the alpha table.

## Decision

**A stage is an Arizona park about 1.6 km square.** The play square runs from -800 m to +800 m on both ground axes. Every landform is a park element in disguise and carries one traversal verb: washes and slot canyons are half-pipes and flow lines, Hohokam canals and flumes are grind lines, open-pit benches are bowls and terraces, lava tubes and adits are tunnels and drops, karst sinkholes are flooded caverns, cliff dwellings and mesa rims are elevated lines and ledges, Madrean sky islands are hops. The society that made a landform (Hohokam, Ancestral Puebloan, mining, the dam era) is recorded on its row and feeds codex fragments. Each world's two zones are two parks. TADR-010's open stage is unchanged as the level unit; this record sets its size and its build.

**The terrain is hybrid.** Terrain3D v1.0.2 draws the ground, cuts its holes and supplies the level of detail. It is vendored under `addons/terrain_3d/` with its editor plugin left out of `project.godot`, so smoke imports stay clean. Rendering uses 1 m vertex spacing and a region size of 1024 vertices, so four regions cover -1024 m to +1024 m: the play square plus a 224 m apron for rims and skyline. Ramps, tunnels, caverns, rims, bowls, rails and the hero landmark are Blender recipes under `tools/blender/park/`, each with two detail shells, placed from the stage's park table. A tool bakes each park table into a ground grid, a hole mask and a surface map under `assets/park/<map_id>/`; the runtime loads the bake, never the map function, and a freshness test fails when a table changes without a re-bake.

**There is one collider path.** The physics ground is trimesh tiles 160 m on a side, built from the 2 m grid the height query reads, with the two triangles per cell that the query interpolates. Cells under a hole are left out of the trimesh and read the floor of the recipe beneath them. Terrain3D's collision stays at mode 0. Recipes collide through trimeshes built from their own collision meshes. A terrain event swaps one tile's shape on one tick, as today's terrain changes swap a finished collider (`terrain_event.gd:7-11`). A parity test samples 10,000 seeded points per stage and requires the query and a downward ray on the collider to agree within 1 cm. If the tiles exceed the memory budget in [PARK.md](../sketches/core-loop/design-v3/PARK.md) section 9, HeightMapShape3D tiles may replace them only after passing that same parity test, holes included. A Fable reviewer reviews this path before it merges.

**Each stage has one park file and each fact one table.** `src/world/park/<map_id>.json` holds the stage's `park_rows`, `event_rows`, `home_rows`, `contraption_rows`, `weather_rows`, `activity_rows`, its `landmark` row, its `routes` and its `waymarks`. `src/world/park/park_schema.gd` closes every enum and checks every cross-reference. The stage table (`alpha_defs.gd`) keeps what it owns today: stage number, world, display name, Warden kind (`warden_family`), variant, music and pressure. The park file never repeats those. PARK.md section 2 lists the exact fields and the systems that read each table. The sketch keeps these as JSON plus a validator; production would carry them as typed Resources under TADR-003 when a heavy-lane card promotes them.

**One hero landmark per stage, seen from everywhere, houses that stage's Warden.** The landmark is the tallest built thing in its park and is placed so at least 85% of open play cells on a 50 m grid have a clear sight line to its top (a test, starting value). The Warden mounts on or in it: open air, underground, in a building, suspended or flying. The bay docks at a slot the landmark row names, so it never searches for a fit. Boss arenas (Brindle, the Waking, the Mind) stay authored and small and are not parks. Meteor Crater is the ending bowl after the Mind; its recipe family also makes the small craters an impact event leaves.

**Three Warden kinds, one per world, two mountings each.** The kind ids are the existing family ids: `reactor` (the reactor relic) in Spillway Works, `swarm` (the swarm remnant) in the Dig, `mind` (the Mind's hand) in the Highdraft. Each kind keeps the posture grammar: a telegraphed pulse, posture that fills to a stagger, four chunks that break one per stagger, and the harpoon drop as the finisher. Each mounting adds one mechanism to play around. Every Warden is a harpoon anchor: the ball can latch its hardpoints, ride the line, fire from the line, and be dragged when the Warden or its mounting moves. Its pulse may shrug a line after a telegraph, and letting go never costs the ball its velocity. The three existing variants become the second mounting of each kind (`meltdown` on the flume, `brood` in the sinkhole, `choir` on the spires), so their codex entries stay true. The stage rows change so each world fights one kind (PARK.md section 7).

**Stages are dynamic through one director.** TADR-010's director runs one `event_rows` table per stage. Event families are natural (a flood fills a bowl, a cave-in opens a tunnel, a collapse drops a line), Fleet incursion (a drill barge or dropship lands and sets up a new enemy home), ancient awakening (a buried machine erupts as a miniboss) and industrial (a boiler or reactor blows: a hazard plus a terrain change). Rows fire on the difficulty clock's tier or on a Warden phase. Every row is telegraphed for at least its lead time, never stops the ball, and always leaves an open route from the drop-in to the landmark; a route-graph test proves the last for every set of events that may be live together. Every row names a music cue, so a drop in the music lands with the change in the world. Enemy homes reinforce from their landforms, contraptions are rows the stage places, and weather is a row that changes the view and the event odds but never the ball's handling. Time of day is fixed per stage.

**Approval and acceptance gates.** Andrew approves a storyboard per stage and a sheet per Warden kind by click before that stage's terrain or that kind's body is built. Every stage is greyboxed first and dressed second. A measured traversal line per pilot per stage is hard acceptance: six pilots, eight seeds each, inside the bands in PARK.md section 8, on the greybox and again after the recipes land. A floor line with no harpoon and no contraption must also reach the Warden, so no verb is ever required. The parks run behind `--table=park` until all six pass; then the alpha table takes their map ids and the old maps retire with their tests.

## Consequences

The 2 m grid grows from 40,401 samples to 641,601 per stage, and the trimesh from 80,000 to 1.28 million triangles. Collider memory and build time become a budget line (PARK.md section 9), and the build moves off the main thread: the next stage loads on worker threads from Warden engagement and is applied on the launch landing, so no stage change stops the ball.

Content carries the variety. A new event, home, contraption or weather that fits an existing kind is a table row; a new kind is code and a schema revision. Six park files, eight recipe families, three Warden bodies and their storyboards are the session's largest content load, and the storyboard gate is the brake on building hollow parks.

The run's level unit becomes larger, so TADR-010's note that an interrupted stage loses more play applies with more force. Nothing here adds a durable field; events, homes and weather all end with the encounter.

Several sketch files gain readers they did not have: the event director reads triggers from Warden phases, the music engine reads cues from event rows, and the bay reads a dock slot from the landmark. Files shared with the pilot lane (`tools/bot_driver.gd`, `src/core/run_log.gd`, `src/core/event_kinds.gd`) need serial integration (PARK.md section 10).

The Bible's map and Warden passages need the amendment drafted in BIBLE-AMENDMENT-park.md. The decision trail (`docs/decisions.tsv`) records each approval.

## Later qualification and reconsideration

Open and not chosen here: final event frequency, overlap and intensity (TADR-010 leaves them open, and PARK.md gives only starting values); whether a ball can skim water at speed, which would be a TADR-009 water row; the difficulty clock's cap on park stages; the stage count per world (two, unchanged).

Qualify in engine-hosted tests and in play. Headless tests: the schema and cross-reference test, the bake freshness test, the collider parity test with holes, the landmark sight test, the route-graph test under every allowed event overlap, events firing on each trigger kind with the lead never shortened, homes reinforcing and stopping when broken, and a bot latching, riding, firing from and finishing each Warden kind. Bot measures: the traversal bands per pilot per stage. Performance on card 1 against the pre-wave base: GPU and frame p99 within 1.3x, CPU main-thread p99 within 10 ms measured with the card to itself. Andrew's sitting on the export judges whether the parks are fun to cross and whether the Wardens read; automated play does not establish feel.

Revisit this record if the trimesh tiles and the fallback both miss the memory budget, if Terrain3D blocks a Windows export, if a stage cannot reach 85% landmark sight without breaking its traversal line, or if Andrew's sitting finds the parks hollow at this size.
