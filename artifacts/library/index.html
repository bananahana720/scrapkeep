Scrapkeep Library

The goal: a finished-feeling Linux alpha, accepted at the full-alpha sitting after a world 1 sitting. You open it from your app menu, play from the title screen through all three worlds and six dressed parks, and accept it at your playtest sitting: 11 areas, none "Not yet", no blocker open, frame times in budget, zero placeholders.

The mission: every run gives you things to look forward to, explore, discover and experience, the way Isaac does; everything visual and intuitive, with information popping where and when you need it.

Where things stand
  • World 1 comes first, fully polished. On 10 Nov you ruled that the world 1 sprint ends at "world 1 fully polished" before you play. The build then comes to you as "ready for your verdict". You played the dam on 30 Oct.
  • Main is the wave 39 promotion (cd095277, 12 Nov); waves 37, 38 and 39 all landed that day. Wave 40 is open: the first build wave from the three world 1 critiques (screen reads, kill and hit looks, sounds, crew heads, models). The work runs from this project: one thread drives the wave board and your PC, one thread per unit.
  • Your rulings of 10 and 11 Nov: the year is 2010, the map standard of ten points, harder and never easier, one ride through a world, no slop in anything the player meets, test where the change is and gate the release once, and write for a reader with no context. On 11 Nov: Heat, Overtime and Called Shot before the sitting, test only where needed, four build systems folded into three, and early stop in band reads. On 12 Nov: the project coordinates, the score is a DJ set mixed to play, the four look rebuilds land before the sitting, and the sitting run ends won at Brindle. On 13 Nov: a hidden seventh pilot, Patch, built after the sitting, and the ball goes to the cutaway look. The register has every ruling.

The game

A roguelite where you pilot a rolling scrap-mech ball with a harpoon. You bowl through enemies, grapple and slam, carve half-pipes, and grow mightier over a run, Binding of Isaac style, while the chaos scales with you. The presentation is a melodramatic anime and comic-book playground with Scott Pilgrim as the touchstone; written lore stays in a dry crew-log voice. The music is jazz-rooted steampunk-cyberpunk with house and electro bass, and every mini-boss, boss and intense moment gets its own bass-heavy EDM anthem. It plays hard but fair, Souls-like: bosses are learned, and a cracked build still steamrolls.
