## Scope and acceptance

The output is a proposed bestiary organized as boss blueprint → subsystem lineage → specialized enemy → optional veteran adaptation. Each family must have a common physical mechanism, visible anatomy and action rhythm. Branches must change player decisions, not merely size, color or health. Every proposed combat mechanic must identify its inherited rule, variation, tell, common counters and useful salvage. Each environment must show how its machines live and work there.

Working roots: The Cancrusher for the spillway, Counterweight Crown for the foundry, and the Grand Pruner for the walking orchard. Sluiceback remains a spillway miniboss. Existing game labels are preserved separately; this document does not rename game content.

Three research workers use gpt-6-astra at high effort. They have no write access in their briefs; the coordinator serializes document and visual-artifact changes. Source facts and fictional adaptations must stay separate. No paid research services or installations.

## Result

[ENEMY-TAXONOMY.md](ENEMY-TAXONOMY.md) contains three branching diagrams, fifteen ordinary design cards, six veteran adaptations, three miniboss combinations and three boss syntheses. It includes a source-to-design table per family, working-environment descriptions, material and animation rules, and explicit relationships to earlier concepts.

PM corrections: Crossbelly's broken mount drops its loaded magnet cassette; disabling a belt does not turn off a permanent magnet. Orchard repairs cannot reverse boss structural breaks. The review led to explicit compaction anatomy for Rinsebelly, a binding mechanism on Grand Pruner itself, and old-to-new naming/behavior mapping.

No agent dropouts. Source research and paper consistency checks are complete; behavior remains INCONCLUSIVE at evidence levels 2–3. No code, gameplay, controls, meshes, animation clips or runtime boss names changed.
