# Scrapkeep: rolling chaos, build discovery, and escalating worlds

Research snapshot: 5 September 2026. Publisher discovery and comparative research; no production scope or session goal is set. Confirmed preferences are maintained in [DISCOVERY.md](DISCOVERY.md).

## Finding

**The strongest candidate identity is a rolling scrap creature that turns the battlefield into its own playground.** Collecting changes the ball, collisions create opportunities, and quick infrastructure changes where those collisions can go. Cute animal pilots give the accumulated machine a personality; run loot changes its behavior.

This is a design and positioning hypothesis. Rolling mechs already exist in Go Mecha Ball, and rolling combined with defense already exists in Rock of Ages. Scrapkeep needs a memorable relationship among collecting, collisions, builds, and terrain. A larger feature list alone would not establish a distinct appeal. [Go Mecha Ball](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2008510/Go_Mecha_Ball/), [Rock of Ages 3 publisher overview](https://maximument.com/news/rock-of-ages-3-make-break-is-rolling-out-today/).

Working pitch: **Roll a tiny animal's scrap ball into an outrageous war machine. Bowl through enemies, build your own ricochet routes, and turn the wreckage of enormous battles into your next ridiculous combination.** This is proposed positioning, not an approved store description.

## Publisher constraints guiding the comparison

- Katamari-like rolling, collecting, and bumping are the main appeal and character identity.
- Close third-person view; PC/controller first. Prove solo play before co-op; future player collisions remain part of the fantasy.
- Successful expeditions take 30–45 minutes through themed stages, branches, exploration, combat, quick defense encounters, and bosses.
- Mech-scale physical growth should still fit designed routes. Gurren Lagann inspires increasingly enormous battles and destruction; literal giant transformations remain exploratory.
- Isaac-style attack-changing combinations and high-damage skill shots; class diversity inspired by TF2 and Gunfire Reborn.
- Low-cooldown class abilities, action-charged ultimates, autonomous class infrastructure, and companions assembled from run loot.
- Unlock new options and challenges between expeditions; rebuild combat power each run. Enemies retain readable opportunities to contest the player's plans.

### Movement lessons from creators

Keita Takahashi's firsthand Katamari postmortem credits sound and vibration with improving collection feel, while discussing unwieldy rolling from uneven attachments and camera/scale problems. These are historical development observations, not defects independently reproduced in current games. The resulting Scrapkeep hypothesis is to separate expressive scrap wobble from a dependable collision envelope. Pickup sound, cockpit reactions, and immediate steering recovery deserve early attention. [Creator's 2004 postmortem, republished 2024](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-the-singular-design-of-namco-s-katamari-damacy-2004-).

SEGA documents braking, a directional Spin Dash, and several camera controls in Banana Rumble. These establish useful control options to examine, not evidence that the same controls will feel good in Scrapkeep. [Official manual](https://manual.sega.jp/supermonkeyball/bananarumble/en/index.html).

Dungeon Defenders' designer describes combat-time building and maintenance that enemies can interrupt. For Scrapkeep, preserve a readable commitment and recovery opportunity while shortening the interaction. Repair chores are not yet part of our concept. [2011 developer explanation](https://blog.playstation.com/2011/10/03/dungeon-defenders-takes-over-psn-october-18th-with-move-3d-support/).

The reception researcher read 30 English reviews, deliberately selecting five positive and five negative per game. Repeated anecdotes concerned disrupted rolling in Katamari, delayed variety/repetition in Go Mecha Ball, and balance in Rock of Ages 3. These are prompts for our own tests, not prevalence estimates or reproduced defects. The linked ledger preserves query parameters, recommendation IDs, specific counts, and examples. The balance of positive and negative reviews in this sample does not represent each game's overall reception.

There is a concrete commercial example of an unusual ball-based roguelite reaching substantial sales: Devolver's 9 April 2026 preliminary results report **over one million BALL x PIT units on Steam in its first ten weeks through December 2025**. This is a publisher-reported result for one successful title, not an addressable-market estimate, a typical indie outcome, or a forecast for Scrapkeep. [Devolver preliminary results, page 3](https://investors.devolverdigital.com/files/downloads-and-publications/2026/devolver--full-year-2025-announcement.pdf#page=3).

Our likely audience is a hypothesis: players who enjoy physical mastery and odd machines, then return for surprising builds. Katamari fans may seek less combat pressure; action-roguelite players may expect more aiming precision. Their overlap and willingness to buy this particular combination are unknown. Public review counts cannot settle that.

Recommended initial positioning is a premium solo action roguelite centered on rolling and scavenging, with tactical defenses visible in gameplay. This follows Andrew's direction and the comparison, not a measured genre preference. Co-op, mobile, figurines, a price, and content commitments should not carry the initial promise before the solo game earns it.

## Original directions to explore

The following are proposals synthesized from the references and publisher interview. They are not source-game mechanics, finalized names, or commitments to produce three worlds.

### 1. Build a ricochet route

Explore a salvage spillway, then defend its pump while enemy rollers descend banked chutes. Drop a spring bumper at a bend; slam a shield carrier into it; catch the exposed machinery from the impact. A companion paints the next heavy target with a visible conductive mark. A class-specific attack converts that opening into damage or disruption.

The most useful item question becomes **what does the next collision do?** A proposed spring item stores a rebound; a magnetic item catches loose scrap along the exit path; an echo item repeats a compatible impact effect after a delay. Their combined result should be physically visible. Shared composition rules, stacking, and balance remain design work.

Enemies can plant visible braces, approach a bumper to dismantle it, or occupy a better bank angle. Their commitments create chances to interrupt or flank. The test is whether those responses keep collision play engaging without repeatedly stopping the player.

### 2. Make each class change the ball's route

| Class fantasy | Proposed movement/infrastructure relationship | Proposed signature attack opportunity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grapple | Drop a winch anchor, swing around it, and redirect an enemy into the exit path. | Aimed hook connects a moving trajectory to a powerful impact. |
| Hover mortar | Deploy an updraft bellows with an autonomous mortar nest; use brief lift to cross a ground hazard. | Designate the landing zone while airborne, then return to rolling combat. |
| Cyborg laser | Deploy a prism bumper that supplies a bank angle and a beam-redirection point. | A charged cutting shot rewards the alignment created by the ricochet. |
| Lightning | Place storm rods along a useful route; rolling past charges the network. | A well-timed discharge exploits enemies pushed between conductors. |

The hover class needs particular scrutiny: extended flight could dilute the game's rolling identity. Short lifted arcs with satisfying landings are one candidate. Class-specific ultimate charge should reward useful contributions and avoid safe repetitive charging exploits.

### 3. Escalate what the ball can affect

Treat **physical ball size, battlefield scale, and destructive reach** as separate design choices. The route-scale growth answer settles only the first for now.

| Expedition image | What the player does | What makes the scale feel larger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salvage spillway | Bumps local machines, collects their parts, and protects a pump. | Familiar obstacles become movable or collectible; a breached sluice releases an enormous scrap surge. |
| Stormglass foundry | Links defenses across relay yards while a siege machine approaches. | The same rolling actions charge industrial machinery capable of striking a district-sized threat. |
| Walking orchard | Boards broad terraces on a migrating mechanical greenhouse and protects systems across its back. | The enormous enemy or machine becomes the playable environment. Its breakdown creates the next traversal opportunities. |

This gives the Gurren Lagann reference an interactive expression: the hero's actions have increasingly enormous consequences. A brief ultimate that docks infrastructure into a giant rolling siege shell is a second, more literal option. It would need its own suitable arena and camera/handling test; it is not implied by the three-stage illustration.

The first generated image's orderly shell underplays the Katamari direction. Future visual studies should show an unmistakably rolling mass of mismatched salvage, a readable animal cockpit inside it, and enormous machinery beyond it. Art should make the rolling action and accumulated run history visible in a still image.
