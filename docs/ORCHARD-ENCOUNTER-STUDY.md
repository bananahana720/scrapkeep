# Orchard encounter study: familiar tools, enormous consequences

5 September 2026. Proposed encounter and animation direction, derived from [the enemy taxonomy](ENEMY-TAXONOMY.md) and Andrew's publisher answers in [DISCOVERY.md](DISCOVERY.md). This is a paper specification for later prototyping.

The first orchard encounter should teach a relationship the player can carry into the boss: a working tool announces its commitment, misses if the player chooses a useful route, and exposes something worth knocking loose. The hero keeps rolling throughout. Small machinery supplies both the lesson and the parts for an increasingly personal machine.

## What this study settles provisionally

The canopy family reads as **inward jaws versus outward feet**. Snipcart collapses an open wedge; Spreadleg pushes its feet toward the flanks. “Opening a gap” does not itself mean that entering that gap is safe.

All three scales share a fork around a circular pivot, a short diagonal brace and a replaceable working end. Snipcart is low and forward. Spreadleg is a compact inverted Y above two articulated legs; raised upper arms are removed from the revised art direction. Grand Pruner repeats those mechanisms around a mobile greenhouse.

The player's animal remains inside an irregular rolling scrap mass. Enemy maintenance machines are autonomous. Healthy trees, protected contact surfaces and repaired equipment establish a cultivated place worth saving. The hostile control override is a proposed explanation for the conflict, not a new animal-enemy faction.

## Ninety-second teaching encounter

These are approximate presentation beats, not locked wave timers or a promised boss kill time. A strong build can shorten or skip familiar cycles. The sequence follows an orchard turning apron to an adjoining boss terrace; the full area still includes exploration, optional branches and other encounters.

| Approximate beat | Readable situation | Player opportunity | What happens if ignored |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–10 seconds: arrive | Snipcart sorts twigs while Spreadleg tends a support. Loose scrap traces an approach beside a broad outer service route. | Roll, collect and see the tools doing ordinary work before aggression. | No attack begins outside the first encounter's readable camera space. |
| 10–25 seconds: inward cut | Snipcart aims, opens its jaws and straightens its brace. Its tool stops tracking before closing. | Roll outside the closing reach, bank back into the retracted housing, or shoot its exposed hinge. | The previously aimed central approach is struck. Surviving machinery can recover and reposition for another attempt. |
| 25–40 seconds: outward feet | Spreadleg settles its central frame and turns its shoes outward. The legs remain articulated. | Cross early before the spread, interrupt a leg before commitment, or take the outer route and shoot an ankle. | A tight habitual circle runs into the outward foot stroke. Ordinary frame contact remains a bump. |
| 40–60 seconds: compare | Surviving workers occupy the same apron. The first paired lesson staggers their damaging strokes. | Read the moving part and choose a different route. A quick device deployment can improve the opportunity without becoming a requirement. | Both workers continue their announced cycles; neither waits indefinitely for a tutorial response. |
| 60–75 seconds: collect and recognize | Loosened parts lie near the exit. A much larger fork and cream brace move beyond the ordinary terrace ramp. | Collect salvage and reach the terrace on the ground. Companion assembly can wait. | Surviving workers retain readable attacks but do not become invulnerable progression gates. |
| 75–90 seconds: transfer | One Grand Pruner arm prepares a sweep; its local service housing and ground exit are visible. | Leave the committed reach and return to ram the exposed housing, or use ordinary aimed attacks. | The sweep punishes a bad route. A successful counter visibly damages that assembly and loosens scrap; the boss remains active. |

The illustrated storyboard isolates Snipcart and the boss to make the visual inheritance legible. It does not depict every beat of this longer teaching encounter.

## Four poses to carry through animation

| Pose | Snipcart | Spreadleg | Selected Grand Pruner arm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Position | Cart aims a folded elbow; jaws are slightly separated. | Feet remain near the center, angled outward beneath a compact upper fork. | One tool unfolds toward a terrace; other major tools stay folded. |
| Brace / prepare | Frame settles, short brace extends and jaws open into a visible wedge. | Central frame settles and upper support steadies; leg pivots stay free for the stroke. | Padded hands seat an intact arm in its cradle; a continuous band tightens from the binding spool to that cradle. |
| Committed operation | Blades pass in offset planes along the aimed closure; housing does not chase the target. | Two legs rotate outward through announced ground arcs; no limb appears or changes length. | The selected supported tool sweeps its announced reach. Irrigation and other arms do not also activate during this introductory exchange. |
| Release / service | Elbow folds back and exposes the side housing before the cart begins a new approach. | Foot stroke stops, ratchet releases and legs recover through the same joint paths. | Band slackens and the arm folds into local service access. Broken structures remain broken. |

The circular pivot must stay visible as a stable landmark. Animate the working tool first and its supporting brace second. Hose vibration, leaves and cockpit reactions stay subordinate to the attack. A distinct mechanical cadence supports the motion cue; color is supplemental.

As Spreadleg widens its stance, its central frame lowers consistently with the linked leg motion. Keeping the body at a fixed height while stretching the legs would defeat the mechanical premise. Resolve the actual pivot paths in a later simple articulated model before detailing the surface.

The spool must visibly connect to the selected arm. A decorative reel somewhere on the boss cannot communicate which arm will move. Its reachable latch interrupts preparation without granting whole-boss immunity or rebuilding a removed arm.

## Clearance and camera contract for a future greybox

Let **D** be the diameter of the largest normal gameplay collision envelope, not the span of every decorative scrap sticking out. The earlier movement research proposes expressive attachment wobble around a dependable envelope; this study retains that hypothesis.

Start the outer bypass at approximately **2D clear width**, outside the union of the workers' active tool footprints. That is a construction starting point, not a proven safe dimension. Check it at maximum ordinary growth, expected entry speed and the chosen steering response. Windup must give the player enough time to reach a viable exit from intended approach positions. Width cannot compensate for an exit that cannot be reached in time.

Do not require passage beneath Spreadleg. The early center crossing is optional; the outer ground route remains the dependable alternative. Keep tree supports, loose decorative clutter and deployed devices from silently closing that route. A later implementation must decide how device placement preserves it.

Use a close chase view with enough forward and lateral space to see the committed tool and at least one achievable exit. Do not use a cinematic camera cut or automatic steering to manufacture success. Test view obstruction at growth extremes; this art does not establish a shoulder offset, lens, collision response or camera solution.

After a damaging hit, the encounter should let the player recover steering and find a route before another forced hit. Specific recovery protection remains a later combat decision. No timed guard, parry, hover, grapple or particular companion is required here.

## Salvage that remembers its previous job

The taxonomy's two component ideas remain the starting point:

| Found part | Proposed useful function | How the player recognizes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shear Linkage, from Snipcart | Gives an assembled companion a short sweep. | The same fork and cream link move on the companion; its sweep is short and spatially visible. |
| Adjustable Brace, from Spreadleg | Helps a planted companion resist displacement. | Padded feet settle and the small brace straightens before it holds position. |

An optional combination is a companion that plants during its sweep, primes a nearby threat, and gives the rolling hero an opening for a high-damage attack. Priming is an exploratory interaction, not a new guaranteed property of the two individual items. It needs an explicit compatible effect from the run's loot pool; simply combining two mechanical parts must not silently grant every support behavior.

A rooted device trades mobility for position. The enemy can threaten it with the same announced tool stroke or approach it while its useful arc faces elsewhere. It should not automatically negate every attack, and ordinary damage should remain useful against it. Enemies never confiscate an already-earned inventory part to reverse player progress.

Collection should show a physical part join the ball's accumulated exterior, with its usable companion behavior explained when fitted. The exact assembly UI, component slots and whether fitting is immediate remain open. The default learning encounter works without a companion chassis or an item-selection pause.

Class expression can build on the same opening: a grapple redirects the approach, a short hover crosses a foot stroke and returns to rolling, a laser lines up the housing, or lightning converts a primed cluster. These are opportunities rather than keys that unlock an otherwise immune enemy. Class-specific loot and ordinary stat rewards belong in the broader run pool; this encounter should not try to teach all four classes and the whole item system.

## Boss damage changes the place

Separate three visible consequences: a successful ordinary hit damages the machine; a targeted counter loosens a useful part; a structural break changes traversal. They do not all need to happen on every bump.

For the introductory exchange, detach an outer access plate and reveal the working mechanism. Later, sufficiently damaged pruning structures can fold into banks or ramps. Give that result a visible support and a usable landing area. A painting of a ramp does not prove it is traversable.

The huge machine offers increased reach and environmental consequences while the player remains within the current ordinary-growth envelope. A later colossal player transformation remains an open publisher direction. Nothing in this study settles that separate choice.

## Evidence and production landing

The grounding references remain the [University of Minnesota's pruning and training guidance](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/pruning-and-training-apple-trees) and [apple-growing guidance](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/growing-apples), with irrigation and binding sources in the [taxonomy source table](ENEMY-TAXONOMY.md). Real horticulture informs tool purpose and signs of care; combat force, autonomous opponents and giant scale are fictional adaptations.

The research connection is deliberate: collection has a visible physical consequence, collisions create an attack opportunity, and run loot can change what the next collision enables. That is the candidate identity developed in [the competitive research](MARKET-RESEARCH.md), not evidence of market demand for this exact encounter.

The next playable proof, once production scope is chosen, should contain one rolling hero, Snipcart, an outward-foot comparison, a single reachable large tool and collectible parts. A full boss roster, complete stage, all classes and co-op are unnecessary to answer the first question: **does the player learn a machine by moving around it, then enjoy using that knowledge against a larger relative?**

Observe route choice before and after the first missed cut, whether the player aims for the exposed mechanism without a text prompt, whether the largest ball keeps control, and whether players recognize the boss preparation. Try a strong damage build as well as a baseline build. Do not label success from a still image or prescribe numeric balance before those observations.

This study supplies art direction, pose intent and a reviewable encounter proposal. It does not contain a mesh, rig, animation clip, camera implementation or playable test.
