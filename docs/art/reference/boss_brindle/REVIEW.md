# Brindle model-sheet review

## Result

The requested images exist, but the exact-copy and same-scale requirements are not fully met. These are reference drafts for review, not reliable modelling blueprints. Use the approved board to resolve every conflict. Do not trace the turnaround dimensions or use its views as registered modelling planes.

I visually compared each generated view and detail panel with the supplied approved board. Three turnaround attempts, two detail attempts and two variant attempts were generated. I selected the third turnaround, second details and second variant. This is visual inspection of raster images, not a test against a shared 3D model. No human acceptance is claimed.

## Source hierarchy

1. Approved geometry and markings: bosses-world-boss-brindle.jpg.
2. Palette, shape and materials boards supplied with the request: finish only.
3. User instructions: exact palette roles, 1.84 m pilot-ball diameter, no invented markings, service effect remains proposed.

The generated sheets' internal captions are descriptive. Their claims of transcription do not override the discrepancies listed here.

## Scale and projection failures

- The five views are not at a common drawing scale. For example, the front crane span is visibly much larger on the page than the back span although both represent the same machine.
- The 11.0 m and 6.0 m body dimensions were provisional prompt assumptions. The source supplies neither dimension. They are not canon or measurements.
- The 11.0 m dimension endpoints do not reliably coincide with the complete crane envelope.
- The 1.84 m label is correct, but its pictured ball-to-body ratio does not reliably match the provisional 6 m height.
- The ball became a simplified triangular lattice sphere. It does not faithfully reproduce the petrol-and-copper Hoberman gyrosphere and cockpit.
- No independently measured orthographic camera or metre-to-pixel scale exists. The small amount of perspective remaining in some views is a failure of the requested sheet specification.

## Inferred construction register

These are all identified categories of hidden construction used or implied by the sheets. None is an approved design change.

1. Orientation convention: FRONT means the banner/nameplate face. RIGHT SIDE is interpreted as the service-hatch side seen on screen-left in the source three-quarter. Confirm this convention before rigging.
2. Service-hatch mounting face: near-front side/corner panel. The source does not settle the exact angle between this face and the nameplate face.
3. Rear shell: simple continuation of stepped oxide/petrol panels. Seam positions, rear lower box and its light are unconfirmed.
4. Rear maintenance rails/platform continuation and their support brackets.
5. Roof deck footprint, floor, rail connections, commander seat/controls and access route.
6. Rear utility equipment count, cross-sections, caps and placement. Extra cylinders visible in side/top views are unsupported additions.
7. Wheel topology: final turnaround suggests three main pods per side; variant explores four main corner pods total. The approved view does not resolve the arrangement.
8. Hidden axle locations, wheel thicknesses, fender backs, suspension links and small recessed rollers.
9. Underside structural rails, central clearances and recessed lower-front machinery.
10. Far-side crane root bearings, ram mountings, winch brackets, cable guides, reeving and swivel attachments.
11. Grapple rear-facing fingers, exact finger count, hinges and internal ring construction.
12. Hatch rear face, hinge travel, internal supports and plunger travel. No spring, clutch or release mechanism is established by the source.
13. Hidden edges of the upper louvers and small roof vents added by the generator.
14. Far-side lamp placement. The second front lamp is an unconfirmed symmetry assumption.
15. Banner suspension rod, hidden fastening and rear cloth face.
16. Provisional whole-body dimensions. Only the pilot ball diameter comes from the user.
17. Wear on hidden surfaces. Visible wear is also approximate, not a traced texture.

## Variant

boss_brindle-variant-1.png shows the four-main-corner-pod reading, with small recessed rollers, instead of the turnaround's apparent three-main-pods-per-side reading. The banner edge was corrected in a second generation.

The lower chassis inset did not become the requested side elevation; it reads as a frontal chassis view. The far corner pods remain obscured, so the image does not independently prove the total count. Upper armour, duplicated lamps, crest, grapple microgeometry and undercarriage retain the same kinds of drift as the turnaround. The variant is an ambiguity illustration, not an approved alternative or a mechanical resolution.

## Colour and canon checks

- Prompts specify the requested hex values and separate material roles. The renders visually retain oxide/petrol enamel, bone cover, brass ownership tag, charcoal rubber and mint contact.
- Exact albedo hex values cannot be verified from shaded raster pixels. Apply the user's hexes directly in material authoring.
- No danger footprint is included. No hazard striping, real-company branding, HIRED text or invented crew number is visibly added.
- Existing source crests are approximated. They must be traced from the approved source or supplied as approved decal art.
- The human utility-fleet history adds no new visible symbols here.
- Crane slack remains a proposed gameplay consequence. These static sheets do not establish which attack is interrupted.

## Questions for the modeller

1. Are there four main corner wheel pods, six main pods, or a different arrangement of wide split wheels and recessed rollers?
2. Does the hatch sit on a side wall or an angled front-corner face, and what defines the machine's forward travel direction?
3. What are the approved body width, length, height and crane operating envelope relative to the 1.84 m ball?
4. What is the actual crane boom plane, cable routing, finger count and ram attachment arrangement?
5. Which rear tanks, panels and rails should exist, and is there a rear service opening?
6. Can exact crest and nameplate artwork be supplied or traced from the approved board?
7. What is the hatch opening angle, plunger stroke and reel clearance?
8. Does reel contact interrupt the adjacent requisition crane as proposed, and which attack/state should it cancel?

Resolve wheel layout, orientation and dimensions before building one shared 3D blockout and rendering aligned orthographic views. Use the source board for visible design; use these generated sheets to discuss the unresolved construction.
