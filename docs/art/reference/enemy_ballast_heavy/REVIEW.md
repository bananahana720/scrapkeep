# Ballast heavy reference-sheet review

The files are saved, but the turnaround does not meet the requested exact modelling-reference standard. Three correction passes improved it without resolving view scale, lifting-lug geometry and brake-lever consistency. Use the approved board as the design authority. Do not trace the generated turnaround as calibrated geometry.

The details sheet is the stronger reference for the visible foot and linkage. Its stencil remains a reconstruction, not an exact trace. Both variants show questions to resolve, not approved designs.

## Source and review method

- Design authority: `enemies-highdraft-ballast-heavy-reference.jpg`, user image 1.
- Finish only: the supplied palette, shape and material boards. Their unrelated bodies and marks were excluded from the design brief.
- Compared every generated view visually with the source hero, scale, brake-foot, grown/tethered, brace, ram and ring-out panels. Compared visible parts across the generated views as well.
- Inspected each generated result when the image tool displayed it. Copies were not re-rendered or re-inspected solely because their path changed.
- No 3D reconstruction, camera calibration, rig test, exact decal tracing or numeric colour calibration was performed. A generated image is not proof of mechanical feasibility.

## Inferred construction

These decisions are provisional. None is established merely because it appears in a generated image.

1. Orientation: the broad face in the hero panel is called front; right side looks along the slab's long axis. The board does not name these axes.
2. Dimensions: 7.0 m length, 3.2 m depth, 4.4 m overall height and 1.25 m slab thickness were working estimates from the ball comparison. Only the 1.84 m pilot diameter is supplied canon. The sheets do not consistently realize the proposed dimensions.
3. Neutral pose: a level standing slab, matching leg angles and evenly planted feet, inferred from the board's differing brace and action poses.
4. Four feet: two near/far pairs at two end stations. The board occludes the far-side connectivity. A shared shoe with split pads is the alternative in variant 2.
5. Mirrored hidden load links, pivots, bearing caps and underside brackets. Their count, shaft lengths, handedness and rear-facing arrangement need confirmation.
6. Four top lifting loops in two paired stations. Visible loops support the general form; total count, rear positions, cross-section and mounting feet are inferred.
7. A plain rear and top slab, with the same bevel language and no hatch. Unseen seams, wall thickness and interior ballast are unspecified.
8. Repetition of the source brake lever at corresponding feet. The board directly shows one close-up; repeating its attachment and handedness is an inference.
9. Toe-pad centre split, heel blocks, sole continuity and interior foot shape. Variant 2 shows the meaningful connectivity choice.
10. One plate on the source-visible near assembly; no repeated rear or opposite plate. Its fastener treatment and decal are reconstructed. The generated orange mounting support is a mismatch, not a deliberate design addition.
11. Plain hidden fasteners, rear cheeks and paint coverage. Surface wear cannot be reconstructed exactly on unseen faces, and generated views also fail to match some seen-face wear.
12. Cable eye hardware on the inland slab end, single versus paired cable routing, cable diameter, anchor-post shape and stake fittings. The board supports a tether proposal but does not fully define these fittings.

## Unresolved global mismatches

The requested exact copy is not achieved. Besides the per-view differences above, paint wear is denser than the source in places, the oxide often renders brighter and more orange than the requested base colour, and several pale bearing caps, fastener counts and limb cross-sections differ. Neutral studio illumination changes perceived colour; the hex labels do not prove exact material values. Set materials from the user's hex values rather than sampling shaded pixels.

The primary turnaround omits cables to isolate the body, while the hero board includes a cable. This is a presentation choice, not evidence that a cable-free body is approved. Variant 1 retains both source tether readings. No weak point should be inferred from the brass plate or pale bearing caps.

## Questions before modelling or rigging

1. Are there four independent feet, two broad brake shoes, or another paired arrangement? Confirm the far-side load path.
2. Which face is mechanically the front, and where do the inland cable eyes attach?
3. Is the normal body tethered? Is the grown body distinguished by a second cable, or is the hero's second cable merely hidden?
4. What are the actual slab dimensions, machine height and leg spacing relative to the 1.84 m ball?
5. How many lifting loops exist, and where are their centres on the top surface?
6. Does each foot have the same long brake lever? Confirm its pivot centres and brace travel.
7. Is there a higher-resolution source for the brass stencil and its mounting plate? It needs an exact decal trace.
8. Is a service hatch or damage weak point planned? The supplied board establishes neither.

The next reliable step is a single 3D blockout with an actual 1.84 m sphere, followed by orthographic renders from that shared geometry. These generated sheets alone should not settle the unresolved dimensions or connectivity.
