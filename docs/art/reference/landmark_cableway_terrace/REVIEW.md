# Cableway terrace reference review

Date: 2026-10-23.

## Result

The requested turnaround and details PNGs are saved, with two ambiguity variants. They are useful visual references, but the turnaround does not pass the requested standard for consistent orthographic modelling. Do not trace its side or top view, derive dimensions from its pixels, or treat its rear as approved construction.

Nine image generations were inspected against the supplied approved board. The turnaround received two correction passes. The details sheet and each variant received one. Corrections removed several added objects, but projection and part-shape differences remain. No shared 3D model generated these views.

## Authority and scope

Design authority is the supplied highdraft-landmark-cableway-terrace.jpg. The palette, shape and material boards supplied finish guidance only. The cited Bible and Decisions passages were supplied as board notes; their full documents were not independently reviewed.

The landmark includes its supporting rock terraces, clamped boulder, road, towers, basket, winches, canopies, furnace and repair activity. Distant mountains, trees, sky and smoke were omitted. The pilot ball is a scale reference, not another cable car.

No readable crew number, HIRED stamp, faction mark or company logo was found on this board. None was intentionally added. The visible gloves were retained. No new mint landmark weak point was invented. The source's mint pilot tool tip remains on the scale ball.

## Inferred construction

These items are not approved facts:

1. The finite site boundary, rock backs and undersides, shelf depths, terrace connections and continuation of the road beyond the crop.
2. Hidden pylon faces, foot anchors, upper blocks partly outside the original crop, rear collars, stay attachment and bearings.
3. Which cable carries load, which drives motion, hidden return routing, guide-wheel arrangement and tensioning. Two visible cables do not settle the mechanism.
4. Trolley rear rollers, axle ends, hook closures, strap attachment details and basket rear/underside bracing.
5. Hidden cargo and back faces of the holed scrap. Exact cargo count cannot be recovered.
6. Boulder rear shape, cavity depth, whether cavities connect, hidden bands, clamp anchors and shaft terminations.
7. Power winch rear/end cap, enclosure seams, drum connection, frame joints, rear railing and chimney mounting.
8. The removable service cover and plain axle/bearing cavity. No service hatch or weak point is established by the board.
9. Canopy depth, cloth seams, rear tie-offs, unseen posts and whether the cropped lower cloth is a separate cover.
10. Furnace interior depth and relation to the chimney. Their visual proximity does not prove a duct connection.
11. Repair-winch rear, net back side and knot pattern, glove backs, unseen tool and bag surfaces, and crew rear anatomy/clothing.
12. Unseen pilot-ball lattice and cockpit details. The specified diameter is canonical; these sheets are not a pilot construction reference.

## Dimensions and colour

Only the 1.84 m pilot-ball diameter comes from the user. The initial prompt proposed a 13.0 m site width, 7.0 m height and depth, 3.5 m basket clearance and 3.0 m road width to guide generation. These were invented layout assumptions, not measurements of the approved board. The sheet marks station dimensions provisional and does not consistently retain all requested dimension lines. Clearance and road width are not verified.

The prompts contain the supplied exact colour hexes. Shaded raster pixels are not an exact palette specification. Use petrol #365754, oxide #9b513a, sandstone #c6a274, charcoal #242a28, brass #d9b44a, copper #b77843, bone #d2c5a5, amber #e5a044, frost #c8f0ff and mint #8bf0d3 in the actual material definitions. Mint remains restricted by the user's weak-point rule. No hazard-coral body paint was intentionally introduced. Worker lilac follows the approved image visually; it has no authoritative numeric value here.

## Variants

- Variant 1 proposes moving the existing transport span farther behind the work terrace. It retains one basket and the main assemblies. It is marked unapproved. The “plan” still has tilt, changed support depths and unverified geometry; it conveys placement only.
- Variant 2 reads the cropped lower yellow cloth as a separate third cover. Its outer support and depth are explicitly inferred. A correction removed an added fourth cloth extension, isolated post, tool crate, bottle and foreground chain. Bags, posts and workshop proportions still differ from the source.

The main sheets use the two-primary-canopy interpretation. This is a provisional choice, not a finding that the source contains exactly two cloth pieces. Variant 2 preserves the other reading for review.

## Questions for the modeller

1. Keep the loaded basket above the road as shown, or move the line rearward as variant 1 proposes?
2. Is the cropped lower yellow cloth a separate cover, a continuation of another canopy, or another object?
3. What are the actual span, tower height, terrace depth, basket travel envelope and clearance above a 1.84 m ball?
4. What are the two cables' mechanical roles, return paths and tensioning method?
5. Is the large holed object natural rock, worked stone or a utility component, and how is its restraint anchored?
6. Does the winch need service access? If so, approve the removable-cover proposal or supply the intended construction.
7. Which rear surfaces, cargo arrangement, bolts, ropes and tools must match exactly before a measured model is built?

## Files and verification

The four final PNGs are accompanied by PROMPTS.md, this review and FILE_CHECKS.json. The JSON records dimensions, byte sizes, SHA-256 hashes and PNG integrity checks. Intermediate images are kept in review-history/ and their exact generation prompts are in PROMPTS.md.

The first file-check attempt could not import Pillow. The fallback uses Python's standard library to check PNG signatures, every chunk CRC, IDAT decompression, decoded scanline lengths and IEND. This checks file integrity, not visual or mechanical accuracy. This directory is not a usable Git repository, so no commit was made.

The next reliable production step is a measured 3D blockout with fixed orthographic cameras. These sheets need that reconciliation before they can serve as matching front, side, back and top construction drawings.
