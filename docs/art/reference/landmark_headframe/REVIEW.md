# Landmark headframe review

## Status

Delivered two reference sheets and one explicitly provisional cable variant. These are generated visual references, not a construction-consistent orthographic drawing set. Repeated correction passes fixed several visible failures, but exact copying and equal scale across every view remain unmet. Use the approved board to resolve conflicts. Do not trace hidden construction from these sheets without checking it in a shared 3D blockout.

The approved source is `dig-landmark-headframe-noon.jpg`. The three other supplied boards informed finish only. No historical project design decisions were used.

## Source facts and dimensional authority

- The board shows a tall solid-legged headframe, two exposed spoked sheaves, crown balcony, overhead crossbar with two short unlit projections, central guide/ladder structure, intermediate landings, low crossmembers, separate foot blocks, an offset winding house, connecting low gallery, ground rails and nearby rectangular utility housings.
- The supplied notes specify a 120 m headframe, a 100 m pit and a 40 m tipple. The pit and its five bench faces are environment, so they are absent from the isolated sheets.
- The notes do not identify which axis or endpoints the 40 m tipple measurement spans. The final turnaround preserves that uncertainty in its caption.
- The 1.84 m pilot ball is a separate scale reference. It is not part of the landmark. Its required diameter relative to tower height is 1.84 / 120 = 0.0153333.
- No headframe weak point, ancient beacon, glove mark, crew number, HIRED stamp or company/faction logo is legible in the board. None was deliberately added.

### Scale and projection failures that remain

- On the 1536 x 1024 turnaround, top-row tower height is roughly 425 pixels; the three-quarter tower is roughly 400 pixels, judged from finial to concrete base. That is about 6% drift despite a targeted correction. Do not scale one view from another.
- At 425 pixels per 120 m, a correct pilot ball would be about 6.5 pixels across. The rendered ball appears closer to 9–10 pixels and its lattice is not sufficiently resolved. The printed 1.84 m label is the authority; the drawn ball is not an accurate gauge.
- The top footprint differs from the front footprint at the claimed common scale. No exact footprint width or depth is established.
- The false scale bar was removed. Thin 120 m height dimension remains, but it does not turn these raster images into measured drawings.
- Orthographic captions describe the requested camera setup. They do not prove the generated structures are projections of one consistent object.

## Inferred parts and construction

Every item below is an inference, not a newly established design fact.

1. Front/right/back coordinate convention. “Front” is the broad visible face in the board; the winding house is to its right.
2. Hidden rear legs and their depth, spacing, tilt, cross-section, rear crossmembers and diagonal bracing. The main sheets extend the visible solid-plate language to the unseen faces.
3. Rear and side crown support geometry, axle support blocks, bearing housings, wheel thickness, grooves, spokes, hub fasteners and exact wheel-axis relationship.
4. Crown deck depth, underside supports, rear railing and service access. The two visible top projections are treated as unlit metal finials; their actual purpose is unknown.
5. Rope circuits, returns, tangency, hidden shaft descent and winding-house entry points. Main-sheet prompts assume the leftward lines return toward the shaft; the final drawings do not prove a continuous physically workable route.
6. Shaft guide-rail section, ladder construction and partial cage, landing floors, rear platforms, rail post spacing and exact platform levels.
7. Foundation depths, foot sleeves, steel base plates, corner anchors and bolted plate-joint construction. Source supports square blocks and fixings, not exact bolt specifications.
8. Winding-house back and side walls, roof panel divisions, roof entry fittings, underside, rear service rails and hidden gallery supports.
9. Utility cabinet backs, door seams, small pipework, mounting pads and minor guardrail connections.
10. Proposed removable four-fastener service cover, its recess, bearing assembly and cable-access opening. Both open and closed states are speculative. No weak point is inferred from them.
11. Wear, edge exposure and bolt/seam placement on hidden faces. Even visible-face wear was interpreted rather than transferred exactly.
12. The tiny pilot-ball rendering uses a simplified lattice indication. The board's foreground pilot is perspective-enlarged and cannot determine its size beside this tower.
13. Variant only: two static crown-frame backstays and two unseen ground anchor blocks. Dashed anchors indicate uncertainty, not their size or placement in the game.

## Cable variant

`landmark_headframe-variant-1.png` shows the alternative that the board's two leftward diagonal lines are static backstays attached to the crown frame. Their endpoints are unseen in the board. The variant labels the ground anchors as hypothetical and is explicitly NOT APPROVED.

Checked the alternate sheet for the same two-wheel crown, main solid legs, open shaft, side landings and right-hand winding-house assembly. It preserves these main features but also has small railing, wear and fixture drift. Only the cable interpretation is offered for consideration. Do not adopt its other incidental changes. The dashed anchors float diagrammatically to the left; their placement is not a measured site layout.

One variant was made. Remaining ambiguities concern dimensions and hidden detailing rather than two equally evidenced body designs.

## Colour and material checks

Requested exact base-colour targets are petrol #365754, oxide #9b513a, sandstone #c6a274, charcoal #242a28, brass #d9b44a, copper #b77843 and bone #d2c5a5. Their text labels are legible on the detail sheet. Amber #e5a044 and frost #c8f0ff were not assigned to invented parts. Mint #8bf0d3, ancient green and hazard coral were not introduced as body paint or weak points.

Rendered lighting and generation alter pixels; exact base-colour values are not certified by the raster sheets. Set shader base colours from the hex values, then review the lit result. Metal bevels remain crisp, though some added seams and fasteners are more detailed than the distant source can establish.

## Changes made after inspection

1. Regenerated turnaround to align elevation heights, place the house behind the right-side projection and remove dangling cable stubs.
2. Regenerated turnaround again to resolve the apparent four-sheave top view into two continuous wheel profiles and remove the incorrect scale bar. Equal-scale and pilot-ball corrections did not fully succeed.
3. Regenerated details to label the closed hatch as inferred, fix the winding-house caption and remove invented triangular foot gussets.
4. Added the separately labelled backstay interpretation instead of silently treating the unseen cable path as canon.

## Questions for the modeller before a final mesh

1. Which axis and endpoints define the 40 m tipple, and which visible assembly is included?
2. Does 120 m end at the highest finial, crown beam or another datum? The sheets assume the highest finial.
3. How are the two sheave axes oriented in plan, and are their diameters equal? The board alone does not settle both.
4. Are the leftward diagonal lines moving hoist ropes or static backstays? Where do all rope circuits terminate?
5. What are the approved tower footprint, winding-house dimensions and tower-to-house offset?
6. Which landings, ladders, guards and supports continue onto hidden faces?
7. Should the proposed service cover and plain bearing recess exist at all? There is no source-approved open hatch or weak point.

Resolve these in one shared blockout, then render the five cameras from that object at a fixed orthographic scale. That is the required next step for dependable modelling dimensions.

## Delivery verification

Visual review used each generated result shown by the image tool. It was not a 3D, topology, structural, engineering or human acceptance test. A Python Pillow import was unavailable, so PNG signatures and dimensions were checked with the standard library instead. The current directory is not a Git repository; no commit was possible.
