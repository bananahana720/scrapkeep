# Sinkhole reference review

## Disposition

The requested PNGs and exact prompts are saved. These are annotated visual references, not a tracing-accurate construction package. The strict same-scale orthographic and exact part-copy requirements remain unmet after regeneration. A modeller must use the approved board and the written dimensions to resolve the issues below before building final geometry.

Reviewed against the supplied image 1, dig-landmark-sinkhole-afternoon.jpg. Images 2–4 supplied finish guidance only. No source repository, game scene or hidden 3D model was available or inspected. Review consists of direct visual comparison of the supplied board and each generated image displayed by the image tool. It does not establish 3D consistency or owner acceptance.

User-specified dimensions govern: 250 m opening, 120 m depth, pilot ball diameter 1.84 m. The board is a perspective illustration and does not establish exact component measurements, underground geology, or hidden circumference.

## What was checked

- Dark fractured basalt cap above pale limestone.
- Continuous wall gallery, timber deck and rail, dark bolted post collars and diagonal iron supports.
- Open stepped bowl and water table, without structures crossing the pool.
- Left entry service assembly, including thick collared upright, thinner vertical runs, wall fixings and sagging cable.
- Single narrow ancient column on opposite wall, with upper circular drum, narrow green strip and low base.
- Lower pipe assembly to the board viewer's right of the column.
- No invented faction insignia, crew numbers, gloves, HIRED stamps or danger paint.
- No claimed service hatch or weak point. Neither can be identified in the board.
- View projection, component placement, scale labels and pilot reference.
- Grey sheet background, readable captions and PNG integrity.

## Inferred construction inventory

1. Nearly circular hidden rim outline and its deviations. The 250 m opening comes from the notes, not a measured image.
2. Complete hidden gallery circumference. A full ring is specified by the notes; its exact hidden bay sequence is unknown.
3. Hidden south and east wall strata, ledges and irregular block shapes.
4. Rim-to-bowl depth distribution, basalt cap thickness and limestone shelf elevations. Only total depth is specified.
5. Gallery elevation. Approximate mid-depth placement follows the board but is not measured.
6. Gallery deck width, rail height, post spacing, beam lengths, bracket spacing and fastener counts. The initial prompt proposed 3 m width and 1.1 m rail height; these are not approved dimensions.
7. Concealed gallery anchors, wall-plate backs and under-deck connections.
8. Timber continuity, hidden rail ends and concealed gallery approach.
9. Entry roof extent and its connection to the cap. Main sheet assumes a shallow undercut; the variant explores a short roofed alcove.
10. Thick service upright cross-section, concealed lower end and exact collar count.
11. Cable terminals, rear conduit routing and thin service-post cross-sections.
12. Ancient column depth, back, attachment, internal function and base depth.
13. Ancient drum thickness, band arrangement and hidden fittings.
14. Exact ancient stripe symbols. Pixels do not establish a readable glyph sequence.
15. Pipe diameters, elbow construction, concealed/submerged ends and canister connections. Final generated twin-leg circuit is an unsupported addition, not an accepted inference.
16. Water depth, underwater rock geometry and pool perimeter outside the visible foreground.
17. Cut faces and exterior geological thickness shown for illustration.
18. Sparse scrub placement on hidden surfaces.
19. North/east/south/west assignment used to organize views. The board specifies no compass bearing.
20. Small pilot lattice and cockpit details beyond what is visible in the board. Pilot is a scale object, not part of the sinkhole.

## Alternate reading

landmark_sinkhole-variant-1.png shows the foreground roof as a short natural rock-roofed alcove instead of a shallow rim undercut. Both fit the visible ceiling because its hidden extent is not shown. Only one variant is supplied.

The variant is unapproved. It preserves the foreground reveal more clearly than the turnaround, but adds a third collar to the thick upright and changes the cable routing. Its right section retains some depth cues. The footer's claim that only roof depth changes is therefore too strong; these incidental deviations must not propagate to a model.

## Questions for the modeller

1. Where does the entry gallery meet the overhang, and how far does that roof extend?
2. Does 120 m mean rim to exposed floor, water surface or submerged lowest point?
3. What are the gallery elevation, usable width, railing height and repeat bay spacing at the stated 250 m scale?
4. What is the approved hidden wall outline and gallery approach?
5. Is the ancient circular feature a shallow drum or a rounded enclosure, and what is its exact side profile?
6. What pattern belongs on the ancient strip, and how narrow must it remain through the plinth?
7. How do the small canister and lower pipes connect where rock obscures them?
8. What is the function and exact construction of the thick entry upright?
9. Which visible source proportions should be retained if they conflict with literal 250 m / 120 m scale?

Build a dimensioned blockout with shared cameras before producing tracing plans. Reproject all views from that single model to resolve scale and rotation consistency.
