## Contract

### What this section fixes and what it refuses to fix

It fixes who may write, in what order, what is held when any step fails, what is preserved, what may never be resumed, and which interruption points must later be tested. It refuses to fix the storage mechanism that would make a commit durable, because TADR-004 line 38 refuses to accept a temporary file, a rename, a checksum or a backup as proof of power-loss durability and no execution on either target OS family exists at this base. Every durability statement below is therefore a *requirement on a later qualification*, not a claim about behaviour. The literal marker **"unqualified on paper; re-verify when S0-08 lands, and again when the TH-22 proof lands"** is carried by every ordering stage; the S0-08 half of that trigger has already fired and is reconciled by source review at the end of this Contract, leaving the TH-22 half live.

### The vocabulary

Four vocabularies are closed. Nothing outside them appears in the tables below.

**The four observables** are HF-07b's, unchanged: `C` the committed aggregate, `S` the staged candidate set, `A` the acknowledgement set, `L` Session's live state (HF-07b lines 26 to 29). `A` is live only and leaves no durable trace (HF-07b line 169), so no recovery decision may read it.

**The six recovery statuses** are the S0-07 proof's (S0-07 line 42), adopted here as the contract's status set and published as `RecoveryDiagnostic` (HF-03a line 35).

| Status | Meaning | Playable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `missing` | No committed record exists at the committed path. | Yes, as a first launch; nothing is restored and nothing is overwritten. |
| `committed` | The committed record parses, is supported, and no preserved candidate contradicts it. | Derived from `run.lifecycle.terminal` (HF-07a rule 2, line 61). |
| `committed_stale_temp` | The committed record is usable and one or more preserved candidates exist that cannot carry a newer lifecycle fact. | Derived, as `committed`. The preserved candidates are listed and never deleted. |
| `corrupt` | The committed record does not parse, or a required key is absent or mistyped (HF-07a line 56). | **No.** |
| `unsupported_newer` | The committed record's `schema_version` exceeds what this build supports. | **No.** Inspectable without becoming playable (TADR-004 line 40). |
| `ambiguous_lifecycle` | Lifecycle continuity cannot be established: a newer record may carry a spent-revival or terminal fact that the record being offered as playable does not. | **No.** |

Two further statuses are named for completeness and are **not** resolved here: `incompatible_content` (the record parses and is supported, but a domain ID does not resolve under its `content_revision`; TADR-004 line 34, HF-05 line 27) and any migration outcome. The compatibility predicate, the accept/migrate/reject decision and the migrated commit are HF-09's (Specify schema and gameplay-content migration). This section asserts only the three rules that hold for them regardless of HF-09's answer: the source is preserved and never overwritten, the record is not playable until the question is settled, and the hold is never resolved by rolling back to a living generation.

**The five message classes** are the `SaveError` and `RecoveryDiagnostic` value classes Persistence publishes (HF-03a line 35). They are engineering classes, not strings, not wording and not layout; the view, the focus behaviour and the pointer behaviour are PD-02's.

| Class | What it asserts | Retry offered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `MC-RETRYABLE` | A recoverable save error whose cause may no longer hold on a second attempt. The transition is held and is not reported as saved (TADR-004 line 32). | Yes, as the same transaction identity. |
| `MC-INVALID` | The candidate is not eligible. Retrying the identical bytes cannot succeed. | No. A fresh candidate may be staged from live state. |
| `MC-UNSUPPORTED` | The stored record cannot be read by this build. Nothing may be written over it. | No. |
| `MC-AMBIGUOUS` | Lifecycle continuity cannot be established. No automatic resolution exists. | No. |
| `MC-PREFERENCE` | A preferences write failed. It is not a critical write (HF-07b line 171) and holds no gameplay transition. | Yes, as a preferences write. |

**The three path roles.** The committed path holds `C`. A candidate path holds one member of `S` and is derived deterministically from that candidate's `transaction_id` and its attempt ordinal, counting from 1 and increasing by one for each re-entry at WO1 for that identity, so two candidates never share a path — including two attempts at one transaction — and a candidate never shares the committed path (S0-07 line 34). The ordinal exists so that a retry writes a new path instead of writing over the partial candidate P1 preserves; without it, "a fresh candidate path derived from the same `transaction_id`" would be the preserved path and the retry would violate P2. Attempts of one `transaction_id` are attempts at one transition, not two transitions, which is what AC-2 turns on. A preserved path holds a record this contract refuses to destroy. All three are in one directory on one filesystem, because a replace that crosses a filesystem is a copy and loses the property a replace exists for. The concrete directory and file names are **not** fixed here: they live under the engine's user data directory (receipt lines 110 to 132, `OS.get_user_data_dir`, and lines 174 to 180, `data_paths.html`), and naming them is TH-21's and an implementation card's. `RecoveryState` holds the preserved-source references (HF-03a line 49); no envelope field records them, because HF-07a line 201 rejects a backup pointer and a recovery status inside the aggregate.

### R1. The single-writer protocol

TADR-004 line 38 requires single-writer ownership for each target OS. Five rules, each with the authority it comes from. Rules W1 to W3 are properties of this application's structure and are established by the module contract. Rule W4 is a property of the operating system and is **unqualified on paper; re-verify when S0-08 lands, and again when the TH-22 proof lands**.

| ID | Rule | Source | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| W1 | Persistence alone stages, replaces and writes the files of `DurableAggregate`, `SaveTransactionState` and `PreferencesRecord`. No other module opens the save directory for writing, and Session installs a loaded value into fresh live records rather than writing one. | HF-03a lines 35 and 49 | Structural. Enforced by the module boundary, not by the filesystem. |
| W2 | At most one transaction is in flight per document. A second durable transition does not begin stage 1 until the in-flight transition has reached acknowledge or has been held by the hold rule. The four stages never interleave with another transaction's stages over the same document. | HF-07b lines 37 to 40 and 42; TADR-004 line 28 | Structural. |
| W3 | The aggregate and the preferences record are serialised independently of each other. They are separate documents with separate paths, no shared key and no ordering between them, so an interleaving cannot produce a mixed state. What is forbidden is coupling, not concurrency: a preferences write never holds a gameplay transition and no gameplay transition waits on a preferences write. | HF-07b line 171; HF-07a line 204; TADR-004 line 24 | Structural. |
| W4 | A second process writing the same user data directory is outside W1 to W3 and is not excluded by them. Until an exclusion mechanism is selected and qualified per OS family, a second concurrent instance is an unbounded hazard, not a handled failure: it can interleave a replace with another writer's validate and defeat D1.3, which re-reads candidate bytes from storage. The contract therefore **requires** a per-OS exclusion mechanism and **claims none**. | TADR-004 line 38; S0-08 line 99 ("Single-writer exclusion between processes was not asserted") | **unqualified on paper; re-verify when S0-08 lands, and again when the TH-22 proof lands** |
| W5 | An exclusion mechanism, once selected, may not become a second authority over gameplay facts. It may refuse a write; it may not decide playability, resolve a lifecycle flag, delete a preserved file or acknowledge a transaction. | HF-07b lines 31 and 169; HF-03a line 49 | Structural constraint on W4's eventual answer. |

W4 has a visible consequence for the recovery path as well as the write path. Because `A` leaves no durable trace, a second process cannot be detected after the fact from the files alone; a foreign candidate is indistinguishable from this process's own interrupted candidate. That is why the ambiguity checklist below keys on the *content* of a preserved candidate rather than on its author.

### R2. Write, flush and commit ordering

Six ordered steps, `WO1` to `WO6`, refine HF-07b's stages 1 to 4 without changing them. They are this section's write-flow steps; HF-04a's `O1` to `O4` are the runtime lifecycle's operations — release held physical input, cancel prepared actions, solo pause and resume — and are always named as such below. Stage 3 is split at the replace because that is where the observable state changes and where the interruption matrix needs two distinct rows. Every step carries the marker in its own column; `ok` in the last column would be a claim this section is not entitled to make.

| Step | HF-07b stage | Action | Observable effect | Qualification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WO1 | 1 stage | Open the candidate path for writing. The path is derived from `transaction_id` and this attempt's ordinal, as the three path roles fix it, so a re-entry at WO1 for the same identity opens the next ordinal's path rather than a preserved one, and it is never the committed path. A failure to open surfaces as a save error before any byte is written. | `C` unchanged; nothing enters `S`. | **unqualified on paper; re-verify when S0-08 lands, and again when the TH-22 proof lands** |
| WO2 | 1 stage | Store the complete serialised candidate in one write, with the array order HF-07a rule 5 (line 64) fixes. A partial candidate is never left as the only artifact of an attempt without also being recognisable as partial at recovery. | `C` unchanged; `S` gains an image that is written but not yet flushed. | **unqualified on paper; re-verify when S0-08 lands, and again when the TH-22 proof lands** |
| WO3 | 1 stage | Flush, check the error state, then close. Flush is documented as writing the file's buffer to disk (receipt lines 29 to 42 and 67 to 81); it is not documented as reaching the storage device, and S0-08 line 99 records that `FileAccess.flush` returning `OK` "is a library claim about buffered data and not a device guarantee". A successful flush therefore establishes that the write path reported no error, and nothing more. | `C` unchanged; `S` holds a complete image whose durability is unknown. | **unqualified on paper; re-verify when S0-08 lands, and again when the TH-22 proof lands** |
| WO4 | 2 validate | Re-read the candidate bytes from storage, never the in-memory object, and run the gates of HF-07b lines 38, 79, 88, 97, 108 and 119. Re-reading is what makes eligibility a property of what is stored (HF-07b line 54, D1.3); it is not a durability check, because a read can be served from the same cache the write went to. | `C` unchanged; the candidate is eligible or rejected and preserved. | **unqualified on paper; re-verify when S0-08 lands, and again when the TH-22 proof lands** |
| WO5 | 3 commit | Replace the committed record with the validated candidate, in one directory, on one filesystem. **A rename alone does not prove durability** (TADR-004 line 38, verbatim: "Temporary files, a rename, a checksum or a backup alone do not prove power-loss durability."). What a replace can give is an ordering property — no reader observes a partially replaced committed path — and even that property is per-OS and unqualified here. Whatever makes the directory entry itself durable is a separate step with no API in this card's receipt. | `C` becomes `G+1`/`T'` and carries the transition; `A` does **not** yet contain `T'`; `L` still holds. | **unqualified on paper; re-verify when S0-08 lands, and again when the TH-22 proof lands** |
| WO6 | 4 acknowledge | Only after WO5 returns success for this exact `transaction_id`, record it in `A` and release `L` through O4 of the runtime lifecycle (HF-07b lines 40 and 71). `A` is live only and writes no file, so WO6 has no filesystem step and no qualification question of its own. | `A` gains `T'`; `C` unchanged. | Structural (HF-07b line 169): `A` is live only, so this stage writes no file and has no durability question of its own. The ordering it depends on does: WO5's returned success is only as meaningful as QW2 and QW3 make it, so this stage is **unqualified on paper; re-verify when S0-08 lands, and again when the TH-22 proof lands** on both OS families like every stage above it. |

Three ordering prohibitions follow, and each closes a false-success path the card's verification bullet names.

1. **No acknowledgement before a returned commit.** WO6 runs only on WO5's success for the same identity (HF-07b line 52, D1.1). The S0-08 `commit_path` case observed the refusal and the later record (S0-08 line 46).
2. **No success report from a step that only reported no error.** WO3's flush result, WO5's replace result and a subsequent successful read are each necessary and none is sufficient for the word "saved". Until the qualification questions in R3 are answered on an OS family, the strongest true statement after WO5 on that family is "the committed record now reads as the new generation", not "the transition is durable".
3. **No reuse of a candidate across transactions.** WO5 accepts only the candidate that last returned eligible from WO4 for that `transaction_id` (HF-07b line 53, D1.2). A re-staged candidate is a new attempt and re-enters at WO1.

The numbered sub-questions, identical for both families:

- **QW1.** Does a completed WO3 guarantee that the candidate's bytes survive a power loss occurring immediately afterwards, on this OS and filesystem? The documented sentence "writes the file's buffer to disk" does not distinguish the application buffer from the device.
- **QW2.** Does a completed WO5 guarantee that the *directory entry* naming the committed path survives a power loss occurring immediately afterwards? No API in the receipt addresses this, and S0-08 line 99 records that directory-entry durability after rename was not asserted.
- **QW3.** Is the WO5 replace observed atomically by a concurrent or subsequent reader on this OS and filesystem — never a partially replaced committed path, never a missing one?
- **QW4.** What mechanism excludes a second process from the same user data directory on this OS, and does it survive a killed process without leaving a stale exclusion that locks a legitimate launch out of its own save?
- **QW5.** When the volume is full or the path is not writable, does every one of WO1, WO2, WO3 and WO5 report a distinguishable error rather than a short or silent success? The receipt documents `DirAccess.open` returning `null` with `get_open_error()` (receipt lines 83 to 105) but supplies no excerpt for `FileAccess`'s error accessors, so the error surface itself is unqualified.

Until QW1 to QW5 are answered on a family, this contract's guarantees on that family are exactly the ordering and the holds, which are properties of the program, and not durability, which is a property of the platform.

Reclaiming preserved artifacts is an unresolved policy in the Unresolved questions below.

**No failure below `MC-AMBIGUOUS` ever goes backwards.** Rolling `C` back to an older generation is not in any permitted cell. The only writes that ever change `C` are WO5 for a candidate that passed WO4 at a strictly greater generation.

**Why refusal rather than rollback.** The tempting alternative is to fall back to the last generation that reads as living so the player keeps playing. Restoring an unsafe state is unrecoverable; refusing is not.

### R7. The ambiguity-conditions checklist

Lifecycle continuity is established when, and only when, the record about to become playable can be shown to carry every durable lifecycle fact that any other readable artifact in the save directory may carry. Each condition below defeats that demonstration. If any condition holds, the status is `ambiguous_lifecycle`, or `corrupt` or `unsupported_newer` where the row states one, nothing is playable, everything is preserved, and no older living generation is selected. AC-6 is the one row that is a rejection rather than a refusal, and it is listed so the checklist keeps the two apart.

| ID | Condition | Why continuity fails | Source | Resolution at this base |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AC-1 | A preserved candidate parses, its `generation` is strictly greater than the committed generation, **and** its resolved lifecycle differs from the committed lifecycle — `run.lifecycle.revival_spent` or `run.lifecycle.terminal` true where `C` has false. | The newer artifact may have been committed and then partially lost, or may never have committed. The files cannot distinguish the two, because acknowledgement leaves no durable trace and QW3 leaves the replace's atomicity unqualified, and one of the two branches means the revival is spent or the run is closed. | TADR-004 line 40; S0-07 line 42; S0-08 line 51 (`interruption_ambiguous`) | Refuse. `C` stays the source of record, `playable` is false, both files are preserved. |
| AC-2 | Two or more preserved candidates parse at generations strictly greater than the committed generation **and they do not all carry one `transaction_id`**. | Even if each individually matched `C`'s lifecycle, their mutual order and which, if any, committed cannot be read from the files, and `A` leaves no durable trace. | AC-3 | The committed record does not parse, or a required key is absent or mistyped, **and** any other artifact exists in the save directory. | The lifecycle facts of the record that was most recently authoritative cannot be read at all, so no other artifact can be shown not to be older than it. | HF-07a line 56; TADR-004 lines 34 and 40 | Refuse, with status `corrupt`. | AC-5 | `run` is present and `run.lifecycle` is absent, or a lifecycle flag is present with a non-boolean value. | An absent required key is invalid data and never a default, so the flags are unknown rather than false. | HF-07a lines 56 and 105; TADR-004 line 32 | Refuse, with status `corrupt`. |

| AC-7 | An artifact exists whose `transaction_id` this owner never staged in this session and whose generation is greater than `C`'s. | Under W4 no exclusion mechanism is qualified, so a second process may have produced it; authorship cannot be read from the bytes. | TADR-004 line 38; S0-08 line 99 | Refuse, by AC-1 or AC-2 according to its lifecycle facts. |

**The one condition deliberately excluded, and why.** A preserved candidate that parses, has a strictly greater generation, and whose lifecycle facts are **identical** to the committed record's is *not* ambiguous. It is `committed_stale_temp`: preserved, listed, and the committed record stays playable. S0-07 line 113 records the open option of tightening the rule to "any newer valid temp blocks resumption", and this section declines to tighten it, for three reasons. First, no source requires it: TADR-004 line 40 names a newer *spent-revival or terminal* result. Second, such a candidate can only be carrying a level completion, a settlement, an offer or profile knowledge, and none of those was ever presented as done, because `L` may not present a transition as done before its identity is in `A` (HF-07b line 31) and playable state resumes only after acknowledgement (HF-07b line 71, SEAM-6). Replaying that content is a repeat of work already held in front of the player, not a duplicated reward: the settlement's `settlement_id` never entered `C`, so N1 and N2 are untouched. Third, tightening it would turn the ordinary outcome of any power loss during a save into an unplayable profile, which trades a real and common denial of service for no invariant. The cost of being wrong here is one replayed level; the cost of being wrong under AC-1 is a refilled revival or a resurrected run, which the Bible forbids outright (BIBLE line 584). This decision is recorded as consuming S0-07 line 113 and is a re-verification trigger below.

### R9. The failure half of a preferences write

HF-07b line 171 decided the preferences protocol: the same four stages over its own document and its own `preferences.schema_version`, no `generation`, no `transaction_id`, no convergence identity, no ordering against the aggregate, no place in `A`, and **not a critical write** under TADR-004 line 32. HF-07a line 204 records that it carries no `content_revision` and no gameplay progress. The HF-07b review reserved the failure-handling half to this card; these are the rules it adds and none of them changes a stage or a field.

| ID | Rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PW1 | A failed preferences write holds exactly one thing: the durable preference record. The changed value stays applied for the running session, because Presentation consumes preferences as a snapshot rather than through a durable read (HF-03a line 51). Reverting the live setting would add a second visible failure to a non-critical one. | HF-03a lines 49 and 51; HF-07b line 171 |
| PW2 | It shows `MC-PREFERENCE` in the settings surface, stating that the setting is active now and was not saved. The wording is PD-02's; the assertion is that no message may say saved and no message may imply gameplay progress was affected. | TADR-004 line 32; BIBLE line 602 |
| PW3 | It holds no gameplay transition, delays none, and fails none. Symmetrically, no gameplay transition waits on it. A preferences failure can never put a run into `SAVE_HELD_RETRYABLE` or `RECOVERY_BLOCKED`. | HF-07b line 171; TADR-004 line 24 |
| PW4 | Retry is a preferences write of the current live values, not of the failed candidate's bytes. There is no transaction identity to reuse and nothing to converge, so the retry is simply the next attempt. Its failure repeats PW1 to PW3 and nothing accumulates. | HF-07b line 171; HF-07a line 204 |
| PW5 | On the next launch the last committed preferences record loads. A value that only ever lived in a failed write is gone, and that is the correct outcome: the player was told it was not saved. No fallback writes it into the aggregate, and no aggregate field can hold it (HF-07a line 191 fixes the envelope at six keys). | HF-07a lines 191 and 204 |
| PW6 | A corrupt or unsupported preferences record at load is preserved untouched, the session runs on the product's default values, and the player is told the record could not be read. | PW7 | A later preferences write must not overwrite a preserved unreadable record. Preservation is the same rule P1 to P3 give the aggregate, applied to the second document. | TADR-004 line 34; HF-03a line 49 |
| PW8 | The preferences document's own single-writer rules are W1 to W3. W4's unqualified cross-process hazard applies to it equally, with a smaller consequence: a lost or mixed preferences record costs settings, not lifecycle facts. | W1 to W4 above |

What this half deliberately does not decide: whether a settings screen blocks its own exit while a preferences write is in flight, what the error looks like, and how many times a retry is offered. The first two are PD-02's; the third is a numeric threshold this section does not set.

## Scenarios

Seven worked cases. The first four are the card's required review cases; each is read for the two named defects, **false success** (the game says or implies a durable result that is not durable, or says loaded when it did not load) and **silent data loss** (a durable fact disappears, is replaced by an older one, or is duplicated, without the player being told). None of these was executed; each is the contract read against its sources, and the paper check under Procedure performed replays a model of the recovery decision for all of them.

### V1. Corrupt newest generation

The committed record was replaced and the replace did not complete, or the volume damaged it; `save.json` no longer parses. A candidate from the interrupted attempt is present.

| Step | Point | What happens | Held state | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| V1.1 | SP0 | `load_aggregate` reads the committed path. Parsing fails, or a required key is absent (HF-07a line 56). | `C` unusable, preserved untouched | FC-13 |
| V1.2 | SP0 | The directory is listed. One candidate is present. AC-3 holds: the lifecycle facts of the most recently authoritative record cannot be read, so no other artifact can be shown not to be older than it. | every artifact preserved | AC-3 |
| V1.3 | SP0 | The decision is refusal. Status `corrupt`, `playable` false, `RecoveryDiagnostic` names both artifacts. | `L` installs no run | `MC-AMBIGUOUS` |
| V1.4 | SP0 | The player may inspect, and may start a **new** run only if that writes no byte over either preserved artifact. | both artifacts intact | `RECOVERY_BLOCKED` |

**False success:** closed at V1.3. A corrupt record is never reported as loaded and never defaulted into an empty-but-valid profile, which would look like a successful first launch. HF-07a line 56 makes an absent required key invalid data rather than a default, so there is no path where a partially readable record becomes a plausible-looking one. **Silent data loss:** closed at V1.2 and V1.4. No repair, no deletion, no overwrite, and no rollback to a living generation, which is the specific loss TADR-004 line 40 names. The residual cost is honest and visible: the player cannot continue until a repair rule exists, and no such rule is written here.
