## Contract

### What this section fixes

Three states, one direction of derivation and two interfaces. The states are acquired, equipped and effective, and no rule below merges or reorders them. The direction is that the effective set is rebuilt from the equipped selections over the acquired instances and is never edited in place, so a removed selection cannot leave a residue and a repeated selection cannot add a second copy. The two interfaces are compatibility, which decides whether an effect a selection declares may reach a player, device or companion action at all, and invalidation, which decides what happens when a selection, an instance or a definition stops being usable. Everything a product answer owns stays a parameter row with a question ID.

This section owns no engine behavior, no content and no numbers. TH-04 owns what an installed capability set means for an action. TH-05 owns when a growth-bearing rebuild is installed. TH-01 owns where the intermission sits in the loop. HF-05 owns definition identity and admission. HF-07a and HF-07b own the durable fields and the commit boundary. Devices and companions belong to TH-10 and this section defines only the interfaces their effects consume.

### The three states

`Acquired`, `equipped` and `effective` are three different things with three different owners and three different lifetimes. The table is the complete state vocabulary of this contract.

| ID | State | What holds it | Durable trace | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ES-1 | Definition only. The item exists as an admitted definition at a gameplay content revision; no run has an instance of it. | ContentAdmission's `CatalogueRegistry`, reached through a `CatalogueHandle` | None. A definition is never serialised | HF-03a line 48; HF-05 lines 24, 27 |
| ES-2 | Acquired. The run holds one owned instance with a stable within-run `instance_id` and an `item_id` naming its definition. The instance may be equipped or set aside. | Session's `RunState` inventory | `run.checkpoint.equipment.owned[]` with `instance_id`, `item_id` and `instance_fields`, once its settlement commits | BIBLE lines 460, 548; HF-03a line 45; HF-07a lines 132 to 135 |
| ES-3 | Equipped. One equipped entry names a `mount_id` and the `instance_id` it holds. The entry is a selection; it is not an effect. | Session's `RunState` equipment selection | `run.checkpoint.equipment.equipped[]` with `mount_id` and `instance_id` | BIBLE line 548; HF-07a lines 136 to 138 |
| ES-4 | Contributing. The rebuilt effective set carries at least one row whose provenance is that equipped entry, because at least one compatibility query admitted one of its declared effects. | Session's `EffectiveBuild` | None. No derived effect, compatibility outcome or rebuilt stat is stored | BIBLE line 552; HF-03a line 45; HF-05 line 56; HF-07a line 135 |
| ES-5 | Installed. The rebuilt effective set has crossed D01 as values and is the capability set the encounter evaluates. | Encounter's own records, initialised from values | None | HF-03a lines 32, 46; TH-04 lines 55, 57 |
| ES-6 | Held. A requested change, a restore or a migration is refused and held with a diagnostic, and no partial effective set is installed. | Session's `SessionControl` and `PendingTransition`, with the diagnostic published for display | None. A held operation writes nothing durable | HF-05 lines 101 to 119; HF-07b line 42; HF-04a line 81 |

ES-3 and ES-4 are deliberately separate. An instance can be equipped and contribute nothing, because line 552 of the Bible states that every effect does not automatically apply to every source. An instance cannot contribute without being equipped, because the rebuild enumerates selections.

A tree selection is a selection in exactly the same sense and enters the same rebuild. The slot's `unlocked` fact, its `tree_id` and its connected unlocked prefix of node keys are the durable selection facts (HF-07a lines 128 to 130), the selection stays a connected unlocked prefix (HF-05 line 54), and `EffectiveBuild` is rebuilt from current equipped selections and selected tree paths together (HF-05 line 56). Reconfiguring equipment changes the equipped list and never a tree selection (HF-07a line 136; BIBLE line 465), and a tree change needs its own authorized opportunity (BIBLE line 542; TADR-002 line 32).

### One direction of derivation

| ID | Rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EQ-01 | The derivation runs acquired to equipped to effective and never the other way. An equipped entry may reference an owned instance. A rebuilt effective row may reference the equipped entry it came from. Nothing in the effective set references, creates, edits or removes an equipped entry or an owned instance. | BIBLE lines 548, 552; HF-03a lines 45, 57; HF-05 line 56 |
| EQ-02 | The rebuild is whole-value replacement. Session constructs a fresh `EffectiveBuild` value and replaces the previous value entire. There is no add-effect operation, no remove-effect operation and no in-place edit of a live effective set, so nothing survives a rebuild by not being visited. | TADR-002 line 32; HF-03a line 57; BIBLE line 552 |

### Owned data

This contract reads and writes exactly the records the state table assigns, through the interfaces above, and nothing else.

1. **Session writes `SessionControl`, `RunState`, `ProfileState`, `EffectiveBuild` and `PendingTransition`.** The equipment controller is a composed controller inside Session's boundary, not another mutation owner (HF-03a line 21). `RunState` holds the owned instances, the equipped entries and the build-slot selections; `EffectiveBuild` holds the rebuilt derived effect and stat set; `ProfileState` holds the discovery knowledge a recipe bonus is recognised from. Session settles each accepted result once, rebuilds from all current selections and installs the result as values (HF-03a lines 45, 57). It never writes a durable record.
2. **Encounter writes `EncounterState`, `TickState`, `AttackLedger`, `EncounterRandomState` and `EncounterInbox`.** It receives the installed capability set, the definitions and a seed as values, evaluates actions against them and holds no writable `RunState` alias (HF-03a line 46). It never edits the effective set, never equips or unequips, and never resolves a definition ID (HF-03b line 68).
3. **ContentAdmission writes `CatalogueAdmissionState` and `CatalogueRegistry`.** The run's pin selects a revision without modifying the catalogue, and equipping never edits an admitted definition. An equip changes a selection and the rebuilt set, never a `DefinitionView` (HF-03a line 48; HF-05 lines 35 to 37).
4. **Persistence writes `SaveTransactionState`, `DurableAggregate`, `PreferencesRecord` and `RecoveryState`, and it alone stages or replaces them** (HF-03a line 49). Session supplies the accepted inputs on D04; Persistence builds, validates, commits and acknowledges the candidate (HF-07b lines 37 to 40). HF-10 required fix RF-6 at line 64 records the one admitted scenario step that reads otherwise; this section states the ownership HF-03a fixes. A preferences write is not a gameplay commit and no equipment fact travels through `PreferencesRecord` (HF-07a line 205).
5. **CollisionQueries writes `CollisionWorldState` and `QueryBatchState`.** It resolves the installed extent against the physical world and returns movement and contact results. It commits no pose, equips nothing and evaluates no compatibility (HF-03a lines 33, 47).
6. **InputAdapter writes `PhysicalInputState` and `InputContextState`.** Clearing physical input cannot mutate a selection, a prepared action, a pause state, a save or the run, and an input callback only records a request (HF-03a line 50; HF-04a line 46).
7. **Presentation writes `PresentationState` and `FeedbackState` from published values.** The equipment dashboard, the effect and compatibility explanation, the removal preview and every cue are display state built from detached copies. Presentation performs no physics query, chooses no target, marks nothing equipped and rerolls nothing, and no dashboard state is stored (HF-03a lines 37, 51; HF-03b line 79; HF-07a line 128).

The one derived record is `EffectiveBuild` and it has one writer. A second copy of a derived effect anywhere else is a second authority and a defect, whatever it is called (HF-03a line 58).

### Seams stated and not decided

**Seam 1: devices and companions, to TH-10.** This section fixes the compatibility query's three target classes, the default-deny outcome, and the rule that a device or companion effect row is produced and invalidated by the same rebuild as a player effect row. It fixes no ownership, no count, no lifetime, no recall rule and no cross-level persistence, all of which are TH-10's with Q-P15 open. TH-10 can specify every one of them without adding an interface, a record or an event here, because the rows it needs already exist and are already invalidated.

**Seam 2: encounters and what an encounter awards, to TH-06.** What places an item in an encounter, what a rare enemy, event, secret room or boss awards, and how a reward becomes an accepted `EncounterResult` are encounter content. TH-06 is being written concurrently, is not admitted at this base, and is not cited here. This section states only the interface at the seam, which is that an award reaches inventory as a settlement Session accepts once at RL3 and which adds an owned instance and equips nothing.

**Seam 3: the authored schema, to SC-03 through HF-06a row N-025.** Item, shared-modifier and transformation definitions declaring effects and compatibility are `schema` work whose producing card is SC-03 and whose open content is Q-P14. This section names no field of those definitions. What it requires of them is that each has a domain ID, that its declared compatibility is a set of references validated for existence and expected family, and that it declares no derived effect and accumulates no per-run field.

**Seam 4: loot and fixed secret evaluation, to TH-09.** Which eligible endpoint combinations grant which fixed bonuses, and how a discovery is disclosed, belong to TH-09 and Q-P14. This section fixes only that a fixed secret bonus is a compatibility outcome over selected endpoints, that it is rebuilt rather than stored, and that re-equipping or reloading cannot stack another copy.
