## Contract

### What this section fixes

Three kinds of fact, three owners, three lifetimes, and one rule about randomness that separates them. A themed drop is encounter content: it is random once, at the settlement that produced it, and its outcome is then preserved rather than recomputed. A recipe is deterministic authored content: it is never random, it is evaluated by TH-08's rebuild against the current selection set, and it is never stored. A discovery is permanent profile knowledge: it is a domain ID in a set that only grows, it carries no effect of its own, and it commits at the next durable transition. Every defect this subject is prone to — a reload that rerolls a drop, a re-equip that stacks a second bonus, a discovery that grants power directly or disappears on a respec — is excluded by the direction one of those three facts moves, not by a guard an implementation could forget.

This section owns no content, no engine behavior and no numbers. TH-06 owns what an encounter awards. TH-08 owns the inventory, the equipped selection and the rebuild. TH-11 owns routing and the position a discovery is made in. TH-05 owns growth and the boundary a rebuild installs at. TH-01 owns where the intermission sits. HF-05 owns definition identity and admission. HF-07a and HF-07b own the durable fields and the commit boundaries. SC-03 and SC-08 own the definitions, under Q-P14 and Q-P37.

Counts for a reviewer: three fact kinds, nine acquisition rules, eleven recipe rules, ten discovery rules, twenty-eight interface rows, seven owned-data owners, thirteen ordering rules, seventeen edge cases, fifteen failure modes and thirty-one parameter rows, of which sixteen carry a source line and fifteen are `unset` with a question ID.

The separation is a separation of randomness and of direction, stated once here.

- LK-1 is random at most once, inside the tick that committed it, from the encounter's own `EncounterRandomState` at P4 and P5. After the settlement commits, the outcome is a stored fact restored by value. A reload reads `resolved_ids`; it never re-enters the draw.
- LK-2 contains no randomness at all. BIBLE line 562 places the surprise in discovering the recipe, not in rerolling its reward, and HF-05 line 58 requires the same effect for the same combination. A reload therefore does the opposite of LK-1: it stores nothing and recomputes everything, and it arrives at the same value because the function is total over the selection set (TH-08 EQ-04 at line 59).
- LK-3 is monotone. It is a set that only grows, within a run and across a run boundary (HF-07b N7 at line 134). It never carries a value, a counter or an effect, so it cannot stack and cannot be rerolled.

A single event can produce one, two or all three facts. Defeating a rare enemy may settle a drop (LK-1), that drop's presence may make a recipe eligible at the next rebuild (LK-2), and Q-P37's answer may make the resulting relationship a discovery (LK-3). Each of the three still travels its own route, commits at its own boundary and is invalidated by its own rule. This section adds no combined operation, because a combined operation would be a fourth kind of fact with no owner.

### Owned data

This contract reads and writes exactly the records the fact table assigns, through the interfaces above, and nothing else.

1. **Session writes `SessionControl`, `RunState`, `ProfileState`, `EffectiveBuild` and `PendingTransition`.** `RunState` holds the owned instances, the equipped entries, the build-slot selections, the resolved rewards and the offered choices; `ProfileState` holds the discovery knowledge and permanent option unlocks; `EffectiveBuild` holds the rebuilt rows, including every recipe row. The loot and recipe controller is a composed controller inside Session's boundary, not another mutation owner (HF-03a line 21). Session settles each accepted result once, rebuilds from all current selections and installs the result as values (HF-03a line 57). It never writes a durable record.
2. **Encounter writes `EncounterState`, `TickState`, `AttackLedger`, `EncounterRandomState` and `EncounterInbox`.** It resolves an award's random outcome once at P4 and P5, determines the reward result values and publishes them at P6. It holds no writable `RunState` or `ProfileState` alias, settles nothing, learns nothing, and never resolves a definition ID (HF-03a line 46; HF-03b line 68).
3. **ContentAdmission writes `CatalogueAdmissionState` and `CatalogueRegistry`.** Item, recipe, discovery and unlock definitions are admitted, immutable and never edited by a run. The run's pin selects a revision without modifying the catalogue, and a discovery never writes back into a definition (HF-03a line 48; HF-05 lines 31, 35 to 37).
4. **Persistence writes `SaveTransactionState`, `DurableAggregate`, `PreferencesRecord` and `RecoveryState`, and it alone stages or replaces them** (HF-03a line 49). Session supplies the accepted inputs on D04; Persistence builds, validates, commits and acknowledges the candidate (HF-07b lines 37 to 40). No loot, recipe or discovery fact travels through `PreferencesRecord`.
5. **CollisionQueries writes `CollisionWorldState` and `QueryBatchState`.** It resolves movement and interprets contacts on Encounter's ordered request and returns results. It commits no pose, awards nothing, evaluates no eligibility and reads no profile (HF-03a lines 33, 47, 61).
6. **InputAdapter writes `PhysicalInputState` and `InputContextState`.** Clearing physical input cannot mutate a selection, a settlement, a profile entry, a prepared action, a pause state, a save or the run, and an input callback only records a request (HF-03a line 50; HF-04a line 46).
7. **Presentation writes `PresentationState` and `FeedbackState` from published values.** The discovery dashboard, the acquired-item display, the bonus explanation and every cue are display state built from detached copies. Presentation performs no physics query, settles no reward, marks no discovery learned and rerolls no loot, offer or miscast, and no dashboard state is stored (HF-03a lines 37, 51; HF-03b line 79; HF-07a CSV row 24).

The one derived record is `EffectiveBuild` and it has one writer. A second copy of a bonus anywhere else is a second authority and a defect, whatever it is called (HF-03a line 58).

### Algorithms and update order

One intermission that changes a bonus runs in this order. O1 releases held physical input and sets the dashboard's input context, O2 cancels uncommitted preparation and O3 holds ticking, all after the completion transition's identity is in `A`. An admitted `request_equipment_change` or an admitted `request_respec` replaces the selection in `RunState`; Session resolves the affected definitions on D03, rebuilds `EffectiveBuild` whole in TH-08's three passes with every recipe's eligibility evaluated in pass two, and publishes the new snapshot on D06. An unauthorized respec produces a scoped rejection shown on D06 and changes no selection, so no bonus moves. Where Q-P37's answer makes something observed in that intermission a discovery, Session writes the ID into `ProfileState` in the same drain and the entry waits for the next durable transition. When the player commits to an onward exit, the outgoing encounter is retired, the next level's definitions are resolved, the fresh encounter receives the rebuilt set as values, and O4 restores play. The arrays and sets the intermission changed commit with the next durable transition that carries them.

### Seams stated and not decided

**Seam 1: what an encounter awards, to TH-06.** What places an item in an encounter, which rare enemy, event, secret room or boss carries one and under what objective are TH-06's and the content cards'. Rechecked at the Handoff-5 closure integrated read (TRACKING EV-0325) against the admitted bytes with no change needed. **Seam 4: the authored definitions, to SC-03 and SC-08.** Item and recipe definitions are SC-03's under Q-P14; discovery and permanent unlock definitions are SC-08's under Q-P37 and Q-P14.
