## Contract

### What this section fixes

Four things and no fifth. **Who acts**: a device and the companion are composed controllers inside Encounter's boundary, and no callback of theirs is a second gameplay-time authority. **What reaches them**: a compatible effect reaches a device or companion action only as a row of the installed capability set, produced and invalidated by TH-08's one rebuild, and nothing here adds a second derived set. **What the companion is**: exactly one main assembled companion per run, whose assembly is `RunState` and whose durable trace is three fields, and whose rescue is a recoverable moment that cannot become a standard revival because no field can express one. **Which identity is which**: a chassis-type epithet and an assembled-instance identity are different things, and the rule that names each is Q-P29's.

Everything a product answer owns stays a parameter row with a question ID. No cap, count, lifetime, recall rule, resource rule, part list, component model, chassis roster, command roster, persistence answer or naming rule appears anywhere below.

This section owns no engine behaviour, no content and no numbers. TH-03 owns what one committed attack is. TH-04 owns what an action and an effect window are. TH-05 owns when a growth-bearing rebuild is installed. TH-06 owns the schedule and its admission. TH-08 owns compatibility and invalidation. TH-01 owns the loop. HF-05 owns definition identity and admission. HF-07a and HF-07b own the durable fields and the commit boundary.

### Owned data

This contract reads and writes exactly the records the vocabulary table assigns, through the interfaces above, and nothing else.

1. **Encounter writes `EncounterState`, `TickState`, `AttackLedger`, `EncounterRandomState` and `EncounterInbox`.** The active device state, the acting companion's action state and every open support window are `EncounterState`'s, and Encounter is their sole writer. A support controller is a composed controller inside that boundary; it holds no writable `RunState` alias and resolves no definition ID (HF-03a lines 21, 46; HF-03b line 92).
2. **Session writes `SessionControl`, `RunState`, `ProfileState`, `EffectiveBuild` and `PendingTransition`.** The companion assembly and whatever device facts carried over are `RunState`'s; the rows a support's effects come from are `EffectiveBuild`'s. Session settles each accepted result once, rebuilds from all current selections and installs the result as values, and it never writes a durable record (HF-03a lines 45, 49, 57).
3. **ContentAdmission writes `CatalogueAdmissionState` and `CatalogueRegistry`.** Device, chassis, component and command-capability definitions are immutable admitted values; assembling a companion changes a selection, never a `DefinitionView`, and no per-run field accumulates on the admitted path (HF-03a line 48; HF-05 lines 35 to 37).
4. **Persistence writes `SaveTransactionState`, `DurableAggregate`, `PreferencesRecord` and `RecoveryState`, and it alone stages or replaces them** (HF-03a line 49). Session supplies the accepted inputs on D04; Persistence stages, validates, commits and acknowledges the candidate carrying `run.checkpoint.companion` and `run.checkpoint.device_carry_over` (HF-07b lines 37 to 40, 76). No support fact travels through `PreferencesRecord`.
5. **CollisionQueries writes `CollisionWorldState` and `QueryBatchState`.** It resolves a deployed device's placement and the companion's movement against the physical world and returns the results; it commits no pose, deploys nothing and evaluates no compatibility (HF-03a lines 33, 47).
6. **InputAdapter writes `PhysicalInputState` and `InputContextState`.** Clearing physical input cannot deploy, recall, cancel a committed support action or mutate an assembly; an input callback only records a request (HF-03a lines 36, 50; HF-04a line 46).
7. **Presentation writes `PresentationState` and `FeedbackState` from published values.** The companion readout, the device markers, the rescue flourish and every support cue are display state built from detached copies. Presentation performs no physics query, chooses no target, marks nothing deployed and stores no dashboard state (HF-03a lines 37, 51; HF-03b line 79; TH-08 line 192).

The derived record remains the one `EffectiveBuild`, with one writer. A second derived record kept for devices or companions is a second authority and a defect, whatever it is called (HF-03a line 58; TH-08 line 255).

### Seams stated and not decided

**Seam 1: compatibility and invalidation, from TH-08.** This section consumes EC-01 to EC-10 and EN-01 to EN-09 as written and adds no interface, record or event there. It uses the three target classes, the default-deny outcome and EN-06's rule that a support effect row is produced and invalidated by the same rebuild. If an answer to Q-P15 ever required a support to hold a selection of its own in the rebuild, that would change TH-08's EQ-03 inputs and is a finding against TH-08 rather than a local exception here.

**Seam 2: the schedule and its admission, to TH-06 and Q-E10.** Which commitments the encounter lets exist at once, including a support's, is TH-06's responsibility and Q-E10's value. This section fixes only that a support's commitment is subject to that policy at the phase TH-06 names and that a refusal costs nothing.

**Seam 3: the authored schema, to SC-06 through HF-06a rows F-06, F-07 and N-027.** Device definitions and companion chassis, component and command-capability definitions are `schema` work whose producing card is SC-06 and whose open content is Q-P15. This section names no field of those definitions. What it requires of them is that each has a domain ID, that its declared compatibility is a set of references validated for existence and expected family, that it declares no derived effect and that it accumulates no per-run field.

**Seam 4: appearance and naming, to Q-P29 and the art lane.** The chassis personality, module silhouettes, expression conventions and the selected naming rule are Andrew's. This section fixes only that a chassis-type epithet and an assembled-instance identity are distinct, that the domain ID is the durable one and that no envelope field carries a name.

**Seam 5: the durable surface, to HF-07a and HF-07b.** Whether `run.checkpoint.device_carry_over` is ever non-empty, and what keys its `counters` map holds, are Q-P15's answer expressed in HF-07a's fields. An answer that needed a field those two sections do not carry is an HF-07a and HF-07b revision, admitted before any implementation, and never an implementation choice.

**Seam 6: what an encounter awards and what a level asks, to TH-06, TH-09 and Q-P38.** What places a device definition or a companion part in a run, and whether a level's objective names a device, is encounter and loot content. This section states only that an award reaches the run as a settlement Session accepts once and that a protected device is still an Encounter-owned support.
