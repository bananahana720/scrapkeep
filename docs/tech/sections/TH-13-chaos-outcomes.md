## Contract

### What this section fixes

It fixes the five-step internal order of one commitment's P4 resolution and where the draw sits in it; seven draw rules; eight geometry-relation rules and a nine-part table of what a miscast may and may not select; six publication rules that let Presentation show a harmful outcome without a draw; seven rules for a harmful outcome reaching the pilot and its recovery; seven generator-identity and durability rules naming exactly two durable field groups; six reproduction rules for redelivery, replay, overload, restore and revival; twenty-eight interface rows, each naming one HF-03a command, published result or owned record on one HF-03b edge; seven owned-data rules; fourteen ordering rules; twelve edge cases; eight failure modes; and twenty-seven tunable parameters, each carrying either a source line or a question ID. It fixes no number that no cited source states.

It is not a runtime. No outcome named here has been drawn, no miscast named here has been applied, no telegraph named here has been drawn, and no engine, browser or build ran for it.

### Where this section sits

TH-03 CH-01 to CH-06 at its lines 181 to 186 already fix that a chaos cast is an ordinary committed attack resolved once at P4, that its result — intended or miscast — is the single `outcome` drawn by `EncounterRandomState` inside that P4, that the miscast shares the same descriptor with damage at P5 including harm to the pilot and with the presentation records on D06, that once-only survives redelivery, that no cosmetic layer rerolls it, and that most casts do what was intended with no instability meter. TH-04 AO-11 at its line 204 says the same for an ability's random outcome, and TH-04 AV-06 at its line 69 fixes the prepared state whose provisional descriptor is published as `AimPreview`.

Those rules say the draw happens once at P4. They do not say where inside P4, what the draw may and may not decide, what the published value must carry, how the caster is reached, which generator states exist, or what a restore reproduces. **This section specifies exactly those six things and adds no seventh.** Every rule below is a consequence of an admitted clause; where the consequence bounds an answer Q-P13 has not yet given, the bound is stated as a gate on that answer and never as the answer.

### The draw and its position inside P4

HF-04a line 65 orders P4 as *revalidate, then resolve geometry, targets and random outcome once*, and TADR-002 line 30 enumerates the resolution's products in the same order. Two admitted refusals make that order binding rather than descriptive: TH-03 FM-02 at its line 213 states that a diagnosed `query_attack` at commitment yields **no random draw**, and TH-04 AA-07 at its line 58 states that a rejected request **consumes no random draw**. A draw that preceded revalidation or preceded the query would violate one of them.

One commitment's P4 therefore runs five steps in this order, all inside one C-TICK invocation of the one active coordinator.

| Step | What happens | Why it is here | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

### How the previewed, the resolved and the harmful geometry relate

Three geometries are in play and only two descriptors exist: the provisional descriptor `AimPreview` publishes, and the committed descriptor the commitment resolves. **A harmful outcome is not a third descriptor.** It is a value of `outcome` inside the committed one.

### Owned data

A chaos cast reads and writes exactly the records below, through the interfaces above, and nothing else.

1. **Encounter writes `EncounterRandomState`, and no other module writes or reads it.** It holds the encounter's mutable generator position, it is consumed exactly once per commitment at R4, and it ends at `retire` with no durable projection (HF-03a line 46; TH-01 line 60). Encounter also writes `AttackLedger` for the commitment, its resolved geometry, its targets, its drawn outcome and its applied-result identity; `EncounterState` for the committed poses, health, resources, cooldowns, effect windows and prepared actions a miscast changes; `TickState` for the phase position; and `EncounterInbox` for submitted commands. It holds no writable `RunState` alias and resolves no definition ID itself (HF-03a line 46; HF-03b line 92).
2. **CollisionQueries writes `CollisionWorldState` and `QueryBatchState`.** It answers `resolve_movement`, `query_attack` and `interpret_contacts` on Encounter's ordered request and publishes no damage, reward or progression mutation. It holds no generator and decides no outcome; Encounter alone commits the authoritative gameplay pose (HF-03a lines 33, 47).
3. **Session writes `SessionControl`, `RunState`, `ProfileState`, `EffectiveBuild` and `PendingTransition`.** The run-level generator position lives in `RunState` and only Session advances it; Session settles an accepted encounter result once and stages the revival or terminal transition a lethal miscast opens. No individual cast settles anything in the run (HF-03a lines 45, 57; TH-03 line 143).
4. **Presentation writes `PresentationState` and `FeedbackState` from published values.** It holds detached copies, consumes cues without marking anything applied, rerolls no miscast, chooses no target and writes back no transform (HF-03a lines 37, 51; HF-03b line 79).
5. **InputAdapter writes `PhysicalInputState` and `InputContextState`.** An input callback submits a request; no cast, draw, damage, cooldown or encounter progress follows directly from it, and clearing physical input cannot mutate a prepared action (HF-03a lines 36, 50; HF-04a line 46).
6. **ContentAdmission writes `CatalogueAdmissionState` and `CatalogueRegistry`.** A chaos kit's element, effect, attack and device definitions are immutable admitted values delivered through Session with no accumulating per-run field, and admission blocks rather than inventing an unknown required rule (HF-03a line 48; HF-05 lines 35 to 37, 51).
7. **Persistence writes `SaveTransactionState`, `DurableAggregate`, `PreferencesRecord` and `RecoveryState`, and writes nothing that expresses a chaos outcome.** No field in either HF-07a copy carries an element, a miscast, a severity, a warning state or an encounter generator position; the only generator rows are the two `rng_state` groups, which belong to the run and not to any commitment (HF-07a lines 70, 115 to 117, 156 to 158, 192).

### Seams stated and not decided

**Seam 1: the content, to Q-P13.** This section fixes the draw, its position, its reach over the descriptor, its publication, its application and its durability. It fixes no element, no effect, no miscastable action, no frequency, no severity and no warning rule, and it adopts no instability meter and no always-random kit. One bound on the answer is recorded and not taken: an answer that made the danger shape depend on the drawn outcome would require a query that consumes the draw, which the admitted refusal rules forbid, so that answer is a contract revision rather than tuning (XG-06, XT-15).

**Seam 2: the stream separation and the repeatability limits, to TH-14.** Whether route, layout, loot and miscast draw from separate streams, what the `stream_id` vocabulary is, and what repeatability holds across engine, OS and timing are [TH-14](../../../BACKLOG.md#th-14)'s, which is queued and unwritten at this base. This section fixes only that the encounter draws from `EncounterRandomState`, that the encounter never reads or advances a run-level stream, that the only durable generator fields are the two `rng_state` groups, and that no run-level stored outcome may become recomputable (XS-04 to XS-06).

**Seam 3: the run's consequences, to TH-01 and HF-07b.** A lethal or otherwise consequential outcome settles nothing inside the encounter. RL7's revival hold, RL8's terminal settlement and HF-07b's Transitions C and D own everything after the publication, and this section names them without performing them (XH-05, XI-27).

**Seam 4: the concurrent cards.** [TH-07](../../../BACKLOG.md#th-07) and [TH-09](../../../BACKLOG.md#th-09) were written in other worktrees and admitted later (TRACKING EV-0315, EV-0324); no byte of either is cited. A progression or respec change reaches a chaos cast only as a rebuilt capability set installed at Session's authorized boundary, and a commitment already made keeps its descriptor and its outcome across that install (XE-09). A loot drop is a run-level fact settled once from an accepted encounter result, a fixed-recipe result is a rebuild output that is never settled or committed, and neither is a second draw inside a commitment; if a recipe or attunement bonus changes what an outcome applies, it arrives as values in the installed build and changes no rule above (XA-11, XE-09).
