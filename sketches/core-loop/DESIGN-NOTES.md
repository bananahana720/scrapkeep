# Core-loop design notes

These are the design calls agents made while building this prototype, each with the principle it serves. The principles are Andrew's, from [playtest entry 6](../../docs/playtests/PLAYTEST-LOG.md#6-mobility-kit-v2-first-human-play-and-design-principles). The coordinator brings these to Andrew and asks him only when a call would change the direction of the deliverable.

Each writer appends only inside its own section, so the four module branches merge cleanly.

## Spine (wave 1)

1. **The beacon charges as you roll into its ring.** No key starts it, and you never have to stop. Circling inside the ring charges it. Leaving pauses the charge and keeps what you have. Serves momentum is sacred (1).
2. **Reward gates are arches with no collider.** They stand 38 m ahead on the ball's line of travel, 18 m apart. Rolling through one takes its part. If you roll past both, the stub puts them ahead of you again instead of waiting behind you. Serves 1 ("reward choices are gates you roll through, not a menu"). The run test measures speed through a gate: 20.93 m/s in, 21.11 m/s after.
3. **The launch keeps horizontal speed exactly.** Flow turns that speed onto the next stage's entry heading and keeps the vertical part. The world stub adds 20 m/s of lift and never launches slower than cruise, because raising a slow ball is a gift, not a cost. Serves 1 ("the stage exit is a launch into the next stage"). The run test measured 20.99 m/s before the launch and 20.98 m/s after.
4. **The next stage is entered from 14 m above its spawn,** so a launch lands rather than pops in. Serves 1 and the snappy, fluid half of 8.
5. **Stage 1 drops you in already rolling,** at 80% of cruise down the entry heading. Serves quick to start (2) and 1.
6. **A retry restores the entry velocity as well as the stage-start state:** scrap, HP, parts, kills and position. You re-enter the stage the way the launch delivered you. It is one synchronous call, measured at about 1 ms. Serves 2 and 1.
7. **The death screen takes one key.** R retries the stage from its start. N starts a new run as the same class. C goes to class select. In play, holding R for 0.5 s retries the stage. The hold stops a stray tap from throwing away a good run. Serves 2.
8. **Falling through the geometry costs nothing.** The rescue puts you back on the ground with your full horizontal speed and no damage, because the map failed, not the player. Serves 1 ("only the player's own performance costs momentum").
9. **The pylon banks the speed you plant with.** Space slingshots you off along the face toward where you look. The eject speed is the banked speed times 0.85, plus 6 m/s, and never less than a plain jump. E drops off in place, a stop the player chose. Both numbers are F1 sliders (`Slingshot keep`, `Slingshot boost`). Serves 8 ("the pylon keeps momentum"). The movement test measured a plant at 11.89 m/s and an eject at 16.10 m/s, against a 13 m/s jump.
10. **The camera moves to the shoulder while mounted.** The arm shortens to 4.2 m and the ball shifts left of the crosshair. The camera owns the hook, and combat decides when to use it. Serves entry 6 ("snap to a more shoulder view") and 5.
11. **Both classes can fire while rolling.** v2 allowed the zap only while mounted. The runner-gunner runs and guns, so the stub weapon fires any time for both classes. Serves 5.
12. **Collisions have weight on both sides of the seam.** The ball applies knockback itself, so a ram always shoves. Combat turns the same contact into damage once, through `strike`. Serves 1 ("collisions have weight") and TADR-008 (resolve once, publish the result).
13. **Run state changes only through mutators, and each mutator publishes its own fact.** The HUD, the summary and audio cannot disagree with the numbers. Serves fast without debt (7) and TADR-008.
14. **Every fact has a shape as well as a colour** in `event_kinds.gd`, so colour never carries meaning alone. The objective line, the HUD toasts and the field-notes legend all read their wording from the same row. Serves form mirrors function.
15. **Hit-stop is a spine API.** It is capped at 60 ms, never stacks, and runs only in play, so juice cannot become an obstruction. Serves 8 ("as weight and not as obstruction") and 1.
16. **Parts change tuning through multipliers, not by moving the sliders.** The recompute from the part list is idempotent, so a retry restores the build exactly, and F1 still shows the base values. Serves 7.
17. **Rigid physics only.** The kinematic body and the F2 toggle are gone. Serves entry 6 and TADR-006.
18. **The terrain belongs to world (D) behind a frozen API.** For now all three stages share the Spillway map, read three ways: stage 1 runs the top shelf and its chute to a mid-shelf beacon, stage 2 drops into the basin the flood fills, and stage 3 takes the bypass deck to the basin floor. Serves 3 (the flood is a layer on stage 2's beacon), and D can replace the stages.
19. **The flood is announced before it rises.** Its klaxon runs for 5 s before the water moves (`event_announced`, then `event_started`). Serves 1 ("nothing the game does may stop or inhibit the player in a way they didn't see coming") and TADR-010.

Open question for review: the flood's drag in the basin slows a ball that stays in the water. The flood is announced and visible and the ball can leave it, but the reviewer should decide whether drag near a beacon counts as an unforeseen momentum blocker.

## Events and stages (writer D)

1. **Three stages, each its own map, built once at setup.** Stage 1 keeps the wave-1 Spillway shape. Stage 2, the Sluice, and stage 3, the Weir, are new. The idle stages stay in the physics world, hidden and parked 5 km down, because re-adding a trimesh cost about 34 ms a swap and parking costs microseconds. The whole game is ready in about 1 s (measured 0.92 to 1.15 s headless). Serves 1 (no hitch in the launch).
2. **Height queries read the built grid and split each cell the way the collider does.** What the spine, combat and build ask about the ground is the ground the ball rolls on. Serves 7 and TADR-008 (one committed geometry).
3. **The momentum kit is a vocabulary of pure height shapes:** landing hills, banked berms, kickers, sluice chutes, a halfpipe weir, a relay mesa, flood bowls and dry causeways, plus grapple posts and pylon walls as props. The Sluice lands you on a hill, puts a kicker on the straight line between two berms, and drops two chutes into a bowl. The Weir lands you running east, cuts a halfpipe south through the middle, and puts the relay on a mesa with a berm round its foot. Serves 1.
4. **Chutes shallow out at their exits and stay open.** A carved channel that ends in a quarter-pipe wall would turn your speed straight up. Serves 1.
5. **The ground says what it is for.** Any face steep enough for a pylon to bite (over 30 degrees) is tinted verdigris concrete. Kickers, berms and landing hills are tinted amber, the movement colour. Grapple posts are copper with an amber cap, and pylon walls are verdigris stripes. Serves 5 and form mirrors function.
6. **The launch is a cannon rail.** Taking a gate fires the ball at 24 m/s of lift, and the rail holds its velocity to the arc for the whole flight, so nothing it clips on the way out can cost it speed. Rings are drawn on the exact arc it will fly. The launch test measured 21.93 m/s before the gate and 21.92 m/s through the flight and at the next stage's entry. Serves 1 ("the stage exit is a launch into the next stage").
7. **The next stage marks the landing and catches it on a hill.** Amber rings trace the fall onto a landing pad, and the ground falls away along the entry heading so the fall becomes speed. The test measured touchdown at 20.83 m/s, 28.36 m/s half a second later, and the pad 0.9 m from where the ball came down. Serves 1 and 8 (a drop from above that feels like a badass landing).
8. **The event director is pure, stepped and seeded (TADR-010).** A retry reuses the stage's seed, so it replays the same event plan and the same miniboss spots. A new entry draws a fresh plan from the run's stream. Serves 2 (a retry is the same stage, learnable and fair).
9. **Opt-in events are announced, then armed, then yours to take.** Each one is announced 4 s before its ring arms. A 70 m beam in the event colour stands over the ring, and a sign says "roll in: milestone reward". Rolling into the ring starts it. An offer you ignore waits armed and never ends or nags. A loss re-offers the event 12 s later. A win calls `run.record_milestone`, so `milestone_won` reaches build. Serves 3.
10. **No event can block the exit.** Rings, beams, gates, the surge front, the relay and the hauler have no colliders. The flow test ignores an armed Scrap Stampede and clears stage 1 through the beacon, a gate and the launch. Serves 3 ("skipping an event never blocks the exit") and 1.
11. **Surge Run: the water is the timer and the tailwind.** Rolling into the ring releases a wall of water down the route. Six gates must be taken in order before the front reaches each one. Riding just ahead of the front pushes you on at 14 m/s². One visible body carries both the threat and the help. On the Weir the gates alternate up the halfpipe walls. Serves 8 and 1.
12. **Hold the Relay charges while you are in its ring, and pauses when you leave without resetting.** Circling, ramming and turreting on one of its four walls all count. It needs 18 s inside a 60 s window. The spawn director spikes while it is live. Serves 5 (the engineer's turret spot, the runner's circle) and 1.
13. **A miniboss arrives mid-event at a seeded spot.** On the relay it comes at 35% charge, 32 to 46 m from the relay. On a stage with threat of 2 or more, which is stage 3, the Surge Run brings one at half its gates. Six seeds gave six distinct spots, and the same seed gives the same spot. Serves 3 (Andrew's "a miniboss arriving mid-surge") and TADR-010.
14. **Scrap Stampede is stage 1's own event.** A hauler bolts from the pump house down the east chute into the basin at cruise speed. Every ram knocks 8 scrap loose through `run.add_scrap`, and the hauler runs 8% faster after each hit. Three rams win it. Serves 1 (loot on the move, collisions have weight) and TADR-010 (an event unique to a map).
15. **The dust squall is seen before it is felt, and its wind never brakes.** A dust wall walks in from upwind over a 6 s lead. The haze is capped at a fog density of 0.006, so threats at combat range stay readable. The wind blows at 5 m/s². Serves 1, 4 (visual clarity is the balance rule) and 8.
16. **No event push may oppose the ball's motion.** The director drops that component from the summed squall and surge push in one place, so a headwind costs nothing and a tailwind is free speed. The events test found the worst component along the ball's motion is 0 over 36 headings. Serves 1 and 7.
17. **Every event that starts also ends.** When a stage ends under a live event, the world closes it with `passed`, so music and HUD layers never hang into the next stage. Serves 6.
18. **Pressure for the music is spawn pressure plus the events.** Each live opt-in event adds 0.25, and the squall and a rising flood add up to 0.1 each. The events' total is capped at 0.4. Serves 6 (layers rise with the chaos you chose).
19. **A combat module without a miniboss gets a chaser at the same spot.** If `spawn_enemy("miniboss", at)` returns null, the world asks for a chaser there. Serves 7.
20. **Each stage floods its own bowl on a seeded rhythm.** The stage 2 and 3 bowls sit below the ground around them, so the water plane never floats over dry land. The flood's drag is unchanged, and the spine's open question about drag near a beacon stays open for review. The Sluice's beacon ring is half in its bowl, so the dry half is always a choice. Serves 1 and TADR-010 (ebb and flow).
21. **`ctx.terrain.material_at(point)` answers "water" where the flood is, and "concrete" or "sand" elsewhere.** Build's element and material table can then look up what a hit touched without a private rule. Serves TADR-009 (one table) and 7.
