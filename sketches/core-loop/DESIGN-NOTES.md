# Core-loop design notes

These are the design calls agents made while building this prototype, each with the principle it serves. The principles are Andrew's, from [playtest entry 6](../../docs/playtests/PLAYTEST-LOG.md#6-mobility-kit-v2-first-human-play-and-design-principles). The coordinator brings these to Andrew and asks him only when a call would change the direction of the deliverable.

Each writer appends only inside its own section, so the four module branches merge cleanly.

## Spine (wave 1)

1. **Superseded by call 20 (the Warden gate, DEMO-SPEC).** This was: the beacon charges as you roll into its ring, with no key and no stop; leaving pauses the charge. It now applies only on the unsealed path (calm tests, or a stage whose spawner has no Warden). Serves momentum is sacred (1).
2. **Reward gates are arches with no collider.** They stand 38 m ahead on the ball's line of travel, 18 m apart. Rolling through one takes its part. If you roll past both, the stub puts them ahead of you again instead of waiting behind you. Serves 1 ("reward choices are gates you roll through, not a menu"). The run test measures speed through a gate: 20.93 m/s in, 21.11 m/s after.
3. **The launch keeps horizontal speed exactly.** Flow turns that speed onto the next stage's entry heading and keeps the vertical part. The world stub adds 20 m/s of lift and never launches slower than cruise, because raising a slow ball is a gift, not a cost. Serves 1 ("the stage exit is a launch into the next stage"). The run test measured 20.99 m/s before the launch and 20.98 m/s after.
4. **The next stage is entered from 14 m above its spawn,** so a launch lands rather than pops in. Serves 1 and the snappy, fluid half of 8.
5. **Stage 1 drops you in already rolling,** at 80% of cruise down the entry heading. Serves quick to start (2) and 1.
6. **A retry restores the entry velocity as well as the stage-start state:** scrap, HP, parts, kills and position. You re-enter the stage the way the launch delivered you. It is one synchronous call, measured at about 1 ms. Serves 2 and 1.
7. **The death screen takes one key.** R retries the stage from its start. N starts a new run as the same class. C goes to class select. In play, holding R for 0.5 s retries the stage. The hold stops a stray tap from throwing away a good run. Serves 2.
8. **Falling through the geometry costs nothing.** The rescue puts you back on the ground with your full horizontal speed and no damage, because the map failed, not the player. Serves 1 ("only the player's own performance costs momentum").
9. **The pylon banks the speed you plant with.** Space slingshots you off along the face toward where you look. The eject speed is the banked speed times 0.85, plus 6 m/s, and never less than a plain jump. E drops off in place, a stop the player chose. Both numbers are F1 sliders (`Slingshot keep`, `Slingshot boost`). Serves 8 ("the pylon keeps momentum"). The movement test measured a plant at 11.89 m/s and an eject at 16.10 m/s, against a 13 m/s jump.
10. **The camera moves to the shoulder while mounted.** The arm shortens to 4.2 m and the ball shifts left of the crosshair. The camera owns the hook, and combat decides when to use it. Serves entry 6 ("snap to a more shoulder view") and 5.
11. **Both classes can fire while rolling.** v2 allowed the zap only while mounted. The runner-gunner runs and guns, so the stub weapon fires any time for both classes. Serves 5.
12. **Collisions have weight on both sides of the seam.** The ball applies knockback itself, so a ram always shoves. Combat turns the same contact into damage once, through `strike`. Serves 1 ("collisions have weight") and TADR-008 (resolve once, publish the result).
13. **Run state changes only through mutators, and each mutator publishes its own fact.** The HUD, the summary and audio cannot disagree with the numbers. Serves fast without debt (7) and TADR-008.
14. **Every fact has a shape as well as a colour** in `event_kinds.gd`, so colour never carries meaning alone. The objective line, the HUD toasts and the field-notes legend all read their wording from the same row. Serves form mirrors function.
15. **Hit-stop is a spine API.** It is capped at 60 ms, never stacks, and runs only in play, so juice cannot become an obstruction. Serves 8 ("as weight and not as obstruction") and 1.
16. **Parts change tuning through multipliers, not by moving the sliders.** The recompute from the part list is idempotent, so a retry restores the build exactly, and F1 still shows the base values. Serves 7.
17. **Rigid physics only.** The kinematic body and the F2 toggle are gone. Serves entry 6 and TADR-006.
18. **Superseded by D's notes 1 and 3 (three distinct maps).** This was: the terrain belongs to world (D) behind a frozen API. For now all three stages share the Spillway map, read three ways: stage 1 runs the top shelf and its chute to a mid-shelf beacon, stage 2 drops into the basin the flood fills, and stage 3 takes the bypass deck to the basin floor. Serves 3 (the flood is a layer on stage 2's beacon), and D can replace the stages.
19. **The flood is announced before it rises.** Its klaxon runs for 5 s before the water moves (`event_announced`, then `event_started`). Serves 1 ("nothing the game does may stop or inhibit the player in a way they didn't see coming") and TADR-010.

### Correction wave (DEMO-SPEC, 2026-09-26)

Each call names the stage demand it serves and the principle it strains.

20. **The Warden seals every beacon in play.** Sitting in the ring adds nothing; the charge shown is how much of the Warden is broken; its death opens the gates wherever the ball is. Calm stages, or a spawner that returns no Warden, keep the presence path, so a missing module never locks an exit. Demand: the Warden. Serves 3 (one MUST, the rest opt-in) and strains 1: circling cannot clear a stage.
21. **The ground probe now points at the world's down.** The v2 probe turned with the spin: rolling on flat ground it read grounded on 38% of ticks, so drive, rolling resistance, air control and jump coyote all followed the spin phase, and repeat drives from rest took 191 to 266 ticks to reach 90% of cruise. Fixed, it reads grounded on every tick and every drive takes 109 ticks. This changes the approved feel (faster pickup on the ground, jumps that always fire), so F1's "Legacy spin probe (v2)" toggle brings the old probe back for comparison. Demand: every stage. Serves 1 and 2; it strains the entry-6 verdict until Andrew plays it.
22. **Heft lives in the run state and never touches the body.** Mass is separate from the wallet, so spending compacts scrap and keeps growth. A class change publishes once, and a restore changes class silently. Demand: the Warden (punch against plating). Serves 4 and holds TADR-008 line 40: heft never changes mass, radius, torque or air control, and the handling test measures it.
23. **Crush pass-through.** From the cruise tier up, a bow sensor a metre ahead asks combat whether the hit would kill. On yes, the ball ignores that enemy while it dies: no knock, no shockwave, no hit-stop. At heft 3 the ball kept 18.21 of 18.57 m/s. Demand: the fall line and events (fodder). Serves 1 and 4 (the Katamari moment).
24. **Bonus cores are stage-scoped and shown as two pips beside the objective,** one per gate tier they can buy, with a count past two. Demand: events and vaults. Serves 3 (opt-in layers pay visibly) and form mirrors function.
25. **The objective line names the fight: punch against plating, plus the stage hint.** Without a spawner reading, it shows the stage's own plating rather than a misleading 0. Demand: the Warden. Serves 2 (readable at a glance).
26. **The death screen names the gap.** It shows "Punch n / plating m · Heft X" when a Warden or the boss stood in the way. The T replay of stage 3 waits on wave 4's boss state, and the seam is already there. Demand: the Warden and the boss phase. Serves 2.
27. **The treasure burst lives in the spine,** so every codex category pays the same: 20 scrap times the stage, once per entry per run, at the spot. Demand: events, vaults and the world tricks. Serves 3 and 4 (exploring feeds growth).
28. **The run log records only what the bus and the run state say,** one cumulative line per death and per run end, so the balance report reads the same facts the player saw. Demand: the design feedback loop. Serves 7 (fast without debt).
29. **warden_engaged fires once per stage attempt,** on the first sealed ring entry or the first strike on the Warden, so the music's Warden theme enters on the player's move. Demand: the Warden. Serves 6.

30. **The sealed charge stops at 99%, and only this stage's Warden unseals it.** A Warden that vanishes without a kill opens the stage the normal way and is logged as an anomaly, rather than falling back to the ring. Demand: the Warden. Serves 1: a seam fault never locks or silently changes the exit.
31. **A heft drop is its own fact** (`heft_class_dropped`, sound `class_down`), so a theft never plays the class-up stinger. Demand: the boss's crane. Serves form mirrors function.
32. **A retry rewinds the codex's per-run memory for that attempt,** so a discovery made again after a retry fires and pays again. The retry already took its burst scrap back. Demand: events, vaults and world tricks. Serves 2 and 4.

33. **The boss is a flow state behind one module seam.** After stage 3's launch the run enters `boss` and calls `enter_arena` with the ball's velocity. The arena-entry checkpoint makes R retry the fight, not the stage. `boss_defeated` gives a 2.5 s victory beat before the summary. Without a boss module the run ends as before. Demand: the boss phase. Serves 1 (the launch carries into the arena) and 2 (R, T and N on one screen).

34. **The arena is not a stage.** Flow's `in_stage()` is false in the arena, and `stage_content_cleared` tells stage modules to stand down on arena entry and on every arena retry. The flood cannot push, the director cannot spawn, and stage-3 chasers do not follow the ball into the fight. The arena has its own fall rescue, owned by the boss module. Demand: the boss phase. Serves 1: nothing the player cannot see pushes or blocks them.

35. **Contacts are read from the motion before the bounce.** The rigid ball keeps the last four velocities it handed to the solver, and a contact uses the one closing fastest on the enemy. A drop lands as a stomp with the speed it fell at: 17.62 m/s after 6 m, where the live velocity had already bounced to a ram at 0. A roll rams with the speed it rolled at: 13.30 m/s, not the 3.39 left after the hit. Demand: the Warden's vent caps (stomp and slam), the boss's caps and scooter. Serves 1 ("collisions have weight").

Closed: the flood's drag in the basin is allowed (R-21). It is announced, visible and avoidable, a foreseen cost from where the player chooses to roll.

## Combat and classes (writer A)

The contract other modules read is [src/combat/README.md](src/combat/README.md). The numbers below come from the `tests/combat_*.gd` runs on this branch.

1. **Weapons fire bolts, and one aim query sends them.** The reticle's centre ray finds the point, and the centre bolt of every volley flies straight at it and never corkscrews. The ray starts level with the ball, so nothing behind the shooter catches the aim. There is no aim magnetism anywhere. Serves TADR-008 (the reticle shows what the shot hits) and 5.
2. **The runner-gunner is laser-true.** It fires 12 bolts a second at 7 damage, with a thin 0.25 m field and no spread. A chaser 16 m out died 0.66 s into held fire. Serves 5 ("rapid tight laser shots").
3. **Overdrive is the runner's escape hatch.** F blasts a 12 m ring for 30 damage with a heavy shove, then gives 3 s of 1.6 times the fire rate, on a 6 s cooldown. It clears space when you are surrounded, so you keep rolling instead of braking. Serves 5 (the area alternative) and 1.
4. **The arc caster forgives with a wide field and a chain, not a magnet.** It fires 5 bolts a second at 12 damage, with a 0.55 m field and one chain jump within 9 m. In the test the chain reached the chaser beside the target 4 times. Serves 5 and TADR-008, since a homing bolt would make the reticle lie.
5. **The pylon turret is the caster, planted.** While mounted, held fire fires 9 volleys a second, chains two jumps within 16 m, and reaches 95 m. The test measured 10 turret volleys in 1 s. Serves 5 and 8.
6. **The engineer's special is one discharge with two triggers.** Every plant fires it free, powered by the banked speed: that is the opening burst. F fires it any time, powered by your current speed, or by the bank while mounted, on a 5 s cooldown. Speed buys arcs, reach and damage. A plant at 5.0 m/s arced 2 enemies for 20.9 each within 14.2 m. A plant at 25.8 m/s arced 7 for 50.1 each within 23.6 m. Serves 8 ("a faster mount gives a stronger opening burst") and 1, since speed becomes power rather than something the wall takes away.
7. **The shoulder view follows the mount state every tick,** not only the mount and dismount signals. A retry, a death or an E drop can never leave the camera stuck at the shoulder. In the test the blend reached 1.00, and 10 of 10 turret shots sat on the shoulder reticle's ray and struck the chaser under it. Serves entry 6 ("snap to a more shoulder view") and 5.
8. **Patterns are data, and transformers are rows.** A row adds, then multiplies, then floors pattern fields, once per stack. Cross split adds three arms around the aim line, making a cross and then a seven-point star. Spiral wave turns the pattern 23 degrees per volley and corkscrews the off-centre bolts. Parts reach these through `register_part(part_id, ids)` or by using a transformer id as the part id. Combat recomputes from `run.parts`, so a retry restores the build. Serves 4 (Isaac and Gunfire Reborn synergies) and 7.
9. **Stacking can multiply damage without limit, but never what is on screen.** A volley has at most 16 bolts and 8 arms, fire rate stops at 20 a second, and at most 64 player bolts are live. Past the cap a new bolt retires the oldest, so the trigger never stalls and only the far end of the stream thins. Bolts fade from 0.9 to 0.4 alpha as the count climbs. A spiral plus two crosses plus every rate and range tier would put about 103 bolts up; the test peaked at 64 and kept 20 volleys a second. Serves 4 ("cap projectile counts") and 1.
10. **Enemy telegraphs always draw on top.** They use `HAZARD`, with no depth test, at render priority 40. Player bolts draw at 0 and combat effects at 1, and no player weapon uses `HAZARD`. Serves 4 ("never let our own effects hide an enemy").
11. **An enemy hurts you only through the geometry it showed you.** Each attack commits a ring or a lane once, and the damage asks that same node whether the ball is inside. Touching an enemy that is not attacking costs nothing but the bump. Rings and lanes reach 3.5 to 4 m above the ground, so a jump or a glide clears them. In the test a charge lane committed on the ball missed once the ball was moved 12.6 m off it. Serves 1 (nothing stops you unforeseen) and AGENTS.md (damage and telegraphs consume the same committed geometry).
12. **The chaser lunges down a lane; the lobber shells a ring.** The chaser lays an 11 m lane with a 0.6 s wind-up. A knock of 10 m/s or more during the wind-up breaks the lunge, so a ram or overdrive can interrupt it. The lobber commits its landing ring the moment it fires, leading a moving ball by half its travel, and shows the ring for the whole 1.9 s flight. A shell already in the air still lands if its lobber dies, because what was shown is what happens. Serves 1 and TADR-008.
13. **A hit taken shoves the ball** by 5 to 10 m/s along the attack. Serves 1: collisions have weight, and a hit taken is the player's own performance. It uses `add_external_accel`, which CONTRACTS now lists for a committed, telegraphed hit, the Warden pulse and the tug.
14. **Weight divides every knock.** A chaser weighs 1, a lobber 3 and the miniboss 5. The lobber used to be immovable and now slides when rammed. Serves 1 ("collisions have weight").
15. **The miniboss fights in a readable rhythm.** It alternates a leap slam (a 7 m ring) and a charge (a 30 m lane, 1 s brace). After each attack comes a 2.4 s weak window, and the window reads three ways: the armour plates swing open, the core swells and glows, and a halo spins overhead. Since the demo spec it speaks the plating grammar: its plating is 1.25 times the stage Warden's (38, 75, 119), and the weak window sets it to 0, so a small punch lands in full there and chips 20% outside. It has 600 health times stage threat. The base laser killed it 12.4 s into held fire at stage 1, starting at its second window. Serves 3 (the boss half of an event), 8 ("feel like a badass": read, dodge, punish) and 4 (readable threats).
16. **An enemy that falls through the map is put back on the ground, not killed,** so nobody gets scrap they did not earn. Serves 7.
17. **One function decides every hit against plating.** `resolve()` computes punch (a contact's force times heft, or a shot's damage), compares it with the target's plating for that hit and weak point, and returns full or a 20% chip. `strike()`, `punch_now()`, `plating_of()`, `would_crush()` and the amber/slate tint all read it, so the tint, the objective's "Punch 48 / plating 60" and the damage cannot disagree. Measured: a ram at force 14 dealt 32.2 in full to plating 30, a 14-damage bolt chipped 2.8, and `would_crush` matched 20 of 20 contacts. Serves the spec's demand (punch against plating) and TADR-008.
18. **The Warden is a lock, not a fight you can lose to.** It never moves (0.0000 m over 30 s), its pulse only lifts (planar speed across a pulse changed by at most 0.18%, lift about +10 m/s), and every pulse follows a telegraph of at least 2.0 s. The lift and the 10 damage only reach a ball inside the rings it showed. Base runner fire chipped Warden I down in 24.1 s and Warden III in 59.7 s, against 60 s and 90 s limits. Serves 1 (it never takes your line) and the spec's one MUST per stage.
19. **Each Warden lesson is data and reads on the body.** Vent caps are bright discs on top: a stomp or slam on one meets plating 0 for 1.5 times its punch (61.5 from a punch of 41 against plating 60). The magnet arm swings up over the clamp and burns bright during its tug; a bolt on it drops the plating to 0 for 3 s (measured 269 ticks). The arm rides 3.6 m high because at 1 m it sat in the line of body fire and got shot by accident; with that fixed, Warden III took 0 accidental stuns. Serves the spec's lessons and 8 (earn the break).
20. **The tug is 3.5 m/s², not the 6 in the change request.** At 6, a ball driving away through a tug fell to 10.4 m/s against the request's own 0.8 x cruise floor of 11.2, while the same run with no tug bottomed at 12.9. At 3.5 the floor holds, at 11.66. Serves 1 (you can always drive out).
21. **Vaults are plated at the next stage's Warden,** 60, 95 and 120, with 400 health per stage, and never move or attack. Cracking one early says your build is ahead of the curve. Serves 3 (opt-in reward) and the spec's SHOULD tier.
22. **Shells can be batted home.** A bolt, or a rolling ball at CRUISE or faster, that reaches a lobber's shell in flight sends it back at its lobber. The return commits a new ring in amber, because a player-owned blast must not read as a threat, and hits every enemy inside it for 40. Serves the codex treasure hunt ("a deflected lobber shell kills its own lobber") and 1, since the counterplay happens at speed.
23. **Six enemy codex tricks, detected by combat:** Return to Sender, Counter-ram, Rolling Tide (three crushes in a second), Parting Shot (a kill within 1 s of a slingshot), Pulse Return (a cap stomp within 1.5 s of a pulse) and Demagnetized (break the Warden while the magnet has it unplated). Each card's clue names one ingredient and hints at the other. A frozen-chaser shatter waits for build's frost state. Serves the spec's synergies ("shown, discoverable, and accidental").
24. **Chasers have 60 health, not 40.** At heft 1 a ram at 20 m/s has punch 40 and bounces off one; from heft 1.5 it rolls straight through. Measured with the real spawner: heft 3 kept 18.21 of 18.67 m/s through the crush and killed it; heft 1.2 rammed, knocked it and it lived. Serves the spec's Katamari moment (growth turns fodder from obstacle into pickup) and 1.
25. **A kill is recorded on the tick health reaches 0, and the body stops colliding on that tick.** The Warden's `enemy_killed` arrives with it still in the tree, collision layer 0 and integrity 0, so the gate opens from the kill path and a crush exception never ends inside a live collider. Serves 1 (no unforeseen depenetration stop) and TADR-008.
26. **Combat reads build's weapon tuning keys live, at the moment it fires.** Handler order on `part_gained` can never leave a stale multiplier. Serves 7.

Open questions for Andrew:

- F on the engineer fires the pylon discharge from your speed. Would you rather F did something with the pylon itself, such as planting one without a wall?
- A hit taken shoves you 5 to 10 m/s. Is that weight, or does it read as the game taking your line?
- Base fire chips Warden III down in about a minute without its lesson. Does that feel like a lock you broke, or a chore (the spec's rival question)?

## Build and growth (writer B)

1. **The scrap magnet reaches further the faster you roll.** Its reach is 7 m plus 0.3 m per m/s of speed, so about 16 m at 30 m/s. A piece it catches homes in faster than the ball can outrun it and stays caught. Serves momentum is sacred (1): you never slow down or turn back for scrap.
2. **Scrap sticks to the ball, and so do parts.** Held scrap shows as chunks on the ball's surface, growing with the square root of the total and capped at 36. Each part is a piece of hardware: its shape says its family (barrel, cross, coil, rod, vane, capacitor, gem) and its colour says its rarity. Pieces cap at 24 and stick out no more than 0.7 m. Spending at a fabricator takes chunks off and bolts a part on, so the purchase reads on the ball. Serves 1 ("building momentum and rolling and growing"), 4 (visual clarity) and form mirrors function.
3. **Fabricators are hoops you roll through.** They stand across the scrap lines, away from the beacon and the spawn: 2, 3 and 4 hoops in stages 1 to 3. There is no collider. Roll through with enough scrap and the hoop spends it and grants the part. Short of scrap, nothing happens, and the hoop reads "need N scrap (have M)". After a sale the hoop restocks with a new part 2.5 s later. The build test measured 19.34 m/s before a paid pass and 19.86 m/s after, and 18.79 then 18.54 m/s through an unpaid one. Serves 1 ("fabricators are drive-through").
4. **Price depends only on the part's tier and how many parts you hold.** It is 10 scrap times 1, 1.6 or 2.6 by tier, times 1.15 for each part held. A retry restores the part list, so it restores prices too. Serves quick restart (2) and fast without debt (7).
5. **A gate says what it gives while you approach.** It shows the part name large, its effect in one line, and "* completes Storm Lattice *" when taking it would complete a synergy. Rarity shows twice: in the arch's colour and in the topper's shape (a bar for common, a diamond for rare, a star for epic). The offer records carry `name`, `text`, `rarity`, `tier` and `synergy` for presentation. Serves form mirrors function and 1 ("reward choices are gates you roll through").
6. **Offers climb in tier across the run.** (Superseded by 23: tier now follows bonus cores, not the stage.) The test measured a mean offered tier of 1.25, 1.59 and 1.97 in stages 1 to 3. When the run is one part short of a synergy, half the time one offer slot shows that part. Serves 4 (Isaac-style "one more piece").
7. **The scaling curve runs from modest to absurd on purpose.** Tier 1 gives +10 to 15%, tier 2 +25% and tier 3 +50%. Copies multiply, and pattern levels add. The build sheet's output estimate puts one Hot Rivets at 1.12x a bare weapon and a ten-part late build at about 324x. With punch added in phase 1, the same builds measure 1.44x and about 1,444x. That number is a model for shaping the curve. What is actually drawn and how hard it hits is combat's (A), under the visual caps. Serves 4 ("the ability to scale insanely").
8. **Synergies are derived, never held.** (Superseded in part by 17 and 18: synergies are now tag pairs, and Storm Lattice lost its +25%.) Storm Lattice (Cross Split and Chain Arc) makes every split bolt chain, adds a chain level and +25% damage, and puts a gyroscope cage of arc rings on the ball. Thermal Shock (Frost Vane and Arc Coil) makes the slam's arc shatter nearby ice into a shard nova at double damage, then refreezes the water around you. It puts a frosted coil ring on the ball. A retry that drops a part drops its synergy. Serves 4 and form mirrors function.
9. **Frost on water makes real ground.** Ice is a StaticBody3D on the world layer, so the ball, the grapple and the camera all treat it as terrain. It has the same grip as the ground, so there is no handling penalty (BIBLE line 481). It lasts 9 s and blinks for its last 1.5 s, so losing the ground is never a surprise. The test rolled a ball over 3 m of water: with no ice it sank to the ground, and on ice it stayed at the surface and kept 12.4 m/s. Serves 1 ("nothing ... in a way they didn't see coming") and TADR-009.
10. **Ice never forms inside the ball.** Where the ball already sits below the new surface, the slab forms just under it. The test measured 0.000 m of displacement. Serves 1: creating a surface never pops or blocks the ball.
11. **Frost on ice does nothing.** The table has no frost x ice row, so slabs never stack and land about one radius apart in an overlapping road. Serves TADR-009 (default-deny).
12. **Electrified water hurts enemies only.** The arc is the player's own weapon, so it never costs the player HP or momentum. Serves 8 ("feel like a badass") and 1.
13. **Dash and slam elements resolve in build's own physics step.** The `dashed` and `slammed` handlers only queue them. The frost trail lasts 0.7 s after a Frost Vane dash, with a visible frost puff every third tick, even over dry ground, so the part always shows it is working. Serves TADR-008 (a signal applies nothing) and form mirrors function.
14. **A won event pays out in the same frame.** The milestone handler grants the part at once, so the milestone stinger and "New part" land as one beat. This is the one place build mutates inside a handler. Serves 3 ("win great rewards like key milestone rewards").
15. **Events award named milestone parts.** Surge Run awards Rime Heart: you rode the flood, now you freeze it. Hold the Relay awards Tempest Core. Any other event awards the first milestone part the run does not hold yet. Fabricators and vaults never offer milestone parts, and a gate offers them only when overcharged by two or more bonus cores (23). Class mods (Pylon Capacitor, Runner Overclock) are offered only to their class. Serves 3 and 5.
16. **Build owns the tuning mods.** Weapon numbers travel as tuning mods (`weapon_damage`, `weapon_rate`, `weapon_range`, `turret_damage`), so combat reads them with `tuning.mod(key)` and needs no spine change. Pattern levels, transformers and the element table are behind the build API; [src/build/SEAMS.md](src/build/SEAMS.md) lists every id. Serves 7.

17. **Tags make synergies legible.** Every part carries one or two of nine tags: frost, arc, kinetic, split, spiral, chain, anchor, speed and mass. A named synergy is a tag pair. It goes live when two different held parts carry its two tags, so "something that splits" is a clue a player can act on. Serves Andrew's synergy request ("coherent sense for the player to be willing to experiment") and 4.
18. **Eight named synergies, each a behaviour, never a bare number.**
    - Storm Lattice (chain + split): split bolts chain.
    - Thermal Shock (frost + arc): the slam's arc shatters ice at double damage, then refreezes.
    - Lightning Rod (arc + mass): each pickup sparks an arc at the nearest enemy.
    - Tesla Pylon (anchor + arc): arcs leap to two enemies while mounted or hooked.
    - Wrecking Ball (speed + kinetic): rams send out a shockwave.
    - Shrapnel Bloom (split + kinetic): kills burst into shrapnel.
    - Glacier Hull (mass + frost): hard landings crack out a frost ring.
    - Pinwheel (spiral + speed): at fast speed and above, a blade ring cuts the enemies you pass.

    Storm Lattice lost its +25% damage. Serves 4 ("a synergy changes behaviour, never just a percentage").
19. **Every synergy effect is capped.**
    - Shrapnel: 6 bursts a second, 3 targets each.
    - Lightning Rod: 6 zaps a second.
    - Tesla Pylon: 2 targets every 0.5 s.
    - Pinwheel: up to 4 targets, each cut at most once per 0.3 s.
    - Zap lines on screen: at most 12.
    - Rings: one per live synergy, 8 at most, each at its own tilt and in its first tag's colour.

    The behaviour test sent 20 kills in one tick and measured 6 shrapnel strikes. Serves 4 (visual clarity is the balance rule).
20. **Hint cards set goals.** An undiscovered card names one ingredient and gives a clue from the other's tag: "Chain Arc + something that splits." Offers carry a star line:
    - "* completes Storm Lattice *" when taking the part brings a synergy live;
    - "* ingredient: ??? *" when it is a card's named ingredient;
    - "* ingredient: Storm Lattice *" once that synergy is discovered.

    Serves Andrew's "goal setting and creating some excitement" and 1 (you choose by reading a gate at speed).
21. **A synergy is discovered once per run.** Its first activation is the discovery. A retry that drops it and a later reactivation do not repeat it. From phase 2 the lasting record and the scrap burst belong to the codex registry. Serves 2 and 3.
22. **Accidental finds come from the systems, unnamed.** Each element-table row and the spiral + chain tag rule has a plain description, logged the first time it fires in a run. ## Audio and feel (writer C)

1. **What the player does sounds now; what the world announces lands on the beat.** A jump, a hit, a pickup, a death or the gate you rolled through plays the moment it happens. Serves 1 (feedback never lags your own move) and 6 ("stingers land on the beat").
2. **A burst of flow facts becomes one phrase.** Flow stingers queue in one lane. A run start folds stage 1's hit into its fanfare. A stage clear plays its jingle, and the next stage's hits come exactly two bars later, as the cadence resolves. The last clear folds into the run-end finale. Serves 6 and form mirrors function (one event, one sound).
3. **The stage clear is the rice cooker.** The band cadences A7b9 to D major, a Picardy lift out of the minor key. Over it a mallet-and-beep melody climbs the A7 chord, turns, lands on D, and two "done" beeps ring on high D. Serves Andrew's end-of-cycle-melody reference and 6.
4. **You hear which stage you entered.** Stage N plays N stabs an eighth apart. Serves form mirrors function.
5. **One musical source.** `harmony.gd` holds the tempo, key and scales, and both the band and every pitched cue read it. Rewards climb D minor pentatonic (D F G A C), whose notes sit inside every chord of the loop, so rapid scrap pickups, kill streaks and the beacon charge climb in key. At 100% the beacon lands on home, two octaves up. Serves form mirrors function and 4 (growth you can hear).
6. **Danger is low and minor.** Getting hurt is a buzzy F falling to D. An enemy arriving is a low E-F semitone growl, and a miniboss is pitched a fourth lower and louder. The klaxon is two falling minor-third "WAH"s on the beat. Death is a falling minor line into a low D that sags a fourth. Measured: danger cues average 291 zero crossings a second against 3,617 for rewards. Serves form mirrors function.
7. **The band is 172 BPM drum and bass in D Dorian.** The eight-bar loop is Dm9, G13, Dm9, Cmaj9, Bbmaj9, G13, then Em7b5 and A7b9 as the ii-V turnaround. Calm leaves space: electric-piano chords, a sub and a light hat. Pressure brings the two-step kick up, then snare, ghost notes and a jazz ride, then the reese bass, then the lead, then a bell arpeggio. 8. **The lead is a synth saxophone.** It scoops into each note and uses reedy FM and delayed vibrato. The answer changes every loop (inversion, retrograde or a displaced echo), and a few chromatic approach notes are added. The lead is seeded, so a run replays the same. Serves 6 ("jazzy melodic invention, call and response").
9. **Death never stops the music.** On the next beat the band drops to the keys and waits with you. A retry cancels any pending announcement, and the phrase restarts from bar 1 with the stage hit on the next beat. Serves 2 (quick restart, no dead air).
10. **Hit-stop is weight, never obstruction.** It lasts 30-50 ms and is used only for big contact hits: a ram at 20 m/s or more, a stomp falling at 12 m/s or more, strong slam knockback, or a full slam of strength 0.9 or more. Stops are at least 220 ms apart, so a chain of rams never stutters. Weapon fire and damage taken never freeze. Measured on the real `Engine.time_scale`: the longest dip was 41 ms. Serves 8 and 1.
11. **Squash and stretch is an underdamped spring.** A landing squashes the ball along the contact normal, overshoots into a stretch and settles in about a quarter second, with the contact point planted. The ball also stretches along its travel line with speed. Serves 8 ("snappy and fluid").

A slam kicks the camera down and zooms in 4 degrees, for a thump. Big contact hits kick the camera toward the enemy. Serves 8 ("feel like a badass").
