# Park dressing kits (Dressing lane, wave 17)

Andrew, 19 Oct 01:40 (DECISIONS.md section 8): the parks feel empty. Each park should read as lived in or run down, with many distinct props placed naturally, and no stretch of ground along the ride should look bare. Hero assets go up to about 20,000 triangles at LOD0, with LOD1 and LOD2. The look is Soft toy (18 Oct 00:40): chunky, bevelled and smooth. Machines keep crisp edges with rounded bevels; round bodies go smooth. Readability comes first. Dressing must never be mistaken for an enemy, a pickup or a climbable route.

This page is the lane's contract. Writers build to it, and reviewers judge against it.

## Kits

Each park has a kit of at least 12 kinds, and each kind has 2 or 3 variants (different geometry, not only a tint). Each kit tells that park's story: who lived or worked there and what they left behind, such as tools, debris, signage, laundry, campfires, stacked timber, broken machines and plants growing through scrap.

| Class | LOD0 triangles | LOD1 | LOD2 | Draws | Shadows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `dress_hero` (2 or 3 kinds per park) | 6,000 to 20,000 | about 50% (40 to 60) | about 15% (10 to 20) | 1 per LOD | LOD0 only |
| `dress_prop` | 300 to 3,000 | about 50% | about 15% (at least 40 tris) | 1 per LOD | never |

- A LOD must keep the prop's shape: it should fill 85% of LOD0's outline from every side seen from 12 degrees down, and under 80% it fails (`tools/model_audit.py cover`). Where the collapse eats a part at the target, `kit.py` gives that LOD a quarter more triangles at a time until it reaches 85%, up to 75% of LOD0; `scripts/far-faults.mjs` checks every shipped LOD. On 14 Nov the dam's cable drums drew as floating rings at 100 m before this rule (W1-29).
- Each GLB has one surface, one vertex-colour material, baked AO and wear (`polish.py`), its origin at the foot centre, the floor at y 0, and Y up after import.
- Colour travels as linear `COLOR_0` and nothing else. `kit.py` `inspect_glb` asserts every exported GLB has it. Godot imports the GLB with an albedo-white material that ignores vertex colour, so `park_props.gd` `_mesh()` gives each cached mesh (a copy; the import stays as it is) one material with `vertex_color_use_as_albedo` on, `vertex_color_is_srgb` off, roughness 0.8, metallic 0 and no emission. `park_props_test` checks every built surface has `ARRAY_COLOR` and that material.
- Builds are deterministic: a rebuild is byte-identical.
- `manifest.json` lists, per variant: kind, class, footprint radius, height, triangles per LOD, and tags (`tall`, `plant`, `sign`, `machine`, `soft`).
- A kind may carry an optional `line_clear_m` (set with `Kit.kind(..., line_clear_m=30)`; written to the manifest only when set). It must be a number above 6 (`LINE_CLEAR_LOW_M`) and at most 60 (`LINE_CLEAR_KIND_MAX_M`, because the line index sees 64 m from a leg: a rule whose need plus the footprint radius passes 64 m fails `line_clear` closed, "rule beyond the line reach"); `kit_errors` rejects anything else, NaN included. The larger of the height tier and the kind's value binds in `line_clear`. The four brief kinds `laundry_line`, `washline`, `windsock` and `kite_pennant` (brief: `design-v3/park/dressing/KITS.md`) must carry `line_clear_m` of at least 30 in any kit that defines them, so a prop that reads as a ride line stays 30 m from a real one; `kit_errors` (`BRIEF_LINE_KINDS`) enforces it.

## Readability rules (hard)

- **Colours.** Never use HAZARD `#ff5a3c`, or any red, orange or amber stripes. Never use ANCIENT `#9dff3c` or MINT `#8bf0d3`. Nothing is emissive, and every lamp is unlit; pit and sinkhole have a lamp veto. Never use AMBER `#e5a044` or COPPER `#b77843` as a dominant colour. BRASS `#d9b44a` goes only on Fleet-owned gear. Rust and charcoal sit beside timber, canvas or sandstone so they never read as Fleet.
- **Shapes to avoid:**
  - crates, barrels, bolt-holed boxes and stacked tins (these read as caches or breakables);
  - four-facet bolt-holed chunks (scrap);
  - two-post arches with a lintel (gates);
  - ramps, wedges, stairs, leaning ladders and loading boards (kickers and routes);
  - long rails, taut cables and grindable edges;
  - tall poles on a regular spacing (harpoon anchors);
  - hanging or spinning machines;
  - anything with eyes, legs or a face (enemies; critters belong to the Life lane).
- **Placement.** These are the validator's minimums (`park_props.gd` `validate`), checked for every expanded instance:
  - Clear of every `lines.json` leg (pilots and floor), measured from the footprint edge, by height tier: LOW (up to 1.2 m) at least 6 m, MID (up to 3 m) at least 10 m, TALL (a `tall` tag, a hero, or over 3 m) at least 20 m.
  - Route lanes (`ParkRecipes.clear_zones`, 20 m half-width, built for harpoon-anchor posts) bind TALL props only. LOW and MID ignore them; rule 1 keeps them off the actual lines.
  - Built row footprints (long stamps by the width rule below) keep props out only for ride-feature rows (kicker, drop, rail, grind_line, half_pipe, bowl, elevated_line, ledge, tunnel, flow_line, sky_island, any row with a `ride_fact` outside ground, bank, terrace and anchor_field, and any row with a home). Ground, bank, terrace and anchor-field rows are open; the slope (25 degrees) and seat (0.15 m) rules protect against walls. The landmark, spawn, beacon, gates, activity sites, rescue cages, homes and camps keep their keepouts.
  - Each kicker keeps a capsule along its lip (row length / 2 + 8 m wide) and a run-out capsule 40 m ahead of the lip for landing spots, rather than set_dressing.gd's circle of radius width / 2 + 3 (mesa lips are up to 1,600 m wide).
  - Why: the first dam placement passed the old rules (15 to 30 m from a line, 20 m lanes for every prop, a keepout on every footprint including canyon floors) with 619 instances, yet the before and after stills from the chase camera were identical: legal dressing sat outside the frame. Andrew's rule is to keep ride lines, landing spots, telegraphs and enemy silhouettes clear, which means the line itself and the landing, not a 40 m corridor. The tiers keep the line clear and let low dressing sit near it.
  - Telegraph keepout (`telegraph`): outside every `event_rows` event's own zone, grown by that event's `hold_clear_m` (at least 0) plus the instance radius, litter included (litter that fails is dropped). The zone is the event's geometry, as `src/world/events/natural.gd` `plan`, `industrial.gd` `commit_sector` and `event_director.gd` `_park_clear` read it, and is the union of: (a) the site box (the site row after `NaturalFootprint.resolve`, so a collapse with `effect.face` uses the face; half extents from the site or from `effect.footprint_m`) for every non-lightning event, and for a lightning event too when `EventDirector._park_ground` holds (a ground kind, an arrival family, or any `effect.swap`); (b) for a lightning event, a circle of `effect.strike_m` (0 when absent) at the site point; (c) for an event whose effect has `hazard_m`, a circle of radius `hazard_m[0]` at the site point, a superset of the committed cone or annulus. A lightning event with no ground effect keeps only (b), so the dam's `dome_lightning` (260 by 240 m site, hold 0) does not evict 212 props. Rulings: #241 comment 6329056497 (make the rules checked, not remembered) and 6329171089 (approved this rule). The sinkhole gate (REJECT on #246) found no rule keeping props out of event telegraph zones, and a probe found 3 spot props and 1 litter item inside them.
  - At least 25 m from enemy `home_rows` and camps, and inside the play rect.
  - With a file `ground_rows` list, inside a listed row (see "Explicit surfaces").
  - On ground no steeper than 25 degrees, with the foot seated on the baked ground.
- **Explicit surfaces (`ground_rows`).** A vignette file may carry a top-level `"ground_rows": ["<park row id>", ...]`. Some parks have no continuous ground (islands: the ground callable is one plane under every island, so seat and slope cannot stop a prop floating between islands, and the islands are `sky_island` and `terrace` rows with `ride_fact`s, which are ride features). When the list is present it turns the rows into the placement surfaces:
  - A listed row stops being a footprint keepout for props.
  - Every instance, litter included, must stand inside a listed row's footprint, its own radius plus `GROUND_ROW_INSET_M` (8 m) in from the edge, so nothing sits on a drop lip or a bowl edge. The rule is `ground_row`.
  - Every other rule still applies: line tiers, kicker capsules and run-outs, homes and camps, sites, slope and seat.
  - A row with a `home`, or the site of a `home_rows` entry, can never be listed (the 25 m home rule covers its whole footprint anyway), and an unknown id is a file error. A file without `ground_rows` is unaffected.
  - Long stamps (a row with a `stamp` longer than `ParkSites.STAMP_KEEP_M`, 200 m, on either axis) are left out of ParkSites' footprint cells, because set dressing treats long stamps as the landform. `Context` re-adds some of them itself (`Context.stamped`) by width. A long stamp 60 m or narrower (`STAMP_NARROW_M`, the `low_ground_rows` minimum) that blocks props is a ridden built feature (a wash, slot, fin, rail, lip or step face), so it keeps props out unless the file lists it in `ground_rows` or `low_ground_rows`. A long stamp wider than 60 m (a pit bowl, a rim drop, a fan band) is the landform itself and stays open, guarded by slope, seat, the line tiers and route lanes. A `sky_island` is the exception: it is always re-added, whatever its width, and opened only by `ground_rows` or `low_ground_rows`. Open elements (ground, bank, terrace, anchor_field) without a home stay open even when stamped long; home rows stay under `home_clear`.
  - With a file that has both lists, a low instance (1.0 m or less) also satisfies the `ground_row` containment inside a listed `low_ground_rows` row (same 8 m inset); a taller instance still needs a `ground_rows` row, so it fails `ground_row` as well as `low_ground` there.
  - `tools/park_props_plan.gd` honours the list in `--check`, and the spot finder reads it from the file (or `--ground-rows=a,b,c`).
- **Scatter-dressed rows (`scatter_row`).** A park row whose recipe is `scatter` (`ParkRecipes.SCATTER_DRESSED`: the ridden rows the park scatter dresses, such as sinkhole `cinder_cone` and flume `horseshoe_gorge`) keeps tall and hero instances out of its footprint plus the instance radius. This follows World's scatter keep-out card on the wave 18 board (#241): tall pieces stay out of ridden scatter rows. Low and mid instances stay under the line tiers and the other rules there. Neither `ground_rows` nor `low_ground_rows` opens a scatter-dressed row to tall props.
- **Low ground in area rows (opt-in).** A vignette file may carry a top-level `"low_ground_rows": ["<park row id>", ...]`. A listed row admits only instances whose scaled height is 1.0 m or less (`LOW_GROUND_MAX_M`), litter included; an instance taller than that inside it fails `low_ground` (not a generic keepout), and everything outside listed rows stays blocked by its footprint as before.
  - Listing caps: any instance over 1.0 m whose footprint reaches a listed `low_ground_rows` row fails `low_ground`, whether or not the row would block (a wide long stamp is open, but listing it still allows only 1.0 m or lower there; a hero in a listed row fails).
  - A row id may not appear in both `ground_rows` and `low_ground_rows`: a ground row has no height cap, so a row in both would skip the 1.0 m limit. `file_errors` rejects it; list the row in one.
  - Why: the coordinator's principle is that nothing solid taller than about 1 m that the pilot visibly passes through may stand in the corridor. Props carry no collision, and nothing taller than the pilot's hip stands where the pilot rolls, so low clutter may sit on the big area rows (crown's `drop_ramp`, sinkhole's drop and floor rows, islands' `sky_island` strips and the 1,600 m `lookout_mesa` stamp) instead of being pushed out to the verges 30 to 80 m away.
  - Eligible rows, enforced in `file_errors` (anything else is a file error that names the row): a `flow_line`, `bowl`, `drop` or `sky_island` with a footprint of at least 60 m on both axes; or a home site (a `home_rows` site, or a row with a `home`) whose footprint is longer than 200 m on either axis. Narrow features are never eligible: kicker, rail, grind_line, half_pipe, ledge, tunnel, elevated_line.
  - Every other rule still applies inside a listed row: the line tiers (LOW 6 m from the footprint edge), kicker lip and run-out capsules, `home_clear` for camps (25 m), spawn, beacon, gates, sites, cages and event rows, the landmark, slope (25 degrees) and seat, the play rect and, if the file also has `ground_rows`, the `ground_row` containment. For a listed home stamp the 25 m `home_clear` covers the camps only, not the stamp's whole footprint.
  - `tools/park_props_plan.gd` honours the list in `--check`, and the spot finder reads it from the file (or `--low-ground-rows=a,b`) with `--tier=low`, whose probe is 1.0 m tall.
- **Perch anchors.** A variant 6 m tall or taller carries `perch: [x, y, z]` in the manifest (metres, the prop's local frame, origin at the foot centre, on its top surface; `kit.py` takes the recipe's `p.perch(point)` or the top-centre of the highest face), the contract in `PERCH-ANCHOR.md`. `park_props_test` checks every manifest's perch: finite, above the foot, inside the footprint radius, at least 6 m up.
- **Board palette check.** `Kit(park, board={"boards": [paths under docs/art/reference/], "swatches": {hex: label}, "approved": {hex: reason}})` opts a park in; without a board the kit prints `KIT note: <park> declares no board palette; the board check is off` and builds as before. With one, every painted colour must be within `BOARD_NEAR` 0.05 (Euclidean, 0..1 sRGB, the move of `tint()`'s default 0.08 jitter on a mid colour) of a swatch or approved hex, or an approved shade of a swatch (same hue within `SHADE_HUE` 0.03 and saturation within `SHADE_SAT` 0.08, value 0.70 to 1.25 times the swatch's, which covers `shade()` and `tint()`), or a base `C` colour or its shade (`C` as kit.py defines it, snapshotted as `BASE_C` at import, so colours a park adds with `C.update(...)` get no pass); anything else is a hard `off-board colour` error naming the nearest swatch. `C` itself is not yet board-checked (known limit). Separately, and with or without a board, a variant's brass (within `BANNED_NEAR` of `#d9b44a`) must form one tag: at most `BRASS_TAG_PARTS` 4 primitives (a plate and its rivets) inside a 0.6 m box. One tag per vignette is not visible to the kit (known limit). Emission is already refused at export (`kit.py` asserts no `emissiveFactor`). The planted off-board colour in `_selftest_board` is the control; each park adopts its board in its own PR.
- **Vignettes, not scatter.** Props stand in clusters that each tell one moment: a camp corner, a collapsed workshop, an overgrown cart, a laundry line. A sparse litter layer (small debris and plants, off the line inside the tier bands: `band_m` near at least 6 m, far at most 64 m) carries the eye between vignettes. Repeated small props use MultiMesh.

## Frame cost

- Ranges are set on every view. Heroes: LOD0 to 70 m, LOD1 to 180 m, LOD2 to 450 m. Props: LOD0 to 35 m, LOD1 to 90 m, LOD2 to 200 m. Litter: LOD1 to 70 m only. Fade mode is self, with margins.
- Draws: per vignette, one MultiMesh per (mesh, LOD0) and per (mesh, LOD1), and the vignette's whole LOD2 as ONE merged mesh (`Far_L2`: every non-hero member's LOD2 baked into one vertex-colour surface, with the members' positions and kinds kept in meta for the audit); litter batched per 160 m chunk. Measured on the dam and islands files at their peak poses, the per-(mesh, LOD2) batches were 100 to 130 of the 157 to 168 props-only draws (low props, 90 to 200 m out), and the merge takes the dam's peak from 157 to 70 and the islands' from 168 to 83 (medians 22 to 9 and 5 to 3), with the peak primitive delta up 0.5% and 3% (a merged mesh is not frustum-culled per member). A range scale by size (LOW props ending sooner) was not needed. The bar is dressing adding at most 120 draws at any sampled ride-path pose, measured as the OpenGL proxy in the cloud. The park stays inside PARK.md's caps: draws p99 at most 1.3 times base on s3, and at most 1,400 on p1.
- Litter batches extend `visibility_range_end` by the chunk's spread (the farthest member from the bounds centre), because a visibility range is measured to the batch's bounds centre, not to each member (same reasoning as `set_dressing.gd`). A chunk therefore keeps every member visible out to 70 m.
- Heroes keep LOD0 shadows and full ranges on the low preset; the low preset only drops litter and halves the props' LOD0 and LOD1 ends.
- No shadow casters on small props or litter.
- The ruling is DECISIONS.md section 8, "Lived-in parks", 19 Oct 01:40, on main.
- Every park PR posts `NEEDS: gpu` on the Wave board for a 4080 A/B. Cloud frame times never count.

## Density bar (acceptance)

AMBITION-BRIEF.md, "Lived-in parks": every 150 m of ride line has at least 3 distinct prop kinds in view within 60 m of the player camera. Exempt: arenas and engage rings, telegraph and hazard zones, and spaces a ruling keeps open. `park_props_audit.gd` writes `density_windows`, `density_exempt` (and `density_exempt_by`), `density_pass`, `density_fail_windows` (line, start m, kinds) and `density_share` to `summary.json`: per line, 150 m windows of the audit's samples (a tail of at least 75 m counts); a window's kinds are the union of park-prop kinds (group `park_props` only, set dressing excluded) in view within 60 m at any of its poses. A window is exempt when its midpoint lies in a `home_rows` site footprint or the landmark footprint grown 25 m (arena), within 25 m of an `activity_rows` `enemy_camp` point (`camp`), an `event_rows` site footprint grown by `hold_clear_m` (telegraph), or a `--open=x:z:r:<reason>` space (`open`). An `--open` space carries a reason that the audit checks instead of taking on trust (coordinator ruling #241 comment 6329579296): `runout:<kicker>` (everything after the third colon is the kicker id; `ParkProps.runout_near` finds that kicker's run-out capsule within 40 m of the window midpoint) or `no_cell` (`ParkProps.cone_legal_cells` finds no legal cell in the camera's real view frustum, out to 60 m horizontally, over every sample pose in the window). The frustum is the chase camera projected without a Camera3D (basis `Basis(UP, yaw) * Basis(RIGHT, pitch)`, vertical fov and the 1280 by 720 aspect), because near the camera the ground frustum is wider than `fov_h / 2`. A cell is legal when any probe passes every placement rule there: the probes are every variant of every kind at each placement scale of the file (1.0, every template prop scale, and the litter range's minimum 0.9 and maximum 1.15; slope and seat sample the footprint, so scale matters), mid and tall kinds and kinds with a `line_clear_m` included, each judged under its own tier and `line_clear_m`; the audit names the probe that found a cell. The probe context applies the file's `ground_rows` and `low_ground_rows` as `expand` does. Litter scales between the minimum and maximum are not sampled, so the residual tolerance is the grid, the foot point and intermediate litter scales. The scan grid is 2.5 m, so a legal pocket narrower than about 2.5 m can be missed, which lets a `no_cell` claim pass. A missing or unknown reason is an argument error (exit 2). A window whose reason holds is exempt and counted in `density_exempt_by` as `open:runout` or `open:no_cell`; one whose reason fails is not exempt, is judged on its kinds like any other window, and is listed in `density_open_rejected` as `{at, r, reason, why, cell}`. `density_open_checked` counts the windows checked. This replaces the lane's own bare-share target as the acceptance metric. A third reason, `filled:<cx>:<cz>` (Dressing card (b), #241 6335154095 item 2b), is for a window whose legal cells are all used: it holds when the frustum has at least one legal cell, every legal cell (counted once, however many poses see it) lies within a placed vignette prop's radius plus half a cell (litter fills nothing), and the named cell is within one cell of such a cell. A cone scan that reaches its 4096-cell cap never holds. It counts in `density_exempt_by` as `open:filled`.

`park_props_plan.gd --check` also fails a file on spot patterns (`ParkProps.spot_pattern_errors`, card (b)): `row`, when 3 spots lie within 1.5 m of a line through two spots (up to 80 m apart) with consecutive gaps above 0.5 m and at most 40 m and a spacing CV under 0.1; and `yaw`, when 3 or more spots are linked by pairs at most 24 m apart (two tall-tier vignette radii) whose yaws differ by at most 1 degree. It fails when the expansion fingerprint differs from the file's `pin` (a map file with no pin fails too). It prints `"level":"lint"` lines, which do not fail, for a `near` label with stray whitespace or one that ends in its template story cut mid-word.
