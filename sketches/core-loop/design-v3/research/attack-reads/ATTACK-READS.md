## One fact through every layer

Extend the `core/event_kinds.gd:229` threat-row pattern with attack-specific rows keyed by the IDs below. Each row owns label, threat type, glyph, allegiance colour, pose key, warning cue, release cue and floor mode. Reuse `core/class_def.gd:51` for pilot identity and `combat/roster.gd:38` for faction voice; do not copy those palettes or motifs into actors. Labels in this document are proposed codex wording, not floating combat instructions.

Each attack instance carries its row ID plus the authoritative geometry, phase and existing progress. Body motion reads that progress and committed direction; floor rendering reads the same descriptor as containment; audio starts on the phase transition; the codex reads the same label and glyph. A pose key chooses presentation, never a second timer. Cancellation/death clears the pose and pending release cue; a fired shell survives its owner exactly as it does now. Overlapping Warden pulse, magnet and adaptation require separate instance IDs.

`combat/telegraph.gd:142` tests rings, wedges and lanes with terrain-relative height, and `:200` exposes their drawing descriptor. Boss equivalents are `boss/hazard.gd:197` and `:251`. Preserve extra eligibility conditions such as airborne-only nets, moving-body contact, crane sweep-front crossing and quenched slag holes. A footprint alone is not the whole damage test.

The current warning combines type, owner and mechanic sounds, `presentation/audio.gd:718`; these cues are distance-attenuated, not positional, `:734`. New attack cues should replace the redundant type/tag onset with one recognizable phrase, retaining faction timbre. Do not add a fourth simultaneous warning. Existing baked threat sounds contain apparent release accents at fixed sample offsets, `presentation/sfx_bank.gd:823`; remove that false timing when replacing them. Play the actual release accent on the live transition, never on a music beat.

Proposed sound language: a rounded 120–300 ms identity phrase at wind-up start, then a quiet sustained charge if useful, then a separate short strike accent. Use the existing threat register and faction instruments; whimsical rhythm is welcome, reward cadences are reserved. The descriptions below are briefs for Audio, who owns final synthesis and mix. Direction comes from the visible tool and trace until spatial audio is deliberately implemented; stereo decoration must not pretend to locate an offscreen attacker.

## Chase-camera scale and timing

Author from the normal low chase view, rear and three-quarter approaches, not a character-sheet camera. Starting targets at 1080p: moving tool silhouette at least 24 px across at ordinary fight distance, displacement at least 12 px; 48 px and 24 px for Warden/Brindle. These are proposed screen-space checks, not measured results. Test common enemies at 10–30 m, lobbers at 30–60 m and Wardens at their 28–30 m engagement rim. A far warning must retain a visible airborne/tool cue; a 75 m lobber cannot rely on a tiny muzzle glow.

Use roughly the first third of the existing warning to reach the pose, hold its clear direction through the middle third, compress charge in the final third, then strike. These are fractions, never extra seconds. PROJECTILE attacks separate launch anticipation from flight progress. Freeze orientation at commitment; the lobber is the explicit exception before release because its target is not committed yet. Keep pose transforms on visual children, off colliders and roots used by damage. No camera shake during anticipation. Reduced motion keeps the held pose and charge value.

Floor codes: **L** thin projection boundary/axis; **H** translucent hazard haze; **B** both. Hostile is HAZARD red with value contrast; player-owned is the pilot colour; harmless lift is bone. A line must follow the real boundary, not just the centre of a wide lane. No raised walls across the view. Preserve a few height ticks or actual suspended mesh edges where jumping matters. Clear live/pending distinctions and safe holes survive haze.

**K** means Andrew needs a chase-camera keyframe strip before implementation: start, held wind-up, strike, recovery, with the actual footprint and escape shown. **N** means a bounded extension of an existing pose can start without a new keyframe. Every row names its smallest actor slice; shared row/audio plumbing is prepared once below.

## Player attacks, per pilot

Player previews follow current aim while held and commit at release, `combat/combat.gd:952`. `core/ability_state.gd:61` gives a held-full grace of 0.5 s; releases can happen earlier. No cue may promise a fixed strike time. Instant fire, contact and procs have zero guaranteed anticipation; use the already-visible armed tool, velocity or descent as preparation, with an immediate strike cue. Adding a delay to satisfy a pose is explicitly out of scope.

The current source does not give every player attack one immutable telegraph object. Gauge shapes, bolt records, sticky records and contact queries are separate authorities. Export those existing results into read-only presentation facts where needed; do not add a second target solve. Player area forgiveness is 18% plus target hull for ring/line queries, `combat/combat.gd:1009`; the floor is the nominal footprint, not a claim that every point outside is safe. Sticky blast is spherical and the basic slam tests 3D distance, so do not imply an infinite cylinder.

## Play test: a stranger can dodge the first one

Use native Godot at the normal chase camera and normal movement speed. Recruit players who have never seen the tested attack; teach movement in an empty space only. No attack explanation, preview card, slow motion or retry before the scored encounter. Andrew's review settles feel, but familiarity makes his retries unsuitable as first-sight evidence.

For each hostile attack, collect 20 independent first exposures, counterbalanced across approaches, pilot and encounter order. Start on a trajectory that will be hit/caught without a new action, with at least one feasible escape within the unchanged warning. Record the eligibility rule before scoring; do not exclude a failure because the player missed the cue. Avoid repeated family exposure contaminating first-sight results by using fresh participants for close variants such as Warden pulses and both miniboss moves.

Primary score: a new input after wind-up begins that avoids the actual damage/hold query at resolution while staying in the encounter. Require at least 15/20 for each attack, not a pooled average; the two-sided 95% Wilson lower bound then exceeds 50%. Also report all raw counts and invalid setups. Interruption, successful deflection and simply already being safe are separate outcomes, not dodges. A first-sight friendly shell bat is useful but does not inflate the dodge count.

Repeat with fresh players in mixed packs and boss phases with music, gunfire and haze enabled. Capture continuous video/audio, camera and input, attack ID/instance, committed descriptor, phase timestamps and actual hit/hold result. Include slopes, lateral approach, camera turning and partial occlusion; report fully offscreen onset separately. Freeze candidate revision, camera, load and audio settings so failures have a cause to inspect.

Diagnostic cohorts, also fresh: muted body+floor, sound+floor with body obscured, and body+sound with floor reduced to minimum. Ask which move/escape they expected only after resolution. The sound test can establish cue distinction, not magical first-use knowledge of an arbitrary tune. A failure returns to silhouette, direction or cue overlap before lengthening an attack clock. A still or a headless hit test cannot establish this criterion.

Non-damaging reads get matching tasks: identify Warden lift versus core danger, keep grounded under a net, recognize an open weak point, distinguish drone deployment/ejection from damage. W07's immediate crater is scored as part of the preceding pulse and subsequent escape, not as a fictitious independently warned dodge. Player attacks get first-use prediction tests instead: before release, place/aim so the intended target is hit and a control target is missed; explain projectile versus area and charge/cancel correctly. Instant contact/proc reads are judged by source/consequence recognition after one use, never by an invented warning window.
