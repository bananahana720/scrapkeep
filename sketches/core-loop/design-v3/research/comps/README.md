# Comps: what Scrapkeep borrows from the games Andrew loves

Research pack for [CS-01](../../../../../BACKLOG.md#cs-01), 10 October 2026. Risk of Rain 2 and The Binding of Isaac: Rebirth (Afterbirth+ data) are covered under CS-01; Gunfire Reborn under [CS-02](../../../../../BACKLOG.md#cs-02) (11 October 2026, a static study of a clone of the install plus the public wiki); Roboquest is the follow-up card CS-03, which also completes the [four-game map comparison](maps-compared.md). Coordinator: Claude Fable 5.1, with Haiku scouts on the sources, Sonnet data workers on the measurements, Sonnet writers on the notes, and an Opus verifier and reviewer on the result.

## Posture

We borrow relationships, formulas and structure, and we reimplement them. We measure production budgets from metadata and export nothing. Study inputs outside the repository are deleted when a card is accepted, with one recorded exception: Andrew keeps the Roboquest install clone as a private modding archive outside the Scrapkeep pipeline (decision 5, TRACKING CS-03-A1, `docs/project/ASSETS.md` line 23). We trace free upstream sources and fetch them from the original under their own license. No model, texture, audio, music, code text, layout or art from any of these games enters the repository or a build, none seeds image generation, and no name from them is used as a Scrapkeep name. Names appear in these notes only as evidence labels. The rule is written where asset admission lives: `docs/project/ASSETS.md`, "Comps study posture". The study copies live outside the repository and are deleted when this pack is accepted; the receipts that survive are the notes' parameter tables, the numbers-only CSV and the scripts' names recorded in TRACKING.

Every note has the same six sections: what it does, parameters with a source per row, why it works, what Scrapkeep borrows, what it must not copy, and one lab measure. Each carries a diagram. Since CS-03 a mechanical enum gate in the study tools backs the review of that rule: it harvests every shipped enum's value set from the table summaries, scans each note per paragraph or table with stems and split identifiers, and flags a block that tallies two values of a rarity, rule, mode, action or status set or names three private values of any set; a planted control must hit on every run. It caught both review findings of that class when they were planted back in, and it still raises false positives on large tables, so it is a review aid with a reader behind it, not a pass gate. Reproduced value sets failed review in CS-02 and twice in CS-03; counts by unnamed tier, mode or type are the rule.

**Principle vocabulary.** The principles named below are Andrew's, recorded in `docs/playtests/PLAYTEST-LOG.md` entry 6 and paraphrased here in the short forms the project already uses (momentum is sacred, quick restart, opt-in layers, insane scaling bounded by visual clarity, class identities, high-BPM jazzy EDM, harmonize apparent contradictions), plus readability first and the Arizona jungle-gym landforms from later entries. The notes call "insane scaling bounded by visual clarity" by its short name, readable-capped scaling; the two are the same principle. They are not yet Bible text; card DF-05 carries them into the Bible and is not authorized. The "Bible anchor" column names the Bible or ADR text that exists today and would change.

## Andrew's decisions this session

1. **Stage shape:** one open stage carrying the main objective, with authored pockets (arenas, secret rooms, trade rooms) embedded as optional content visible from the open space.
2. **Great rooms** (from a thousand hours of Isaac): layouts you can solve on entry, enemy mixes that force movement, optional risk pockets. The authored library is the means, those three are the ends.
3. **Companions:** a crew of scrap bots assembled from scrap over the run, each with a role. Fully fleshed means they grow with the run, persist across stages and can be repaired or revived, and have personality and voice. Direct command controls were not chosen. The Bible's one-companion rule was amended for this on 10 October 2026 (TADR-011); see the veto list.

4. **No ripped meshes, even resculpted** (11 October 2026): a derivative of a shipped model stays a derivative and would break forge provenance. The clean version of the same workflow is a CC0 or CC-BY base reshaped to a measured grammar; [enemy design grammar](roboquest/enemy-design-grammar.md) carries the grammar and the base-mesh shortlist.
5. **The Roboquest install clone is retained** as Andrew's private modding archive for a game its developers stopped supporting, outside the Scrapkeep study folder at a path TRACKING names. Nothing in the Scrapkeep pipeline reads it.

## Proposed asset-forge budget deltas

From the measured tables in [production budgets](ror2/production-budgets.md), for the forge owner on branch `claude/reverse-engineer-asset-pipeline-a5025f`. Caveat: the game's mesh rows are per piece and our caps are per asset, so a summed per-character pass should confirm each delta before it lands.

1. Foes: add a class of 500 to 5,000 triangles, bosses up to 10,000, with a 40-bone cap. Our kit band of 800 to 1,500 is too tight against the measured monsters (median 528, p90 4,814).
2. Props and pickups: lower the prop floor from 150 to 40 triangles, add a pickup class of 70 to 2,200 triangles with an 8-bone cap, and lower the terrain texture floor from 256 to 128.
3. Missing caps: hero 80 bones, gameplay clips at most 3.5 s, at most 50 clips per hero. Keep the hero band of 3,000 to 6,500.
4. Gunfire's measurements (CS-02) support the ceiling side of delta 1 (its enemy median of 192 sits under the 500 floor, so the floor is not confirmed) and delta 3, and sharpen two readings: enemy bones exceed the proposed 40 at p90 in both games (49 and 155), so the cap is a median target, not a hard ceiling; a stylized hero ships at 17 bones median, far under 80, so the hero cap is a ceiling with room, not a budget to fill; boss clips reach 5.2 s at p90, so the 3.5 s clip cap needs a boss exception or applies to gameplay clips only. Per-piece caveat as above.

5. Roboquest's measurements (CS-03) are the first high-poly data point and support no lowering: skeletal heroes at 34,098 triangles median, enemies 14,152, bosses 29,384 at LOD0; heroes, enemies and weapons sit about 18 to 74 times the other two games' medians and bosses about 60 to 330 times (whole assets against per-piece rows, so part of the gap is a unit difference) and 3 to 4.5 times on props and terrain; hero rigs 47 bones median with a heaviest rig of 68, so the proposed 80-bone hero cap holds as a ceiling; enemy bones median 27 with p90 55 above the proposed 40 for the third time, so that cap reads as a median target with the ceiling left to the per-character sum; clips the shortest of the three (p90 under 1.8 s everywhere); character textures reach 4,096. The note proposes one new delta, labelled weak because it rests on one game: a 2,048-pixel character texture ceiling with 4,096 by exception. See [Roboquest production budgets](roboquest/production-budgets.md).

## Lab arms proposed

- Spawner arm: the current spawner versus a credit director with the dial of row 3, measured on time-to-first-pressure and peak concurrent enemies.
- Pocket arm: pockets placed by the reconciled spec versus uniform placement, measured on the share of pockets a scripted runner sees from the open stage and the re-roll rate.
- Companion arm: a crew that persists and repairs versus stage-bound summons, measured on damage share and losses per stage.
