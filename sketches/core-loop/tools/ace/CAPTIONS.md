# Slice 0 captions

The Slice 0 brief (2026-09-28) asks for bold, playful experiments next to a faithful control. Each parent gets three directions. `slice0.py prep` reads the fenced `caption <parent>-<direction>` blocks below verbatim, so this file is the only copy of the text.

Every caption keeps the same frame. The music is instrumental. The tempo is 172 for calm and 129 for the Warden, in D minor with Dorian named in the text, because `keyscale` has no mode field. The rejected sounds (talking growls, wobble as a main texture) are excluded by name. No caption names an artist or a song.

The guide carries the notes. Every take is a cover of the engine's full guide at strength 0.85, so harmony, bass figures, motifs, the pivot and the arc come from the engine. The caption decides who plays them and in what room. That is why the experiments can be bold without losing the gems. The groove and the motifs are in the guide, not the words.

Each caption spells out the arc. In the composition principles (R4 §1.1), a caption that narrated the timeline beat the verbatim favourite in a blind four-way test, because describing the arc is what builds the layers. The Warden captions name the held breath explicitly ("no kick") so the open section survives the performer.

## X1, the live band in a room

The engine's parts are written like a band's, with a sax call and response, comping keys and a walking reese. X1 hands them to players: a breakbeat drummer on a ride, bass guitar doubling a synth sub, Rhodes, and a three-horn section. The sax lead becomes the tenor's line and the counter becomes the trumpet's answer. At the Warden the same band locks into four-on-the-floor disco-funk with slap octave bass and clavinet. The drop hands the riff to the horns, so the payoff re-casts familiar material instead of introducing something new (R4 §1.3). The risk is a live drummer's push and pull against the screens' 12 ms lock bar. The screens will show whether a room sound costs the groove.

## X2, glitch electro-jazz into a big-room brass drop

This direction goes digital. Calm is chopped and stuttering: bit-crushed ghost hats, tape-stop fills, granular Rhodes and FM bells. The reese stays underneath, so G8's identity holds while the surface breaks up. The Warden turns the same toolkit into a wide room, with a huge kick, stuttered claps and detuned synth brass. The drop is a big brass riff over the funk octave bass, never a wobble. It tests the far end of Andrew's "Euro music hall" wave (G4) and whether glitch texture reads as playful or as noise. The screens' density check against B is the guard on "a mess".
