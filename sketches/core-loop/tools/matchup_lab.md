# Matchup lab

`tools/matchup_lab.gd` is lever 7 of `design-v3/ENEMY-PRIORITY.md`: a headless duel table. Each of the six pilots fights each enemy kind alone, N seeds each, and the lab records time to kill and hull lost. It prints a pilot x kind heatmap, marks the HARD cells and grades the gate. `tests/matchup_lab_test.gd` covers the pure logic and a small real table.

## Usage

```
godot --headless --fixed-fps 90 --path sketches/core-loop --script res://tools/matchup_lab.gd -- \
  --out=<abs ndjson> [--procs=4] [--seeds=1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8] [--cap=40] [--dodge=0.65] \
  [--pilots=tarn,tally] [--kinds=chaser,lobber] [--answer=1] [--range=18] [--idle=0] \
  [--bot-no-mix=1 --bot-no-visuals=1 --bot-no-pilot-tracks=1]

# pack mode: authored packs as the opponent instead of single kinds
godot ... -- --out=<abs ndjson> --packs=run_down,grounded|all [--table=park|alpha]  # --table only picks the pack list [--pilots=...] [--seeds=...]

# pocket sweep: a lab pocket is the arena, a pack of three of one enemy kind is the opponent
godot ... -- --out=<abs ndjson> --pocket=half_pipe|rolling_wall|crevice|hazard_field|rail_grove|all \
  [--mobility=ground,flyer,burrower,hop] [--seeds=1,2,3,4,5] [--runs=3] [--plant=pilot_helpless=1|packs_passive=1|pack_damage=4]
```

- `--out` is required. The ndjson holds one row per duel (`pilot`, `class`, `kind`, `seed`, `give`, `outcome`, `ttk_s`, `hull_lost`, `damage_taken`, `hits`, `kill_dist_m`, `warnings`, `joined`, `duel_s`, `settle_s`, `rest_forced`, `stage_changed`, `phase`). The heatmap text is written beside it as `<out>.heatmap.txt` and printed.
- Each duel is its own Godot process (a bot run repeats exactly only in a fresh one), `--procs` at a time. Run it under `nice -n 10`, at most 6 processes.
- Runtime: about 30 to 40 s of wall time per duel, almost all of it boot, stage load and the rest wait (a duel itself is seconds). Six processes give about 7 s per duel. 27 kinds x 6 pilots x 4 seeds is 648 duels, about 80 minutes; 8 seeds doubles it. The small test table is 12 duels, about 2 minutes.
- `--from=<a.ndjson,b.ndjson> [--out=<txt>]` runs nothing: it rebuilds the heatmap and baseline gate from saved rows (use it to merge batches; the answer arm reads pending there).
- `--range=<m>` sets the spawn distance (default 18). `--idle=1` makes the pilot stand and never fight: the test's planted positive control that a duel can hurt the ball (an idle tarn 6 m from a plated brute or a lobber takes hits and loses hull). Neither is part of the table.
- `--packs=<id,id,...|all>` puts the lab in pack mode (see Pack duels); `--table=park|alpha` picks the pack table (default `park`, the table the launched game plays). In pack mode `--kinds` is ignored. An unknown pack id or table is a usage error (exit 2). `--range` still overrides the pack default of 12.
- `--perched=park,mast` puts the lab in perched mode (see Perched duel): `--kinds=dispatcher` is required, anything else (another kind, several kinds, `--packs`, an unknown mode) is a usage error (exit 2).
- `--tune=<key>=<value>,...` is passed to every duel, so a game variant behind a `ChallengeDials` key (`src/core/challenge_dials.gd`) plays in all of them; compare against the same table without it.
- `--bot-no-*` are the same fast flags `bot_run.gd` takes (audio mix, visuals, cosmetic pilot tracks).

## What a duel is

The harness is `tools/priority_proof.gd`'s: a calm map (no Warden, no director packs), `Game` in test mode, the bot driver, the same dodge share (`--dodge`, default 0.65: each telegraph is answered or missed once, decided from the seed). The pilot is the brawler bot (the completionist profile, which fights with every verb) playing the class under test.

**Rest start.** The drop-in leaves the ball fast, so the lab waits until the ball is grounded and slower than 0.5 m/s (`REST_SPEED`) and holds that for 2.0 s (`SETTLE_S`) before it spawns anything. If the ball has not come to rest 6 s after first landing (`REST_WAIT_S`), the lab sets its velocity to zero through the player's own `set_velocity_now` and keeps waiting; at 12 s (`SETTLE_MAX_S`) the duel starts regardless. The row records `settle_s` (seconds from first landing to the spawn) and `rest_forced` (true when the zero-velocity nudge fired).

**Beacon pinned.** The calm map's beacon charges from the start (the ball begins inside its ring, and `ctx.calm` does not stop `beacon.advance`); about 24 s later it runs gates, launch and the next stage, which frees every enemy of the duel, so a long duel read `killed` with `ttk_s` -1 and a negative `duel_s`. The lab sets `flow.beacon_seconds_override` to an unreachable value for kind, pack and perched duels alike. As a backstop, a run whose `run.stage` changes or whose `stage_time` drops below the spawn time ends as `stage_changed` (row field `stage_changed` true), which counts as no result like `no_result` and `wall_timeout`.

**Damage.** `ctx.calm` stays on for the whole duel. It gates the Warden, the director's packs (which swamped a 40 s duel when calm was off: 12 to 27 adds joined), flood and event pushes, and hit knockback; it never gated an enemy's attack or the damage it deals (`combat._on_player_hit` always calls `run.damage`). The enemy under test attacks under the game's own rules, including its attack budget. At the spawn the lab sets max hull to 99999 (`HULL_POOL`) so a lost duel does not end the run. `hull_lost` is the run's `hull_damage` counter over the duel; `hits` and `damage_taken` count `player_hit` events from the spawn.

The lab spawns one enemy 18 m ahead of the ball and the bot fights it until the enemy and anything it made are down (`killed`), the pilot dies (`died`; effectively never with the large hull), or the cap passes (`capped`). Time to kill (`ttk_s`) runs from the spawn to the enemy's death; a split kind (the Splitter) is won when its pieces are down too, while `ttk_s` keeps the main body's death. Hull lost is the run's own `hull_damage` counter over the duel.

Kinds are `Director.KIND_NAMES` that the roster has built, minus `linker` (it guards a partner, so there is nothing to duel alone) and `foreman` (it never attacks; its row is its crew). Both are measured by `priority_proof.gd`. Bosses and wardens are not in `KIND_NAMES`. Every other named kind spawned and died alone in a probe run (27 kinds).

## Spillway passes at speed (W1-133a)

Run the lab headlessly in an imported worktree with a scratch home. This arm writes one JSON receipt. It does not need the duel table's `--out`.

```sh
"$GODOT_BIN" --headless --fixed-fps 90 --path sketches/core-loop --script res://tools/matchup_lab.gd -- \
  --packs=all --table=park --pass=straight,weave,stall --speed=14,28 \
  --seeds=1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8 --pilots=tarn --receipt=<absolute-path>/fast-threat.json
```

The dam has five ordinary packs and the flume six. Each has the Spillway elite. The stage remains part of the key when both ship the same pack. Eight seeds and two speeds yield 16 straight passes for each of those 13 stage/pack combinations, with matching weave and stall runs. `--pilots` takes the existing lab pilot IDs and defaults to Tarn for round zero. Request `--packs=staged_winch` separately to measure the fallback brute and winch drone on the dam. The fixture and narrower pack/seed selections use the same producer.

The crew spawns across a route point with the director's spacing and pack identity. The rider starts 25 m short. Each pass lasts twelve simulation seconds (long enough for the ten-second silt-boil history and its 1.2-second warning) or until death. The lab drives the real player body along the ground at the selected speed on a fixed north–south approach (toward increasing world Z). Weave steers through ±0.65 radians over 1.8 seconds. Stall holds the starting point for the matched duration. The trace measures committed geometry and hull damage on controlled paths. It cannot qualify a natural ram, speed after knockback or human feel. On the dam the site comes from `west_wash_run` in the shared route file once available, and from Sable's corresponding waypoint beforehand. On the flume it is the floor route's first point after the siphon camp.

Each pass reports its site, stage, pack, member count, pilot, seed, mode, speed, elapsed time, attacker warnings, each contact’s body slot, strike tick, ball position and committed shape, threats by kind, hull loss by source and hull loss per minute. A shell counts at impact, a chaser at lunge contact, a jolt mat at its pulse. A committed attack counts at most once even when the ball is in its live area for several ticks. The control for a lobber pack repeats every straight run with the crew's sight reporting zero velocity. The physical rider still moves. To run only the planted cases, use `--pass=straight --plant=zero_lead`.

A completed run writes its individual passes even when the enemies miss. A partial matrix or a missing baseline cannot pass the promise. For a later gameplay round, pass `--baseline=<round-zero receipt> --round-zero-sha256=<published digest>` to compare weaving hull on the same stage, site, pack, seed, speed and pilot. A stall with zero hull loss cannot establish the pressure floor. Keep the first baseline with its command and head for every later round. The W1-131a lane and W1-132 shared tools supply the separate drawn comparisons.

## Pack duels

A pack duel puts one named authored pack in front of the pilot instead of one kind, so a counter pack (a plated rig over a catch cup, three chasers and a lobber) can show up as a hard cell that no single-kind row reveals. The pack table is each stage's `packs` plus its `elite` from `ParkDefs.DEFS` (or `AlphaDefs.DEFS` with `--table=alpha`). `--table` only picks which pack list; pack duels play the same calm legacy map as kind duels. The parent hands each child `--pack-table=<id>`, never `--table=`, because `Game._chosen_table()` reads `--table=` from the command line and would move the duel onto a park or alpha stage map. `--packs=all` is every distinct id in first-seen stage order (a stage's packs, then its elite). Duplicate ids collapse; if one id carried different kinds in different stages the first stage's kinds are used. `pack_kinds(table, id)` and `pack_ids(table)` are the pure lookups.

- Same harness as a kind duel: rest start, calm map, `HULL_POOL`, the dodge share and the brawler driver. `ctx.calm` stays on exactly as in Damage above: it gates director packs, the Warden, flood and event pushes and hit knockback, not enemy attacks or the damage they deal, so the pack hurts the ball under the game's own rules. It is not turned off.
- Every member spawns with `combat.spawn_enemy` side by side across the line at the duel range, spaced `Director.PACK_SPREAD` (4.5 m) with the centered offsets `(i - (n-1)/2) * spread` along the side vector, as `Director._place_pack` does, each dropped with `terrain.drop_point`. The default range is 12 m (`PACK_RANGE`), shorter than 18, so the whole line is in play; `--range` overrides it. Members the roster has not built are skipped and listed in the row's `skipped`. Linker and Foreman are legitimate in packs (they are skipped only as single-kind rows).
- The driver fights the nearest live member (or anything a member made). `ttk_s` is the time the whole pack and its adds are down, the `_cleared()` rule; `first_down_s` is when the first member fell. `kill_dist_m` is -1 in pack mode.
- Row key: the row is `pack:<id>` in the `kind` field, so the table, hard cells, gate, heatmap and `--from` treat it like any kind. Each row also carries `pack` (id), `members` (kinds), `table`, `spawned` (members that spawned) and `skipped`. The heatmap label is `+<id>` cut to 9 characters; when two labels collide (`+plate_pr` for the two plate_press rows, the historical positive control: both read WALL, and the pack has since been removed) the later one ends in a number (`+plate_p2`).
- Answer arm: the axis is the most common `ANSWER_AXIS` among the members (ties go to the earlier entry of `AXES`), then the lookup and the paired re-duel are unchanged.
- Entering the gate: `--from=<kinds.ndjson>,<packs.ndjson>` lists kind rows first (default kind order) and pack rows after, in first-seen order, in one heatmap and one gate; pack rows count toward each pilot's hard cell like kind rows. A pack id from the park and alpha tables with the same id would share a row in a merge, so merge one table's packs at a time.
- Runtime: a pack duel costs the same boot and rest wait as a kind duel (about 30 to 40 s of wall time, 12 s of it the rest start under `--procs=2`). `--packs=all` on the park table is about 15 packs; 15 x 6 pilots x 4 seeds is 360 duels, roughly 45 minutes at six processes. The small pack smoke (2 packs x 2 pilots x 1 seed, 4 duels) takes about a minute.
- Caveats: members spawn without the Director's arrival effects: no elite affixes (the HARD-tier plated_brute affix, the Dig's PLATED on elites), so `dig_elite` and `elite` are measured unarmoured, and every member gets stage 1's HP, threat and world, not its home stage's. A pack whose members all fail to spawn records `no_result` and is left out of the table. The Director's Ballast heavy rim placement (`rim_spot`) and the park safe-spot and pack-gap filters are not reproduced; the line sits flat at the duel range. Pack duels are one pack alone on a calm map, not a live arrival with the director's pressure.

## Perched duel

Tarn's weapon is a 5 m claw (`claw_sweep`, `range_m` 5.0) and `design-v3/ENEMY-PRIORITY.md` names "perched targets, 5 m reach" as the Dispatcher's pressure on Tarn. The kind duel cannot show that, because it spawns a Dispatcher on a legacy map where `Terrain.perch_near` answers INF. The perched duel puts the Dispatcher on a real park perch and records what Tarn does about it. The coordinator ruled this instrument first (issue #149, ruling 16); it builds no pack and changes no enemy, kit or bot.

```
godot ... -- --out=<abs ndjson> --kinds=dispatcher --perched=park,mast [--range=15] [--pilots=...] [--seeds=...]
```

- **Rows.** `--perched=park` is `dispatcher@park`, `--perched=mast` is `dispatcher@mast` (the `kind` field; heatmap labels `disp@park`, `disp@mast`). `park,mast` queues both for every pilot and seed in one parent run. Both rows enter the table, the HARD rule (unchanged bar), the gate and `--from`, which lists them after kind rows and pack rows, first-seen order. Answer axis for both is `range` (the default; ENEMY-PRIORITY lists `long_barrel` and `range_tier` for Tarn).
- **Map.** Both variants play the park table (the child sets `Game.table = "park"` itself, so stage 1 is park_dam, a ParkMap). A kind or pack duel plays the legacy "spillway" map. The two rows share a map so the contrast is the perch alone.
- **Perch sources.** The tops are those `PerchPoints` has on this base: anchor-field post tops (`derive`) and landform tops (`ledges`), filtered by the same validity rules `pick` uses. The Dressing prop-top perch anchors (W18-PERCH3) are not on this base, so they are not a source; rerun the table when they land. The row's `perch_source` says `post` or `ledge`.
- **Choosing the perch.** After the rest start, `terrain.perch_near(ball, salt)` is asked with a salt from the run seed (same seed, same top; seeds spread over tops). If it finds a top within `PerchPoints.RANGE_M` (40 m) the ball stays where it rests. Otherwise one top is drawn from every top of the stage by the seed, the ball is moved with the player's own `reset_to` to a level spot (ground within 0.12 m over the spot and a 4 m ring, about a 1.5 percent grade, and 3 m clear of landform footprints): 16 bearings at `--range` (default 15 m), then the radius stepped out 5 m at a time to `--range` + 15 m, and the rest start runs again before the spawn. `relocated` records this; `standoff_m` is the distance chosen and `standoff_ok` is false when no spot was level, clear and under the top, in which case the best spot is used anyway, ranked clear of landform footprints first, then the top standing at least `PERCH_MIN` over it, then least ground spread (`standoff_clear` records whether it was clear; spots outside the map's play rect are never candidates, and when none lies inside it the row is `not_perched`; `standoff_at` is its x, z). The `standoff_*` fields appear only on rows where the search found a spot (relocated rows, and `not_perched` rows whose spot was unusable); rows where the search found nothing, and near-case rows, omit them. `perch_over_ball_m` is -1 when no spot was found. The hold keeps the ball at the spot. For a relocated row `settle_s` and `rest_forced` describe only the second rest start. park_dam has no level spot near its tops (16 bearings from `--range` out to `--range` + 15 m found none under the 0.12 m bar), so `standoff_ok` is false there. The perched duel therefore holds the relocated ball at the standoff until the spawn: it zeroes the ball's velocity every physics frame with the player's own `set_velocity_now`, spawns after `SETTLE_S` of held contact (the `SETTLE_MAX_S` ceiling still applies), and records `held`. `ball_to_perch_m` at the spawn then sits within a few metres of `standoff_m` whatever the slope; after the spawn the ball is free and may roll, which is the game. The standoff spot must also lie where the top stands at least `PERCH_MIN` over the ground (stamped landforms are terrain, not blockers, so an unchecked spot can sit on the same plateau as a ledge top, a Dispatcher 2 m over the ball, which is not perched); the fallback prefers such spots too. "Under" has no upper bound: the design perch is 6 to 10 m over its own ground (ENEMY-PRIORITY lever 4), but on park_dam the held ball sits 11 to 25 m below the top because it is held on lower slopes; this is not capped. **Exclusion rule.** A perched row counts only when the perch is clear and under: the standoff spot is clear of landform footprints and the top stands at least `PERCH_MIN` over the ground there (for a perch the terrain picked within reach, the resting ball takes the place of the spot). A perch that fails the check, at the standoff or again at the spawn on the real body position (a mast row on a post taller than 9.5 m, or a held ball that drifted uphill, can fail it), ends before any fight with outcome `not_perched`, which is no result like `no_perch`: it stays out of cells and the gate, and the row keeps `perch`, `perch_over_ball_m`, `perched_under` and, when a spot was found, the `standoff_*` fields as evidence (`ttk_s` and `kill_dist_m` are -1). A table may therefore have fewer perched runs than seeds; on park_dam with the canyon walls (#488, read at 814a53ad), seeds 1, 3, 4 and 7 to 12 give a clear-and-under standoff and seed 2 finds no standoff spot and reads `not_perched` (seeds 5 and 6 were not read; seeds 13 to 24 not since the walls). Read `perch_over_ball_m` and `perched_under` per seed before trusting a cell. A stage with no top at all records outcome `no_perch`, which counts as no result like `no_result` and `wall_timeout` (left out of the cell, shown `-` if every seed is one). `park` mode never falls back to a mast.
- **Variants.** `park` passes the top as `spawn_enemy`'s perch arg, so the Dispatcher builds no mast and sits 1.5 m over the top. `mast` uses the same top for the ball but no perch arg: the Dispatcher builds its own 8 m mast on the ground at the top's x, z. That is the contrast row. For a post top the two stand at similar heights; for a ledge top `park` stands on the landform and `mast` stands 8 m over it.
- **Kill definition.** `ttk_s` is the Dispatcher's death time from the spawn, with the kind-mode outcome rule: `killed` only when it and every add it dropped (hookers, plated brutes at higher escalation) are down. `dispatcher_down_s` is its own death time always, so a Dispatcher killed with adds standing to the cap shows as `capped` with a `dispatcher_down_s`.
- **Extra row fields.** `perched`, `perch` [x, y, z], `perch_source`, `perch_rise_m` (the Dispatcher's own `perch_height()`, the top over the terrain under it), `perch_base_rise_m` (the top over the base the perch band is read from), `mast_built`, `ball_to_perch_m` (flat, at the spawn; within a few metres of `standoff_m` on a relocated row because the ball is held), `perch_over_ball_m` (the Dispatcher's y over the terrain under the ball at the spawn) (on a `not_perched` row ended before the spawn it is the top's y over the ground at the spot or the ball) and `perched_under` (that value is at least `PerchPoints.PERCH_MIN`, 6 m), `drops`, `landed_hits`, `escalation` (the Dispatcher's counters, last value before it died or the cap), `relocated`. For a ledge top `perch_rise_m` reads about 0, because the landform is the ground under it; read `perch_base_rise_m` (6 to 10 m) for the rise.
- **Instrument versus design.** The bot has no Claw Pull or Drag logic; its only grapple verb is the harpoon bite within 30 m. A slow or lost `dispatcher@park` Tarn cell may be a bot gap, not a kit gap. Read it with the `dispatcher@mast` row and with `ball_to_perch_m`, `landed_hits` and `drops`: if the Dispatcher drops pods freely (`drops` high, `landed_hits` low) and Tarn never gets within 5 m, the bot did not use the harpoon to get there; if a pylon pilot kills the perch quickly and Tarn does not, the cell is a candidate for a real gap, which the bot cannot settle. A ledge top that the ball can roll up is reachable by anyone, so check `perch_source` before calling a row "perched".
- **Reading a cell.** A cell median over seeds in different regimes (some seeds with the Dispatcher near the ball, some far above it) is a value no seed produced. Read the per-seed `perch_over_ball_m` and `perched_under` before trusting a cell.
- **Runtime.** Same as a kind duel, about 30 to 40 s of boot and rest each, plus a second rest start when the ball is relocated (up to 12 s more). A small table (2 modes x 2 pilots x 2 seeds, 8 duels) at `--procs=2` takes about five minutes on a loaded box.

## Pocket sweep

Challenge's pocket-against-mobility sweep (board #241, comment 6367206273 and the coordinator's option (a) on NEEDS 6367573974). `--pocket=<shape>` plays the duel in one of the five Greybox lab pockets (`tools/lab_pockets/<shape>.glb`) instead of the legacy map, against a pack of three of one enemy kind, and answers one question per run: who won. `tools/pocket_arena.gd` builds the arena and `tools/pocket_terrain.gd` is the terrain the enemies and the driver read. Both are lab-only. `--pocket` is exclusive with `--kinds`, `--packs` and `--perched`, which it ignores; the kind, pack and perched modes are unchanged without it.

- **The arena.** The glb loads at runtime with `GLTFDocument` (the folder is `.gdignore`'d), every mesh gets a trimesh collider with its own transform, and meshes named `slick*` keep friction 0 (the crevice's choke walls; the floor keeps its grip). The pocket sits 300 m over the legacy map (`LIFT`) and the legacy ground's colliders are switched off, so nothing of that map touches it. The legacy map is only the host: the Game, the stage entry and the rest start run on it as before. Mojulo (x, y, z) is Godot (x, z, -y); the entry is x = 0, the lane is the footprint's centre line.
- **Terrain.** The live `Terrain` instance is re-scripted to `PocketTerrain` (its variables carried across by hand, since `set_script` drops them), so every holder of `ctx.terrain`, and every enemy handed it at spawn, reads the pocket: `height` and `drop_point` are a downward ray against the pocket's floor class, `spawn_point` is the entry (x = 2.5, on the lane, 1.6 m over the floor), `entry_heading` is +x, `contains` is the footprint plus 4 m, and `rescue_point` returns the pilot to the entry (counted as `rescues` in the row). Everything else (`map()`, `world()`, overlays) stays the host map's. The floor class excludes the overhead ride structure (meshes named `rail*` and `pillar*`, the rail grove's rails, posts and pillars): a ray from above would stand a walker on a rail. Those still collide; they only leave the height question. A ray that misses (off the footprint) reads the pocket's lowest point.
- **Rest start.** The pilot is held still at the entry (the player's own `set_velocity_now` every frame, as the perched duel holds its ball) until `SETTLE_S` of grounded contact, because the half pipe's floor falls 3% and the drop-in's speed would otherwise carry the ball down the channel (`rest_forced` read true and the ball was at 17 m/s at the spawn). It is released at the spawn.
- **Opponent.** Three of one kind, `Director.PACK_SPREAD` (4.5 m) apart, each dropped with `terrain.drop_point(x, z, 1.8)`, on the pocket's far side: the farthest station (from 3 m short of the far end back to the half-way mark) where all three stand on the lane's own floor (within 0.3 m of the lane centre). Where no station is flat across (the crevice's 5 m slot is narrower than the line; the hazard field's pits), the pack stands in file down the lane at 4.5 m spacing, last member 3 m short of the far end. The row's `spots` records them.
- **Columns** (the map page's mobility kinds): `ground` = `chaser`; `flyer` = `kite_drone` and `winch_drone` (two sub-rows); `burrower` = `burrower`; `hop` = n/a. No enemy kind hops (`roster.gd` `MOVE_CLASSES` lists fixed, ground and airborne; the page calls hop "my grouping, not a code field"), so the hop cells are emitted as explicit n/a cells and queue no run.
- **Runs.** Seeds 1 to 5, three runs per seed, 15 per row. The pilot is the band harness's rotation (class index = seed modulo the roster) stepped one class per repeat, so the three runs of a seed are three classes. The first run of a seed uses the plain seed as the rng seed; repeats add 1000 per run index (`rng_seed` in the row; `seed` is the plain seed, `run` the index). A bot run repeats exactly in a fresh process, so repeats with one pilot and one seed would be the same run three times.
- **Who wins.** The pilot wins iff it kills the whole pack inside the cap and its `hull_lost` stays below the class's real max hull (`real_max_hull`, `run.max_hp` read at the spawn, before the lab's `HULL_POOL` replaces it). The enemy wins otherwise: the duel ends the tick `hull_lost` reaches `real_max_hull` (outcome `hull_out`, `hull_out` true), or the cap passes (`capped`), or the pilot dies. `no_result`, `wall_timeout` and `stage_changed` are void: counted apart and left out of n. The row records `winner`, `ttk_s`, `hull_lost`, `real_max_hull`, `hits`, `kill_order` (slot, kind and time of each fall, the slots numbered across the line from the first spot), `first_down_s` and the existing fields, plus `pocket`, `enemy`, `column`, `rescues` and `plant`.
- **Cell verdict** (comment 6367573974): the pilot's win rate over the runs that measured something, flagged for retune at 0% or 100%. The flyer column pools its two sub-rows and carries each in `rows`; it is flagged when either sub-row is. The output is `<out>.pocket.md` (a 5 x 4 table of win rate, n and flag) and `<out>.pocket.json` (the same plus each sub-row), printed too; `--from=<rows.ndjson,...> [--out=<base>]` rebuilds both from saved rows to merge shards.
- **Controls** (test-only; absent = identical behaviour). `--plant=pilot_helpless=1` stands the pilot still (the `--idle` duel) and sets the `mobs.damage_mult` dial (`ChallengeDials`) to 50, so the first hit runs the hull out; the cell must read a 0% flag. Idle alone is not enough: a chaser pack blows itself up on a still ball. `--plant=packs_passive=1` sets that dial to 0 (the packs still attack but cannot hurt) and must drive a cell to 100%. `pocket_control_ok(plant, cell)` is the reading. Run both controls before reading a real cell. `--plant=pack_damage=<k>` multiplies every enemy hit through that dial and is a probe with no expected flag: pilots kill a pack of three in 1 to 7 s and take 0 or 1 hits, so x4 cannot reach 0% (it was the first low control, replaced on #241).
- **Caveats.** The pockets are geometry only: the hazard field's pits are floor, nothing in them hurts, and the rail grove's rails are collision, not rides. The arena has no back wall at the entry and the half pipe's ends are open, so a ram, a lunge or a throw can leave it (`rescues`, and enemies that fall are put back by their own rescue). The enemies spawn without the Director's arrival effects, as in pack duels. Automated bots do not establish human feel.

## HARD cell, exactly

- Cell value: the median over the cell's seeds of time to kill. A duel not won (died or capped) counts the cap. `no_result`, `wall_timeout`, `stage_changed`, `no_perch` and `not_perched` runs are no result and are left out of the cell.
- Row: one kind across the six pilots. Row median: the median of its six cell medians, floored at 1.0 s (`MIN_ROW_S`) so a row of near-instant kills cannot make a one-second cell hard.
- A cell is HARD when its median is strictly greater than 2.0 x its row median (`HARD_RATIO`).

## Gate

1. Every pilot has at least one HARD cell. PASS or FAIL.
2. The brawler bot wins each hard cell once given that cell's answer item. A win is a kill in at least half the seeds with a median time no longer hard against the baseline row median. The answer item is the first part id (sorted) whose row carries an `answers` tag for the cell's axis and whose `class` gate admits the pilot. The axis per kind is `ANSWER_AXIS` in the lab (range, spread, fuse, cc, air, plating; a kind not listed answers to range). The part is handed over with `run.add_part` before the spawn and the cell is re-duelled on the same seeds.
   `answers` tags exist only on the Systems lane's branch. On a base without them the lookup finds no field, and every hard cell reports `answer: pending (no answers tags on this base)`; the second gate line is then INCONCLUSIVE, never a pass or a fail.

3. No WALL row: a kind or pack that at least one pilot ran and none of those pilots ever killed fails this line (`gate no wall row (every pilot capped, no kill)`), because its median is the cap and the HARD rule cannot see it. A pack must clear this before it ships; a planted wall (the original `plate_press` composition, plated_brute x2 + ballast_heavy, capped in 24 of 24 duels) is the positive control.

The verdict is FAIL if part 1 fails, part 3 fails or any answer arm is lost, INCONCLUSIVE if any answer is pending, otherwise PASS.

## Reading the heatmap

Rows are kinds with their row median in seconds; columns are pilots. Each cell reads `median-ttk[median-hull-lost]`, and a `*` marks a HARD cell. A cell of `40.0*` with seeds all capped means the pilot never killed that kind inside the cap. A `-` means no run produced a result. A row that at least one pilot ran and no pilot that ran it ever killed ends in `WALL` (its median is the cap, so the HARD rule cannot flag it); the gate prints `gate no wall row ...: FAIL (wall rows: <ids>)` and the verdict is FAIL. A pack or kind that is a WALL needs rebalancing before the table is read. After the grid, one line per pilot lists its hard cells with the answer-arm status, then the gate lines.

Caveats: duels start from rest, so a ball that never rests on a slope is nudged to zero (see `rest_forced`); hull lost is damage dealt, not a death count. Automated bots do not establish human feel.
